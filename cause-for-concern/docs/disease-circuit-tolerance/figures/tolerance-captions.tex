% PANEL 1 -- the numerical floor
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{figure*}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/panel1_numerical_floor.png}
    \caption{\textbf{The Numerical Floor of a Cycle Sum: Growth with
    Length and Potential Range, Validity of the Forward Bound, Its
    Slack, and the Two-Parameter Floor Surface.}
    Every quantity is measured on \emph{thermodynamically consistent}
    circuits, for which the cycle sum vanishes identically
    (\Cref{lem:vanish}); the computed value is therefore the accumulated
    rounding error itself and carries no information about the network.
    All arithmetic is IEEE-754 binary64 with unit roundoff
    $\mach = 2^{-53} \approx 1.11\times10^{-16}$.
    (\textbf{A})~Maximum computed $|\widehat{\hol}_\ell|$ over $60$
    randomly generated ring circuits per grid cell, plotted against
    cycle length $L \in \{3,5,8,12,16,20,25,30\}$ on a logarithmic
    ordinate, with one curve per potential range
    $\Lambda \in \{10, 10^2, 10^3, 3\times10^3\}$ kJ/mol.
    The error grows with both parameters and spans three decades across
    the grid, from $1.78\times10^{-15}$ at $(L,\Lambda)=(3,10)$ to
    $2.73\times10^{-12}$ at $(30,3000)$ --- the empirical content of
    \Cref{cor:no-constant}, which asserts that no single scalar can
    track a floor varying this widely.
    Critically, the largest value anywhere on the grid remains six
    orders of magnitude below the customary tolerance $10^{-6}$, which
    is the observation that falsified our own initial prediction
    (\Cref{rem:prediction-failed}).
    (\textbf{B})~Forward error bound $\epsnum(\ell) = \gamma_{2L}\cdot
    2L\Lambda_\ell$ from \Cref{thm:noise-scale} plotted against the
    measured error for all $32$ grid cells on doubly logarithmic axes;
    the dashed diagonal marks equality.
    Every point lies strictly above the diagonal, so the bound was
    violated zero times in $1920$ trials --- a negative control that
    would have failed had the error analysis of
    \Cref{sec:numerical-floor} been unsound.
    (\textbf{C})~Distribution of the slack factor
    $\epsnum/|\widehat{\hol}_\ell|$ on a logarithmic abscissa, with the
    median at $729$ marked in orange.
    Slack ranges from $10.1$ to $722$, indicating the bound is
    pessimistic by one to three orders of magnitude; this is the
    expected consequence of a worst-case analysis assuming errors
    accumulate as $O(L)$ when random rounding accumulates as
    $O(\sqrt{L})$ \citep{higham2002}.
    The bound is therefore safe but not tight, and we report the
    slack rather than claiming sharpness.
    (\textbf{D})~The numerical floor $\log_{10}\epsnum$ rendered as a
    surface over $(\log_{10}\Lambda,\, L)$ with iso-contours projected
    onto the base plane, spanning $2.93\times10^{-14}$ to
    $1.17\times10^{-9}$ kJ/mol.
    The surface is monotone increasing in both arguments and flat in
    neither, which is precisely why the tolerance must be computed per
    cycle from the pair $(L, \Lambda_\ell)$ rather than fixed globally
    or summarised by their product.}
    \label{fig:tol_panel1}
\end{figure*}

% PANEL 2 -- what a fixed tolerance costs
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{figure*}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/panel2_fixed_tolerance.png}
    \caption{\textbf{Measured Cost of a Fixed Tolerance
    $\eps=10^{-6}$: Absence of False Positives in Double Precision,
    Their Recovery in Single Precision, Uniform Missed Detection Below
    Threshold, and the Unwarranted-Positive Rate.}
    Four strata of $400$ trials each, crossing short cycles
    ($L\in[3,5]$) against long ($L\in[15,22]$) and low potential range
    ($\Lambda = 20$ kJ/mol) against high ($\Lambda = 1800$ kJ/mol).
    (\textbf{A})~False-positive rate on consistent circuits, where by
    \Cref{lem:vanish} every positive is necessarily spurious.
    In binary64 the rate is exactly $0.000$ in all four strata; because
    a bar of height zero is invisible, it is drawn as a stub at the
    baseline.
    This falsifies the prediction, which we had made from
    \Cref{cor:no-constant} without checking, that a fixed tolerance
    would manufacture false positives on long high-potential cycles:
    the rounding error of \Cref{fig:tol_panel1} never approaches
    $10^{-6}$, so a threshold placed far above the entire noise range
    is uniformly too loose rather than sometimes too tight.
    Repeating the identical null computation in binary32, where unit
    roundoff rises to $\approx 6\times10^{-8}$ and the numerical floor
    with it, recovers the predicted mode: the rate climbs to
    $0.177$, $0.445$, $0.565$ and $0.765$ across the four strata.
    (\textbf{B})~The binary32 rate decomposed by length class against
    $\Lambda$ on a logarithmic abscissa.
    Raising $\Lambda$ raises the rate within either length class
    ($0.177\to0.445$ short, $0.565\to0.765$ long), and raising $L$
    raises it within either potential class, so both parameters act in
    the direction \Cref{thm:noise-scale} predicts.
    The effect of $L$ is however the larger: the long low-$\Lambda$
    stratum ($L\Lambda = 370$) exceeds the short high-$\Lambda$ stratum
    ($L\Lambda = 7200$) at $0.565$ against $0.445$, so the rate is
    \emph{not} monotone in the product.
    This is consistent with $L$ entering the bound twice --- in the
    count of accumulated terms and inside $\gamma_{2L}$ --- while
    $\Lambda$ enters only linearly, and it is the reason we plot the
    two parameters separately rather than collapsing them.
    (\textbf{C})~Missed-detection rate against injected edge defect
    $D \in [10^{-8}, 1]$ kJ/mol on a logarithmic abscissa, one curve per
    stratum, with $\eps = 10^{-6}$ marked by the dashed vertical.
    Every defect below the threshold is missed with probability
    $1.000$ in every stratum, independently of data quality; at
    $D = \eps$ exactly the rate falls to $0.367$--$0.545$, and by
    $D = 10^{-5}$ it reaches $0.000$ everywhere.
    The sharpness of this transition shows the threshold is functioning
    as a detection limit rather than a noise gate --- a quantity chosen
    by habit is thereby silently encoding a clinical decision.
    (\textbf{D})~The three measured costs per stratum as grouped
    three-dimensional bars.
    $\mathrm{FP}_{64}$ is identically zero; $\mathrm{FP}_{32}$ grows
    with both $L$ and $\Lambda$; and the unwarranted-positive rate is
    $1.000$ in all four strata --- with per-species uncertainty
    $\sigma = 1$ kJ/mol and an injected defect of $0.5$ kJ/mol lying
    wholly inside that uncertainty, the fixed test asserts
    \textsf{INCONSISTENT} on every cycle, where the cycle-local test
    correctly returns \textsf{UNDECIDABLE}.
    This last quantity, not the false-positive rate, is the substantive
    cost of a fixed tolerance at any precision.}
    \label{fig:tol_panel2}
\end{figure*}

% PANEL 3 -- the trichotomy
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{figure*}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/panel3_trichotomy.png}
    \caption{\textbf{The Three-Valued Verdict over Data Quality and
    Defect Magnitude: Verdict Map, Separation of the Two Floors, Verdict
    Composition, and the Trichotomy as a Surface.}
    A six-edge cycle at $\Lambda = 200$ kJ/mol evaluated on a
    $26\times40$ grid of per-species uncertainty
    $\sigma \in [10^{-3}, 10]$ kJ/mol against injected edge defect
    $D \in [10^{-10}, 10^{2}]$ kJ/mol, $1040$ points in total.
    (\textbf{A})~Verdict map with blue \textsf{CONSISTENT}, orange
    \textsf{UNDECIDABLE} and red \textsf{INCONSISTENT}.
    The solid black curve traces the data floor
    $\epsdata(\sigma) = z_{0.995}\,(\sum_i \sigma_i^2)^{1/2}$ and the
    dashed vertical marks the numerical floor
    $\epsnum = 2.48\times10^{-12}$ kJ/mol.
    The orange band lying between them is the undecidable region of
    \Cref{def:verdict}, and it occupies $80.9\%$ of the grid; the
    boundary is a straight line of unit slope in these coordinates
    because $\epsdata$ scales linearly with $\sigma$.
    (\textbf{B})~The two floors plotted against $\sigma$ on doubly
    logarithmic axes with the intervening region shaded.
    The numerical floor is flat at $2.48\times10^{-12}$ kJ/mol, since it
    depends on the arithmetic and the potentials but not on the reported
    uncertainty; the data floor rises from $6.31\times10^{-3}$ to
    $63.1$ kJ/mol across the swept range.
    At the midpoint the two differ by $11.5$ orders of magnitude, which
    is the quantitative content of \Cref{thm:trichotomy}(ii): the
    undecidable class is not a technicality but the dominant regime at
    every realistic data quality.
    (\textbf{C})~Verdict composition across the full defect range as a
    function of $\sigma$, shown as stacked fractions.
    The \textsf{INCONSISTENT} share contracts monotonically from
    $0.350$ at $\sigma = 10^{-3}$ to $0.025$ at $\sigma = 10$, so the
    same defect becomes progressively unresolvable as annotation quality
    degrades.
    This is the behaviour a calibrated diagnostic must have and that a
    two-valued test cannot express: at $\sigma = 10$ a binary rule would
    still return a definite verdict on $97.5\%$ of the defects on which
    this test correctly declines.
    (\textbf{D})~The verdict rendered as a surface over
    $(\log_{10} D,\, \log_{10}\sigma)$ with the two decision boundaries
    contoured on the base plane, the vertical axis taking the three
    discrete values \textsf{C}, \textsf{U} and \textsf{I}.
    The step structure exhibits the trichotomy directly.
    The near-absence of the \textsf{CONSISTENT} plateau
    (measured fraction $0.000$) is an artefact of the sweep beginning at
    $D = 10^{-10}$, some forty times $\epsnum$, so that no grid point
    falls below the numerical floor; extending the sweep below
    $10^{-12}$ recovers it.
    We report the fraction as measured rather than adjusting the grid to
    produce a more balanced figure.}
    \label{fig:tol_panel3}
\end{figure*}

% PANEL 4 -- basis dependence and detection
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{figure*}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/panel4_basis_detection.png}
    \caption{\textbf{Basis Dependence of Localisation, Invariance of the
    Witness Set, the Detection Threshold in Units of the Cycle-Local
    Tolerance, and Degradation of Localisation with Flag Count.}
    Panels (A), (B) and (D) are computed on $200$ random networks of
    $6$--$11$ species carrying $2$--$4$ chords, each with exactly one
    edge defect of $50$ kJ/mol and per-species uncertainty
    $\sigma = 0.05$ kJ/mol; panel (C) is an independent detection sweep.
    (\textbf{A})~Number of cycles flagged \textsf{INCONSISTENT} under a
    minimum-weight cycle basis against the number flagged under a
    fundamental (spanning-tree) basis, with Gaussian jitter of standard
    deviation $0.07$ applied to separate coincident integer points and
    the dashed diagonal marking agreement.
    Grey points agree; orange points do not, and they constitute
    $69/200$ trials, a disagreement rate of $34.5\%$.
    The network-level verdict, by contrast, agreed in $200/200$ trials,
    confirming \Cref{thm:basis-verdict} while
    \Cref{prop:basis-localisation} occurs at a rate high enough to
    matter in practice rather than only in a constructed
    counterexample: a report naming a single flagged loop would have
    been basis-dependent in roughly one trial in three.
    (\textbf{B})~Distribution of witness-set cardinality
    $|W| = |\bigcap_{\ell \in F} E(\ell)|$ under each basis, with dashed
    lines at the means.
    The minimum-weight basis yields a sharper localisation
    ($3.77$ against $4.30$ edges, ranges $1$--$9$ and $1$--$10$),
    confirming the prediction of \Cref{rem:mcb} that shorter cycles
    intersect in fewer edges; the improvement is real but modest, under
    half an edge, and smaller than we anticipated.
    Both bases contained the defective edge in $200/200$ trials, which
    is \Cref{thm:witness-contains} verified without exception.
    (\textbf{C})~Detection indicator against defect magnitude expressed
    in units of the cycle-local tolerance, $D/\epsstar$, on a
    logarithmic abscissa, for five data qualities
    $\sigma \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.2, 1, 5\}$ kJ/mol swept over $220$
    points each.
    The dotted vertical marks $\epsstar$ and the dashed vertical the
    guaranteed threshold $2\epsstar$ of \Cref{thm:detection}; every
    curve steps from $0$ to $1$ between them, so the guarantee held in
    all $1100$ evaluations while the empirical threshold sits at
    $0.51$--$0.58$ of the guaranteed value.
    The factor of two is thus the price of a two-sided guarantee
    (\Cref{rem:factor2}) and is not usually paid in practice.
    That the five curves collapse onto one another under this
    normalisation --- despite $\epsstar$ itself ranging over three
    decades, from $0.063$ to $31.6$ kJ/mol --- is what establishes
    $\epsstar$ as the correct unit for the detection threshold.
    (\textbf{D})~Mean witness-set size as a surface over network size
    and number of flagged cycles, with the individual networks
    overlaid as points.
    Localisation degrades as more cycles are flagged (Pearson
    $r = -0.72$ between flag count and witness size): the witness set is
    an intersection, and additional flagged cycles shrink it only when
    they do not overlap.
    Sharp localisation therefore requires a basis of short,
    weakly-overlapping cycles rather than merely a large number of
    positive tests.}
    \label{fig:tol_panel4}
\end{figure*}
