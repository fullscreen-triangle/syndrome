\chapter{Fuzzy Cellular Circuits and Topological Disease}
\label{ch:fuzzy-circuits}
\label{ch:disease-circuits}

%%-------- PREAMBLE: Chapter Overview -----------------------------------------

The preceding chapters established the partition-theoretic foundations of
cellular information processing and the Poincar\'{e} computing framework.
This chapter applies those foundations to the central question of disease:
what does it mean, formally, for a cell to be sick, and why can a cell not
detect its own sickness?

The answer is both simple and surprising.  A cell is a circuit.  Its
molecular species are nodes carrying voltages equal to their chemical
potentials; its reactions are edges carrying currents equal to metabolic
fluxes; its enzymes are conductances.  Health is a property of that
circuit --- not a state to be compared against a stored template, but
a structural property: \emph{all loops close}.  Disease is the holonomy
of an inconsistent loop --- a quantity that is globally detectable but
locally invisible to any single molecular sensor.

Before developing this framework, we must confront the obstacle that
motivates it: the paradox of cellular blindness.

\section{The Blindness Problem and Why Simulation Does Not Help}
\label{sec:blindness}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

A neuron accumulating misfolded protein aggregates continues to pass every
internal molecular quality check, step by step, until catastrophic failure.
A cancer cell satisfies every local signalling constraint while violating
the global constraints of the organism.  This is not a failure of molecular
machinery.  It is a structural feature of any locally-validating system.
We call it \emph{epistemic blindness}.

\begin{definition}[Epistemic Blindness]
\label{def:epistemic-blindness}
A dynamical system $(X, \mathbf{v})$ is \emph{epistemically blind} with
respect to a partition $\{A, B\}$ of $X$ if the flow $\mathbf{v}(x)$
does not encode membership in $A$ or $B$: there exists no function
$f: TX \to \{A, B\}$ such that $f(\mathbf{v}(x))$ correctly classifies
$x$ for all $x \in X$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Cells are Epistemically Blind to Disease]
\label{prop:cells-blind}
Under generic kinetic models (mass-action, Michaelis-Menten), a cell
operating via forward reaction dynamics is epistemically blind with
respect to the partition \emph{healthy}/\emph{diseased}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The kinetic rate law $v_{ij}([C_i], [C_j], k_{ij})$ at any reaction edge
depends only on current concentrations and rate constants.  It contains
no reference to a healthy reference state $\mathbf{x}^{*}_{H}$.  At the
diseased state $\mathbf{x}^{*}_{D}$, the flux vector
$\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{D})$ encodes only the direction of the next
state transition; it carries no information about the distance
$\|\mathbf{x}^{*}_{D} - \mathbf{x}^{*}_{H}\|$ unless that distance is
directly reflected in current concentrations.  Disease states frequently
involve regulatory rewiring, post-translational modifications, or
epigenetic changes that alter the parameters of $\mathbf{v}$ rather than
concentrations directly, making the current flux indistinguishable from
that of a normal cell elsewhere in the healthy attractor.  Hence no
function of $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}^{*}_{D})$ alone reliably distinguishes
diseased from healthy.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why Simulation Fails: A Formal Argument}

The dominant paradigm in computational systems biology is simulation:
construct a model of cellular reactions and integrate the equations of
motion forward in time~\citep{karr2012}.  The implicit assumption is that
a sufficiently detailed simulation will capture disease.  This assumption
is flawed for a fundamental reason that is independent of model fidelity.

\begin{proposition}[Faithful Simulation Adds No Information]
\label{prop:simulation-fails}
A faithful simulation of a cell's forward kinetics is informationally
equivalent to the cell itself.  It reproduces the cell's epistemic
blindness exactly.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Omega$ denote the set of all trajectories $\mathbf{x}(\cdot)$
consistent with the kinetic equations.  A forward simulation starting
from $\mathbf{x}_0$ selects a single $\omega \in \Omega$ according to the
probability measure $P$ induced by the stochastic kinetics.  The
information content of this selection is
$H(\omega) = -\log P(\omega)$, which equals the self-information of the
corresponding cellular trajectory because both are governed by the same
measure on $\Omega$.  Therefore the mutual information between the
simulation output and the cellular trajectory is
$I(\mathrm{sim}; \mathrm{cell}) = H(\mathrm{cell})$,
the maximum achievable.  The simulation produces no surplus information
beyond what the cell itself contains.  Since the cell is epistemically
blind to disease (\Cref{prop:cells-blind}), the simulation is equally
blind.
\end{proof}

The resolution is not a better simulation.  It is a different question.
An electrician troubleshooting a circuit does not carry a photograph of
what every voltage should be.  They carry knowledge of Kirchhoff's
laws --- the structural constraints that any working circuit must satisfy ---
and they check whether the circuit satisfies them.  The ``healthy'' state is
never stored or compared against.  It is implied by the requirement that
the circuit \emph{closes}.

This chapter develops that insight into a complete mathematical framework.

\section{The No Template Theorem}
\label{sec:no-template}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

A recurring assumption in molecular diagnostics is that disease is detected
by comparing the current cell state against a stored healthy template.
The No Template Theorem shows this assumption is logically impossible for
any physically realisable bounded system --- cell, simulation, or observer
alike.

\begin{theorem}[No Template Theorem]
\label{thm:no-template}
No bounded system $\mathcal{X}$ can store a complete representation of its
own state.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose for contradiction that $\mathcal{X}$ contains a representation
$R(\mathcal{X})$ of its own state.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), itemsep=2pt]
  \item $R(\mathcal{X})$ must be encoded in the physical state of $\mathcal{X}$,
        so $R(\mathcal{X})$ is a \emph{part} of the state of $\mathcal{X}$.
  \item A \emph{complete} representation of the state of $\mathcal{X}$ must
        also represent the part $R(\mathcal{X})$, yielding a representation
        $R(R(\mathcal{X}))$.
  \item $R(R(\mathcal{X}))$ must itself be encoded in the state, so a
        complete representation must further contain $R(R(R(\mathcal{X})))$,
        and so on.
  \item This infinite regress requires storage capacity
        $|\mathcal{X}| \geq |\mathcal{X}| + |R(\mathcal{X})| + |R(R(\mathcal{X}))| + \cdots$,
        which is impossible for any bounded system with
        $|\mathcal{X}| < \infty$ (Axiom~1, finite phase space).
\end{enumerate}
Contradiction; therefore no such complete representation exists.
\end{proof}

The source paper strengthens this to a universal form that explicitly
handles simulations and observers.

\begin{theorem}[Universal No Template Theorem]
\label{thm:universal-no-template}
No bounded physical system can store a complete representation of the state
of any system of equal or greater complexity, including itself.  This holds
for:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{The cell:} cannot store its own healthy state.
  \item \textbf{A simulation:} cannot exceed the cell's own information content
        (and therefore cannot detect what the cell cannot).
  \item \textbf{An experimental observer:} cannot store a complete reference
        state of the cell, because the observer is itself a bounded physical
        system.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{Part (i).}  Let $N_{\max}$ be the number of distinguishable states
of the system.  Storing a complete state description requires
$\log_2 N_{\max}$ bits.  The storage subsystem is itself part of the
system; representing it requires an additional $\log_2\!\log_2 N_{\max}$
bits, and so on.  The total storage requirement
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{total}}
  = \log_2 N_{\max} + \log_2\!\log_2 N_{\max} + \cdots
  \label{eq:storage-diverge}
\end{equation}
diverges, exceeding the finite capacity $N_{\max}$.

\textbf{Part (ii).}  A faithful simulation governed by the same probability
measure on trajectory space as the cell produces no surplus information
(\Cref{prop:simulation-fails}).  More formally, if the simulator has
$N_{\mathrm{sim}} \geq N_{\max}$ states, then by part~(i) the simulator
cannot store a complete representation of itself, and the mutual information
$I(\mathrm{sim}; \mathrm{system}) = H(\mathrm{system})$ equals but does
not exceed the cell's own entropy.

\textbf{Part (iii).}  An experimental observer is a bounded physical
system with finite phase space $\Omega_{\mathrm{obs}}$.  The observer's
model of the cell is a map
$M : \Omega_{\mathrm{cell}} \to \Omega_{\mathrm{obs}}$.
By the data processing inequality, $I(M(\mathbf{x}); \mathbf{x}) \leq H(\mathbf{x})$,
with equality only if $M$ is a sufficient statistic.  But a sufficient
statistic requires $|\Omega_{\mathrm{obs}}| \geq |\Omega_{\mathrm{cell}}|$
and the observer's cell model is only a subsystem of $\Omega_{\mathrm{obs}}$.
By the same regress as part~(i), the observer cannot store a complete
reference.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Incompleteness of Template Diagnosis]
\label{cor:template-incompleteness}
Template-based diagnosis --- comparing the current cell state to a stored
``healthy template'' --- is necessarily incomplete.  No cell can store a
healthy template with sufficient fidelity to cover its own state space.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from \Cref{thm:no-template}: if a complete self-representation
is impossible, a complete representation of an external healthy reference
encoded within the same bounded system is equally impossible by the same
regress argument applied to the combined state (current $+$ template).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Self-Consistency as the Only Viable Criterion]
\label{cor:self-consistency}
The only diagnostic criterion that does not require an external reference
template is \emph{self-consistency}: the Wegscheider condition~\citep{Wegscheider1901}
$\HolL = \mathrm{Id}$ for all loops $\ell$ in the biochemical circuit.
Self-consistency requires only local loop traversal --- information already
present in the circuit --- and therefore scales within the cell's finite
storage budget.
\end{corollary}

The practical consequence of \Cref{cor:self-consistency} is that a cell
must use holonomy computation (\Cref{sec:topo-inconsistency}) rather than
reference comparison as its internal health monitor.  This also explains
why single biomarker assays fail: a single molecular concentration is a
local measurement that, by the Local Invisibility Theorem
(\Cref{sec:invisibility}), cannot detect loop-level inconsistency.

\section{The Biochemical Circuit: Rigorous Derivation}
\label{sec:biochemical-circuit}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The electrical circuit analogy for cellular biochemistry is not a metaphor.
It is an exact identification, derivable from thermodynamics and information
theory without approximation.  We establish the derivation in three steps:
(i)~chemical potential as categorical depth, (ii)~Kirchhoff's current law
from stoichiometric mass balance, (iii)~Kirchhoff's voltage law from
thermodynamic cycle consistency.

\begin{figure*}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{panel1_circuit_foundations.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Circuit topology and Kirchhoff analogs.}
  The biochemical circuit $\Gcond = (V, E, w)$ with molecular species as
  nodes (node voltage = chemical potential $\varpotential_i = \kB T \ln c_i/c_{\mathrm{ref}}$)
  and directed reactions as weighted edges (conductance $w_{ij} = k_{ij}^+ c_j$).
  Left: graph topology showing hub nodes (large degree) and peripheral nodes.
  Centre: Kirchhoff Current Law (KCL) balance at each node at steady state.
  Right: Kirchhoff Voltage Law (KVL) / Wegscheider condition on a representative
  loop, illustrating the loop holonomy $\mathrm{hol}_\ell = 0$ in the healthy
  (detailed-balance) state and $\mathrm{hol}_\ell \neq 0$ in the diseased state.}
  \label{fig:ch08-circuit-foundations}
\end{figure*}

\begin{definition}[Biochemical Circuit]
\label{def:biochemical-circuit}
A \emph{biochemical circuit} is a weighted directed graph
$\Gcond = (V, E, w)$ where:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item $V = \{v_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$: molecular species (metabolites, proteins,
        signalling intermediates), with $|V| = N$.
  \item $E = \{e_{ij}\}$: directed reaction from species $i$ to species $j$.
  \item $w_{ij} = k_{ij}^{+}\,c_j$: edge conductance, the product of the
        forward rate constant $k_{ij}^{+}$ and the substrate concentration
        $c_j$.
\end{itemize}
Each node $v_i$ carries a \emph{node potential} $\varpotential_i$ defined
below.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Chemical Potential as Categorical Depth}

\begin{definition}[Categorical Depth]
\label{def:categorical-depth}
The \emph{categorical depth} of node $i$ in state $\sigma_i$ is the
self-information (surprisal):
\begin{equation}
  \Depth_i(\sigma_i) = -\log_2 P(\sigma_i),
  \label{eq:catdepth}
\end{equation}
where $P(\sigma_i)$ is the stationary probability of state $\sigma_i$
under the network dynamics.  High $\Depth_i$ denotes a rare, informative
state; low $\Depth_i$ denotes a common, uninformative state.  The Shannon
entropy~\citep{Shannon1948} is the expected categorical depth:
$H(P) = \mathbb{E}[\Depth_i(\sigma_i)]$.
\end{definition}

Categorical depth measures how many binary distinctions are required to
specify that node $v_i$ is in state $\sigma_i$ --- the information-theoretic
``depth'' of the state in the partition hierarchy.

\begin{theorem}[Chemical Potential as Categorical Depth]
\label{thm:mu-catdepth}
For an ideal solution at thermodynamic equilibrium, the chemical potential
of species $i$ at concentration $c_i$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \varpotential_i
  = \mu_i^{\circ} + RT\ln c_i
  = -\kB T \ln 2 \cdot \Depth_i(\sigma_i) + \gamma_i,
  \label{eq:mu-catdepth}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_i = \mu_i^{\circ} + RT \ln 2 \cdot \log_2 Z_i$ is a
node-specific constant absorbing the molecular partition function $Z_i$.
The circuit node potential equals the categorical depth up to the thermal
energy scale $\kB T \ln 2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For species $i$ in an ideal solution, the Boltzmann factor gives the
equilibrium probability of microstate $\sigma_i$ as
\begin{equation}
  P(\sigma_i)
  = Z_i^{-1} \exp\!\left(-\frac{\varpotential_i}{RT}\right).
  \label{eq:boltzmann}
\end{equation}
Taking $-\log_2$ of both sides:
\begin{equation}
  \Depth_i
  = \frac{\varpotential_i}{RT \ln 2} + \log_2 Z_i.
  \label{eq:depth-from-mu}
\end{equation}
Solving for $\varpotential_i$ and collecting the constant $\gamma_i$
yields \Cref{eq:mu-catdepth}.
\end{proof}

This theorem is the keystone of the circuit model.  The electrical
potential at a circuit node equals, up to the universal factor
$\kB T \ln 2$, the categorical depth of the corresponding molecular
species.  Reactions run downhill in categorical depth space, exactly
as electric current flows from high to low electrical potential.  The
analogy is not poetic --- it is exact.

\subsection{Node Voltages: Chemical Potentials}

The \emph{node voltage} at species~$i$ is the chemical potential
\begin{equation}
  \varpotential_i
  = \kB T\,\ln\!\frac{c_i}{c_{\mathrm{ref}}},
  \label{eq:node-voltage}
\end{equation}
where $c_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is a reference concentration (typically
$1\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}$).

\subsection{Edge Currents and Conductances}

\begin{definition}[Edge Current and Conductance]
\label{def:edge-current}
The circuit current on edge $e_{ij}$ is the net reaction flux:
\begin{equation}
  J_{ij} = k_{ij}^{+}\,c_i - k_{ij}^{-}\,c_j.
  \label{eq:edge-current}
\end{equation}
The circuit conductance is:
\begin{equation}
  w_{ij} = \frac{k_{ij}^{+}\,c_i}{RT}.
  \label{eq:conductance}
\end{equation}
For enzymatic reactions under Michaelis-Menten kinetics, the
\emph{nonlinear conductance} is:
\begin{equation}
  w_{ij}^{\mathrm{MM}}
  = \frac{k_{\mathrm{cat}}\,[E]_T}{RT\,(K_m + c_j)},
  \label{eq:mm-conductance}
\end{equation}
where $k_{\mathrm{cat}}$ is the catalytic rate constant, $[E]_T$ is total
enzyme concentration, and $K_m$ is the Michaelis constant.
\end{definition}

\subsection{The Michaelis-Menten Enzyme as Biochemical Transistor}

Equation~\eqref{eq:mm-conductance} reveals the two operating regimes of
a Michaelis-Menten enzyme:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Ohmic regime} ($c_j \ll K_m$): conductance
    $w_{ij}^{\mathrm{MM}} \approx k_{\mathrm{cat}}[E]_T/(RT\,K_m)$ is
    approximately constant --- the enzyme acts as a linear resistor.
  \item \textbf{Saturating regime} ($c_j \gg K_m$): conductance
    $w_{ij}^{\mathrm{MM}} \approx k_{\mathrm{cat}}[E]_T/(RT\,c_j)$
    falls with substrate --- the enzyme acts as a \emph{current source},
    delivering a fixed maximal flux $V_{\max} = k_{\mathrm{cat}}[E]_T$
    independent of driving potential.
\end{itemize}
This transition from resistor to current source is exactly the
characteristic of a transistor operating from the linear to saturation
region.  The Michaelis-Menten enzyme is the biochemical transistor.
Allosteric enzymes add gain and switching: cooperative binding ($n_H > 1$)
produces a sigmoidal $J$--$c$ relationship analogous to a transistor
with positive feedback.  This makes allosteric enzymes the biological
equivalent of operational amplifiers: high gain, sharp threshold,
bistable switching.

\begin{remark}[Implications for Disease]
The transistor character of enzymes has direct implications for disease.
A mutation that shifts $K_m$ by one order of magnitude moves an enzyme
from its saturating regime into its ohmic regime (or vice versa), changing
the circuit element type --- not merely the parameter value.  This is a
qualitative, not quantitative, change in circuit topology, which is why
many disease mutations have disproportionately large phenotypic effects.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Kirchhoff Analogs: Formal Derivations}

\paragraph{KCL from Stoichiometric Mass Balance.}

\begin{theorem}[Biochemical KCL]
\label{thm:biochem-kcl}
At steady state, the net flux entering any node $v_i$ vanishes:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{\substack{j:\,(j,i)\in E}} J_{ji}
  - \sum_{\substack{j:\,(i,j)\in E}} J_{ij}
  = 0,
  \qquad \forall\, v_i \in V.
  \label{eq:ch08-kcl}
\end{equation}
This is the exact biochemical analog of Kirchhoff's current
law~\citep{Kirchhoff1847}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At steady state, $\mathrm{d}c_i/\mathrm{d}t = 0$.  Expanding via the
stoichiometric matrix $\mathbf{S}$:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}c_i}{\mathrm{d}t}
  = \sum_{r} S_{ir}\,v_r(\mathbf{c})
  = 0,
\end{equation}
where $S_{ir}$ is the stoichiometric coefficient of species $i$ in
reaction $r$ and $v_r$ is the net flux of reaction $r$.  Decomposing
each net flux into directed elementary steps: for a reaction $j \to i$
with mass-action kinetics, $v_{ji} = k_{ji}^{+}\,c_j - k_{ji}^{-}\,c_i$.
Grouping by direction:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{(j,i)\in E} \bigl(k_{ji}^{+}\,c_j - k_{ji}^{-}\,c_i\bigr)
  - \sum_{(i,j)\in E} \bigl(k_{ij}^{+}\,c_i - k_{ij}^{-}\,c_j\bigr)
  = 0.
\end{equation}
Writing $J_{ji} = k_{ji}^{+}\,c_j - k_{ji}^{-}\,c_i$ for the net
directed flux on edge $(j,i)$ recovers \Cref{eq:ch08-kcl}, which has
the identical form to Kirchhoff's current law: the algebraic sum of
currents at each node is zero.
\end{proof}

The linearised form near equilibrium, using $w_{ij}$ as conductance and
$(\varpotential_i - \varpotential_j)$ as driving potential difference, gives
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{j:\,(j,i)\in E} w_{ji}\,(\varpotential_j - \varpotential_i)
  - \sum_{j:\,(i,j)\in E} w_{ij}\,(\varpotential_i - \varpotential_j) = 0,
  \label{eq:ch08-kcl-linear}
\end{equation}
in exact formal correspondence with the standard KCL balance at a
circuit node.

\paragraph{KVL from Thermodynamic Cycle Consistency.}

\begin{theorem}[Biochemical KVL]
\label{thm:biochem-kvl}
For every directed loop
$\ell = (i_1 \to i_2 \to \cdots \to i_k \to i_1)$ in $\Gcond$:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{(i,j)\in\ell}
  (\varpotential_i - \varpotential_j)
  = 0.
  \label{eq:ch08-kvl}
\end{equation}
This is the biochemical analog of Kirchhoff's voltage law, equivalent to
the Wegscheider conditions~\citep{Wegscheider1901} for thermodynamic
consistency.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The sum telescopes exactly:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{(i,j)\in\ell}
  (\varpotential_j - \varpotential_i)
  = (\varpotential_{i_2} - \varpotential_{i_1})
  + (\varpotential_{i_3} - \varpotential_{i_2})
  + \cdots
  + (\varpotential_{i_1} - \varpotential_{i_k})
  = 0.
\end{equation}
Expanding $\varpotential_i = \mu_i^{\circ} + RT\ln c_i$, the
concentration-dependent part gives
$RT \sum_{(i,j)} \ln(c_j/c_i) = RT \ln \prod_{(i,j)} c_j/c_i = 0$,
because the product over a closed cycle telescopes to 1.  The
standard-state part $\sum_{(i,j)} \Delta\mu_{ij}^{\circ} = 0$ is
precisely the Wegscheider condition for thermodynamic cycle
consistency~\citep{Wegscheider1901}.
\end{proof}

\Cref{eq:ch08-kvl} is the statement that detailed balance holds on loop
$\ell$.  The failure of \Cref{eq:ch08-kvl} for any loop is the biochemical
signature of disease: the circuit has a ``voltage drop'' around a closed
loop, which is thermodynamically impossible unless energy is being
irreversibly consumed or injected at that loop.

\begin{corollary}[Ohm's Law Analog]
\label{cor:ohms-law}
For mass-action kinetics near thermodynamic equilibrium, the net flux
satisfies
\begin{equation}
  J_{ij}
  \approx w_{ij}\,(\varpotential_i - \varpotential_j),
  \label{eq:ohms-law}
\end{equation}
in exact formal correspondence with Ohm's law $I = G \cdot V$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Net flux $J_{ij} = k_{ij}^{+}\,c_i\,(1 - e^{-(\varpotential_i - \varpotential_j)/RT})$.
For $|\varpotential_i - \varpotential_j| \ll RT$, linearising the
exponential:
$J_{ij} \approx (k_{ij}^{+}\,c_i/RT)\,(\varpotential_i - \varpotential_j)
= w_{ij}\,(\varpotential_i - \varpotential_j)$.
This is the near-equilibrium linear Onsager relation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Complete Analogy Table]
The biochemical circuit correspondence is exact:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
\textbf{Electrical circuit} & \textbf{Biochemical circuit} \\
\midrule
Node potential $V_i$ & Chemical potential $\varpotential_i = \mu_i^{\circ} + RT\ln c_i$ \\
Edge current $I_{ij}$ & Net reaction flux $J_{ij}$ \\
Conductance $G_{ij}$ & $k_{ij}^{+}\,c_i / RT$ \\
KCL & Stoichiometric mass balance \\
KVL & Wegscheider thermodynamic consistency \\
EMF source & Standard free energy $\Delta G_{ij}^{\circ}$ \\
Capacitance & Buffer capacity $\mathrm{d}c_i/\mathrm{d}\varpotential_i$ \\
Transistor & Michaelis-Menten enzyme \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\end{remark}

\subsection{Information-Theoretic Circuit Variables}

The chemical potential has an information-theoretic interpretation: since
$c_i \propto P(\sigma_i)$ (concentration is proportional to occupation
probability of state $\sigma_i$),
\begin{equation}
  \varpotential_i
  = -\kB T\,\ln P(\sigma_i)
  = \kB T\,\ln 2 \cdot \Depth_i,
  \label{eq:info-potential}
\end{equation}
where $\Depth_i$ is the partition depth of state $\sigma_i$.  Thus the
node voltage is exactly the categorical depth (up to the thermal scale
$\kB T\ln 2$), and the circuit equations are statements about the topology
of the partition landscape of the cell's state space.

\section{Sequential Resolution Dynamics}
\label{sec:sequential-dynamics}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Having established the circuit model, we address how it evolves.  The
Universal No Template Theorem (\Cref{thm:universal-no-template}) has a
direct dynamical consequence: because no bounded system can represent the
global state, the only well-defined dynamics is \emph{sequential constraint
propagation}.

\begin{theorem}[Sequential Resolution is Necessary]
\label{thm:sequential-necessary}
In the absence of a global reference state, the only well-defined dynamics
on a circuit graph is sequential constraint propagation.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Consider the alternatives.

\textbf{Simultaneous resolution.}  Resolving all nodes simultaneously
requires solving a coupled system representing the global state.  By
\Cref{thm:universal-no-template}, no bounded subsystem can represent this
global state.  The cell cannot solve the coupled system because it cannot
represent it.

\textbf{Continuous-time evolution.}  Continuous dynamics
$\dot{c}_i = F_i(\{c_j\})$ requires evaluating all coupling terms
simultaneously at each instant.  This is equivalent to simultaneous
resolution in the limit $\Delta t \to 0$.

\textbf{Sequential resolution.}  Each resolution step depends only on the
finite set of resolved predecessors.  No global state representation is
required.  The dynamics is well-defined at each step.

Since simultaneous and continuous dynamics require global state
representation and sequential resolution does not, sequential resolution
is the unique dynamics consistent with \Cref{thm:universal-no-template}.
\end{proof}

A central assumption of classical biochemical kinetics is that all reactions
proceed simultaneously.  The partition framework modifies this: because each
partition operation has a nonzero lag time $\tau_p > 0$
(\Cref{ch:axiomatic-basis}), a cell cannot resolve all its states
simultaneously.

\begin{proposition}[Sequential Resolution Necessity]
\label{prop:sequential}
A cell with partition lag $\tau_p > 0$ and $N$ distinct molecular species
resolves species at a maximal rate of $1/\tau_p$ per species, so the full
state cannot be resolved in parallel.  The dynamics are inherently sequential
in partition space.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each species requires one partition operation to distinguish it from its
neighbours.  Two partition operations cannot overlap in time without
creating an ambiguous intermediate state (from the Residue Principle,
\Cref{ch:axiomatic-basis}).  Therefore operations must be serialised,
giving a resolution rate of at most $1/\tau_p$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Partition Lag]
\label{def:ch08-partition-lag}
The partition lag for reaction edge $e_{ij}$ is:
\begin{equation}
  \tau_p^{ij}
  = \frac{\hbar}{\Delta E_{ij}} + \tau_{\mathrm{reorg},ij},
  \label{eq:partition-lag}
\end{equation}
where $\hbar/\Delta E_{ij}$ is the quantum-mechanical minimum transition
time from the energy-time uncertainty relation, and $\tau_{\mathrm{reorg},ij}$
is the network reorganisation time.
\end{definition}

The partition lag spans twelve orders of magnitude across reaction classes:
electron transfer ($\tau_p \sim 10^{-12}$--$10^{-9}$~s), proton transfer
($\sim 10^{-11}$--$10^{-8}$~s), enzymatic catalysis
($\sim 10^{-6}$--$10^{-3}$~s), gene expression ($\sim 10^{2}$--$10^{4}$~s).
This hierarchy provides the physical basis for multi-timescale decomposition
of cellular dynamics.

This motivates a \emph{step-discrete} time variable $t_n = n\,\tau_p$,
at which point the cell has resolved exactly $n$ partition steps.  The
discrete dynamics on node voltages are
\begin{equation}
  \varpotential_i(t_{n+1})
  = \varpotential_i(t_n)
    + \tau_p \sum_j w_{ij}(t_n)\,(\varpotential_j(t_n) - \varpotential_i(t_n)),
  \label{eq:discrete-dynamics}
\end{equation}
which is a discretised diffusion equation on the circuit graph.
The connection to Poincar\'{e} computing (\Cref{ch:poincare}) is direct:
each time step $\tau_p$ corresponds to one application of the backward
propagator $\Back$ in the trajectory completion algorithm.

\begin{definition}[Circuit Step]
\label{def:circuit-step}
A circuit step $t \to t+1$ consists of three operations:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), noitemsep]
  \item Selection of the next node $v_{i_{t+1}}$ to resolve.
  \item \emph{Resolution}: the node's fuzzy value collapses to a discrete
        output satisfying incoming constraints from resolved predecessors.
  \item \emph{Propagation}: boundary conditions updated for all successor
        nodes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Discrete Dynamics]
\label{thm:discrete-dynamics}
The circuit state $\Gcond^{(t+1)}$ depends on $\Gcond^{(t)}$ and the
resolution of node $v_{i_{t+1}}$ alone.  The dynamics is first-order
Markov in steps.
\end{theorem}

\section{Fuzzy State Representation}
\label{sec:fuzzy-states}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{figure*}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{panel2_fuzzy_propagation.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Fuzzy state propagation through the biochemical circuit.}
  Membership functions $\mu_i(c)$ for representative nodes propagate through
  the circuit via the Zadeh extension principle (sup-min composition,
  \Cref{eq:fuzzy-kcl}).  Left: Gaussian membership functions
  $\mu_i(c) = \exp(-(c-\bar{c}_i)^2/2\sigma_i^2)$ at input nodes.
  Centre: propagated membership at intermediate nodes, showing broadening
  of uncertainty with circuit depth.  Right: defuzzified macroscopic
  potential $\bar{\varpotential}_i^{\mathrm{macro}}$ versus the sharp
  classical value, illustrating that fuzzy averaging preserves the circuit's
  aggregate thermodynamic quantities while encoding the irreducible
  concentration uncertainty.}
  \label{fig:ch08-fuzzy-propagation}
\end{figure*}

Molecular concentrations in vivo are not precise real numbers --- they are
inherently uncertain due to thermal fluctuations, finite copy number, and
the measurement uncertainty of any detector.  The fuzzy-set
framework~\citep{Zadeh1965,Zadeh1978} encodes this uncertainty at the
level of the circuit model, without requiring a probabilistic model of the
noise sources.

\begin{definition}[Fuzzy Node State]
\label{def:fuzzy-state}
The \emph{fuzzy state} of node~$i$ is a membership function
$\mu_i : \mathbb{R}_{+} \to [0,1]$, where $\mu_i(c)$ is the degree to
which concentration $c$ is consistent with all available information about
node $i$.  $\mu_i(c) = 1$ means $c$ is fully consistent; $\mu_i(c) = 0$
means $c$ is excluded.
\end{definition}

Typical membership functions are triangular or Gaussian:
\begin{equation}
  \mu_i(c) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{(c - \bar{c}_i)^2}{2\sigma_i^2}\right),
  \label{eq:gaussian-membership}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{c}_i$ is the modal concentration and $\sigma_i$ is the
concentration uncertainty.  At initialisation, observed nodes receive
trapezoidal membership functions centred on the measurement $\pm \epsilon$
(measurement precision); unobserved nodes receive flat membership on
$[c_{\min}^i, c_{\max}^i]$ (maximum-entropy prior).

\begin{definition}[$\alpha$-Cuts]
\label{def:alpha-cuts}
For fuzzy state $\mu_i$, the $\alpha$-cut is
\begin{equation}
  [\mu_i]^{\alpha}
  = \{c \geq 0 : \mu_i(c) \geq \alpha\},
  \quad \alpha \in (0,1].
\end{equation}
When $\mu_i$ is upper semi-continuous, $[\mu_i]^{\alpha}$ is a closed
interval $[\underline{c}_i^{\alpha}, \overline{c}_i^{\alpha}]$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Fuzzy Kirchhoff Equations}

The Zadeh extension principle~\citep{Zadeh1965} lifts classical set
operations to fuzzy sets via the sup-min composition.  Applied to KCL,
the fuzzy current from node~$j$ to node~$i$ is computed by the
\emph{fuzzy addition} of potentials:
\begin{equation}
  (\mu_i \mathbin{\tilde{+}} \mu_j)(c)
  = \sup_{a+b=c} \min\!\bigl(\mu_i(a),\,\mu_j(b)\bigr).
  \label{eq:fuzzy-kcl}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Fuzzy KCL]
\label{thm:fuzzy-kcl}
At each node $v_i$, the fuzzy flux balance constraint is enforced by
intersecting the current membership function with the set of concentrations
consistent with the fuzzy mass balance:
\begin{equation}
  \mu_i \leftarrow \mu_i \cap \mu_i^{\mathrm{KCL}},
  \label{eq:fuzzy-kcl-update}
\end{equation}
where $(\mu_1 \cap \mu_2)(c) = \min(\mu_1(c), \mu_2(c))$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the Zadeh extension principle to the crisp constraint
$\sum_j k_{ji}^{+}\,c_j = \sum_j k_{ij}^{+}\,c_i$ (steady-state KCL,
\Cref{thm:biochem-kcl}).  At each $\alpha$-level, the fuzzy intervals for
all concentrations propagate through the linear flux equations by interval
arithmetic.  The intersection restricts $v_i$ to concentrations
simultaneously consistent with measurement and flux balance.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fuzzy KVL]
\label{thm:fuzzy-kvl}
For each directed cycle $\mathcal{C}$, the thermodynamic constraint
requires
\begin{equation}
  0 \in \mathrm{core}\!\bigl(\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{C}}\bigr),
  \label{eq:fuzzy-kvl}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{C}}$ is the fuzzy number obtained by
applying the extension principle to $\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}} (\varpotential_j - \varpotential_i)$.
Any assignment violating this condition has its membership reduced.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{thm:biochem-kvl}, in the crisp case the cycle potential sum is
zero.  The extension principle propagates fuzzy intervals; the constraint
$\widetilde{\Phi}_{\mathcal{C}}(0) = 1$ is enforced by restricting
cycle-node concentration intervals at each $\alpha$-level so that zero
is in the $\alpha$-cut.
\end{proof}

The macroscopic (defuzzified) chemical potential is recovered by the
centroid:
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\varpotential}_i^{\mathrm{macro}}
  = \kB T\,\ln\!\frac{\bar{c}_i^{\mathrm{macro}}}{c_{\mathrm{ref}}},
  \qquad
  \bar{c}_i^{\mathrm{macro}}
  = \frac{\displaystyle\int_0^\infty c\,\mu_i(c)\,\mathrm{d}c}
         {\displaystyle\int_0^\infty \mu_i(c)\,\mathrm{d}c}.
  \label{eq:defuzz}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Local Validation and Global Inconsistency}

At each individual edge $(i,j)$, the \emph{local validation residual} is
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_{ij}
  = \bigl|w_{ij}\,(\varpotential_i - \varpotential_j) - J_{ij}^{\mathrm{obs}}\bigr|,
  \label{eq:local-validation}
\end{equation}
where $J_{ij}^{\mathrm{obs}}$ is the observed flux on edge~$(i,j)$.

\begin{definition}[Local Validation]
\label{def:local-validation}
The local validation function $\mathcal{V}_i$ at node $v_i$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{V}_i(x_i^{*})
  = \prod_{j \in \mathrm{pred}(i)}
    \Phi_{ji}^{\mathrm{compat}}(x_j^{*}, x_i^{*}),
  \label{eq:validation}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_{ji}^{\mathrm{compat}} : \mathbb{R}^{d_j} \times \mathbb{R}^{d_i} \to [0,1]$
is the compatibility function on edge $(v_j, v_i)$.  The node
\emph{passes validation} if $\mathcal{V}_i \geq \theta$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Local Validation Always Passes]
\label{lem:local-passes}
For any assignment of node voltages $\{\varpotential_i\}$ consistent with
the local edge conductances, $\varepsilon_{ij} < \varepsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}$
for every edge $(i,j)$, even when the global loop holonomy $\HolL \neq 1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Local validation at edge $(i,j)$ checks only the ratio
$(\varpotential_i - \varpotential_j)$ against the expected value
$J_{ij}^{\mathrm{obs}}/w_{ij}$.  This ratio is determined solely by the
concentrations $c_i$ and $c_j$, which are locally consistent by
construction (they are measured at the same spatial location and time).
The holonomy $\HolL$ is a property of the \emph{loop} --- it measures
whether the accumulated phase discrepancy around a closed path is zero ---
and this accumulated discrepancy cannot be detected by inspecting any single
edge in isolation.
\end{proof}

\section{Backward Trajectories and Trajectory Completion}
\label{sec:backward-traj}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{figure*}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{panel3_reference_free.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Reference-free diagnosis via holonomy and backward-trajectory
  convergence.}
  Left: backward trajectory fan --- 25 trajectories from diverse starting
  conditions converging to the health cell (green disc) under repeated
  application of the backward propagator $\Back$, illustrating that the
  self-consistent fixed point is recovered without any stored healthy template.
  Right: Wegscheider log-holonomy $|\mathrm{hol}_\ell|$ versus loop length for

  \label{fig:ch08-reference-free}
\end{figure*}

The trajectory completion algorithm from \Cref{ch:poincare} finds the
maximum a posteriori (MAP) trajectory $\Traj^{*}$ given partial observations
and the circuit structure $\Gcond$.

\begin{definition}[MAP Backward Trajectory]
\label{def:map-traj}
Let node $v_i$ be observed in state $\sigma_i$ at time $T$.  The backward
trajectory $\Back_i$ is the maximum-a-posteriori causal history:
\begin{equation}
  \Traj^{*}
  = \operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\Traj}
    \;P(\Traj \mid \mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{obs}},\, \Gcond),
  \label{eq:ch08-map-traj}
\end{equation}
consistent with the network kinetics and topology, that terminates in
state $\sigma_i$ at time $T$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Time-Invariance of Backward Trajectories]
\label{thm:timeinv}
For an autonomous reaction network $\Gcond$ with time-independent rate
constants, the MAP backward trajectory depends only on the current state
$\sigma_i$ and the network $\Gcond$, not on the observation time $T$:
\begin{equation}
  \Back_i = \Back_i(\sigma_i, \Gcond),
  \quad \text{independent of } T.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The propensity functions $a_{ij}(\mathbf{n})$ depend only on the current
state $\mathbf{n}$, not on $t$.  The continuous-time Markov chain is
time-homogeneous:
$P(\mathbf{n}(t+s) = \mathbf{m} \mid \mathbf{n}(t) = \mathbf{n})
= P(\mathbf{n}(s) = \mathbf{m} \mid \mathbf{n}(0) = \mathbf{n})$.
Applying this shift to the conditional distribution of the past trajectory:
for any $\tau > 0$,
$P(\mathbf{x}(T-\tau) = \mathbf{m} \mid x_i(T) = \sigma_i, \Gcond)
= P(\mathbf{x}(-\tau) = \mathbf{m} \mid x_i(0) = \sigma_i, \Gcond)$.
The MAP over the left-hand side equals the MAP over the right-hand side
for all $T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Categorical Address]
\label{cor:address}
The backward trajectory $\Back_i(\sigma_i, \Gcond)$ is a fixed geometric
property of state $\sigma_i$ within the network's state space --- a
\emph{categorical address} independent of when the cell is observed.
\end{corollary}

The Viterbi algorithm computes $\Traj^{*}$ on the trit-encoded state space
$\{-1, 0, +1\}^N$.  At each backward step, the trit flip that maximises
\begin{equation}
  \log P(\text{state} \mid \text{predecessors})
  + \log P(\text{observation} \mid \text{state})
  \label{eq:viterbi-score}
\end{equation}
is selected.

\subsection{The Trajectory Completion Algorithm}

\begin{definition}[Constraint Operators]
\label{def:constraint-ops}
Define three operators on the fuzzy state space $(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}, d)$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), noitemsep]
  \item $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{KCL}}$: apply fuzzy KCL (\Cref{thm:fuzzy-kcl})
        at every node.
  \item $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{KVL}}$: apply fuzzy KVL (\Cref{thm:fuzzy-kvl})
        to every independent cycle.
  \item $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Back}}$: for each node, compute the Viterbi MAP
        backward trajectory from the current most-probable concentration;
        restrict membership to concentrations whose backward trajectory is
        self-consistent with the network.
\end{enumerate}
The combined operator is
$\mathcal{T} = \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Back}} \circ \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{KVL}} \circ \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{KCL}}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[No Reference in $\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Back}}$]
$\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Back}}$ does \emph{not} compare backward trajectories
against a healthy reference.  It checks whether the backward trajectory from
each node is self-consistent with the network topology and the Kirchhoff
constraints.  No external reference is needed.  This is consistent with the
Universal No Template Theorem.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Convergence via Banach Contraction}

\begin{definition}[Hausdorff Metric on Fuzzy States]
\label{def:hausdorff}
The Hausdorff-Pompeiu metric on the space of fuzzy states is
\begin{equation}
  d_H(\mu, \nu)
  = \sup_{\alpha \in (0,1]}
    d_{\mathrm{H}}\bigl([\mu]^{\alpha}, [\nu]^{\alpha}\bigr),
  \label{eq:hausdorff}
\end{equation}
where for closed intervals
$d_{\mathrm{H}}([a,b], [c,d]) = \max(|a-c|, |b-d|)$.
The product metric on the network state space is
$d(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}, \widetilde{\mathbf{Y}}) = \max_i d_H(\mu_i, \nu_i)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Convergence of Trajectory Completion]
\label{thm:convergence}
The operator
$\mathcal{T} = \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Back}} \circ \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{KVL}} \circ \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{KCL}}$
is a contraction on $(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}, d)$.  By the Banach
fixed-point theorem, iteration from any initial state converges to a unique
fixed point $\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{*}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We show each sub-operator is a strict contraction on
$(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}, d)$.

\emph{$\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{KCL}}$ is a contraction.}
At node $v_i$, KCL relates $c_i$ linearly to neighbour concentrations.
The resulting interval widths satisfy
\begin{equation}
  d_H\bigl([\mu_i^{\mathrm{KCL}}(\mathbf{X})]^{\alpha},\,
           [\mu_i^{\mathrm{KCL}}(\mathbf{Y})]^{\alpha}\bigr)
  \leq \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)}
    \frac{k_{ji}^{+}}{\sum_l k_{li}^{+}}\,
    d_H\bigl([\mu_j(\mathbf{X})]^{\alpha},\, [\mu_j(\mathbf{Y})]^{\alpha}\bigr),
\end{equation}
where the coefficients sum to at most 1.  Intersection with the prior can
only reduce distance.  Contraction constant $c_1 < 1$ whenever at least
one node is constrained by observation.

\emph{$\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{KVL}}$ is a contraction.}
The cycle constraint imposes a multiplicative relation on cycle-node
concentrations (\Cref{thm:biochem-kvl}).  Restriction narrows fuzzy
intervals toward the thermodynamic constraint manifold, with contraction
constant $c_2 < 1$.

\emph{$\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{Back}}$ is a contraction.}
The Viterbi backward trajectory identifies backward-consistent
concentrations.  Restriction reduces support width monotonically, with
$c_3 < 1$.

\emph{Composition.}
$c \leq c_1 c_2 c_3 < 1$.  The space $(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}, d)$ is
complete (finite product of complete spaces).  By the Banach fixed-point
theorem, there exists a unique fixed point $\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{*}$
with geometric convergence rate $c^n$.  The error after $n$ iterations
satisfies
$d(\mathcal{T}^n(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}_0), \widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{*})
\leq c^n/(1-c) \cdot d(\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}_0, \widetilde{\mathbf{X}}^{*})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Crisp Convergence Under Sufficient Observations]
\label{cor:crisp-convergence}
If the observations form a dominating set of $\Gcond$ (every unobserved
node has at least one observed neighbour), the fixed point is crisp:
$\mu_i^{*}$ has single-point support for all $i$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Computational Complexity]
\label{prop:complexity}
For a network with $N$ species, $R$ reactions, maximum degree $\Delta$,
and backward trajectory depth $L$, one iteration requires $O(N \cdot R \cdot L)$.
Convergence in $O(\log(1/\varepsilon)/\log(1/c))$ iterations gives total
complexity $O(N \cdot R \cdot L \cdot \log(1/\varepsilon))$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Health as Self-Consistency of the Fixed Point}

\begin{definition}[Self-Consistent Health]
\label{def:self-consistent-health}
The MAP trajectory $\Traj^{*}$ represents a \emph{healthy state} if and
only if
\begin{equation}
  \HolL(\Traj^{*}) = \mathrm{Id}
  \quad \text{for all loops } \ell \text{ in } \Gcond.
  \label{eq:ch08-health-criterion}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Health is Self-Consistency]
\label{thm:health-self-consistency}
A cell is healthy if and only if the trajectory completion algorithm
converges to a self-consistent fixed point: a state in which every node's
backward trajectory closes on the network itself.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$(\Rightarrow)$ If the cell is healthy, all reaction fluxes satisfy KCL
and KVL, all cycle constraints are met, and the network is in a
self-sustaining steady state.  The backward trajectory of every node
traces the most probable causal history within the network.  Since the
network is self-sustaining, these trajectories close: every node's history
is explained by other nodes in the network.  The constraint system is
self-consistent.

$(\Leftarrow)$ If the constraint system is self-consistent, every Kirchhoff
constraint is satisfied, every cycle closes thermodynamically, and every
backward trajectory is explained within the network.  This is precisely the
condition for the network to be in a viable, self-sustaining steady state.
\end{proof}

This criterion requires no external template.  It tests only whether the
trajectory, when traversed around each closed loop, returns exactly to its
starting point in the partition-space representation --- a purely internal
consistency check implementable with the information available within the
bounded cell, consistent with \Cref{cor:self-consistency}.

\section{Disease as Topological Inconsistency}
\label{sec:topo-inconsistency}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{definition}[Loop Holonomy]
\label{def:holonomy}
For a directed loop $\ell = (i_1 \to i_2 \to \cdots \to i_k \to i_1)$
in $\Gcond$, the \emph{loop holonomy} is the composed constraint map:
\begin{equation}
  \HolL
  = \Phi_{i_k, i_1} \circ \Phi_{i_{k-1}, i_k} \circ \cdots \circ \Phi_{i_1, i_2}.
  \label{eq:holonomy}
\end{equation}
The loop is consistent if and only if $\HolL = \mathrm{Id}$ (up to
tolerance $\epsilon$).  Equivalently in terms of rate constants, the
\emph{log-holonomy} is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{hol}_\ell
  = \sum_{(i,j)\in\ell} \bigl(L_{ij} - L_{ji}\bigr),
  \qquad
  L_{ij} = \ln k_{ij}^{+},
  \label{eq:log-holonomy}
\end{equation}
which equals zero if and only if detailed balance holds on loop $\ell$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Geometric Interpretation]
The term ``holonomy'' is used deliberately.  In differential geometry,
holonomy measures the failure of parallel transport around a loop to
return a vector to its original orientation.  Here, it measures the
failure of constraint propagation around a biochemical loop to return a
signal to its original value.  The mathematical structure is identical:
a curvature tensor on the connection defined by the kinetic rate constants.
\end{remark}

Detailed balance (the Wegscheider condition) requires $\mathrm{hol}_\ell = 0$
for every loop, equivalently $\HolL = 1$.  Disease violates this:

\begin{definition}[Topological Disease]
\label{def:topo-disease}
A biochemical circuit is in a \emph{diseased state} if there exists at
least one loop $\ell$ in $\Gcond$ such that $\HolL \neq 1$, equivalently
$\mathrm{hol}_\ell \neq 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Disease as Inconsistency]
\label{thm:disease-inconsistency}
Disease is a state of the cellular circuit in which at least one loop has
non-trivial holonomy:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Disease}
  \iff
  \exists\, \ell \in \mathcal{L}(\Gcond) : \HolL \neq \mathrm{Id}.
  \label{eq:disease-condition}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the trajectory completion algorithm fails to converge to a
fully self-consistent fixed point.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$(\Rightarrow)$ If the circuit is diseased, the global state is not
self-consistent.  Feed-forward paths cannot generate inconsistency (they
have no loops to close), so inconsistency must arise from at least one
loop returning a value incompatible with its input.  Therefore
$\HolL \neq \mathrm{Id}$ for at least one loop.  In the trajectory
completion algorithm, the constraint operators (KCL, KVL, backward
trajectory) cannot all be simultaneously satisfied: the algorithm converges
to a fixed point with non-zero residual.

$(\Leftarrow)$ If loop $\ell$ has $\HolL \neq \mathrm{Id}$, the
constraint system is self-contradictory on that loop.  The KVL constraint
(\Cref{thm:biochem-kvl}) requires $\sum_{\mathcal{C}} \Delta\varpotential_{ij} = 0$,
but the non-trivial holonomy means the potentials do not close around the
cycle.  This thermodynamic inconsistency generates a net free energy leak
(or sink) on the loop, driving irreversible drift away from the
self-consistent manifold.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Disease is not defined as ``deviation from healthy.''  It is defined as
``the constraint system does not close.''  This is detectable from the
circuit's own topology and the Kirchhoff laws, with no external reference.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Residual Accumulation and Disease Progression}

\begin{definition}[Consistency Residual]
\label{def:consistency-residual}
The consistency residual of loop $\ell$ at step $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \delta_\ell^{(t)} = \|\HolL^{(t)} - \mathrm{Id}\|.
  \label{eq:consistency-residual}
\end{equation}
This is the magnitude of the holonomy --- the degree to which the loop
fails to close.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Residual Accumulation]
\label{thm:accumulation}
In a diseased circuit, consistency residuals accumulate monotonically:
\begin{equation}
  \delta_\ell^{(t+|\ell|)} \geq \delta_\ell^{(t)},
\end{equation}
where $|\ell|$ is the loop length.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At step $t$, the loop has residual $\delta_\ell^{(t)}$.  When the loop is
traversed again, each node resolves against the output of the previous
step, which already carries the accumulated residual.  Resolution against
inconsistent boundary conditions cannot reduce inconsistency: the
constraint functions propagate the residual forward.  Therefore
$\delta_\ell^{(t+|\ell|)} = \delta_\ell^{(t)} + \delta_{\mathrm{new}}
\geq \delta_\ell^{(t)}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Disease Progression is Monotonic]
\label{cor:progression}
Once a loop becomes inconsistent, it cannot spontaneously return to
consistency without external intervention.  Degenerative diseases
progress; they do not spontaneously reverse.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Holonomy Decomposition}

Every loop holonomy admits a unique decomposition into four mechanistically
distinct components:

\begin{theorem}[Holonomy Decomposition]
\label{thm:holonomy-decomp}
The holonomy of any loop decomposes as
\begin{equation}
  \HolL
  = \mathrm{Id}
  + \delta\HolL^{\mathrm{drift}}
  + \delta\HolL^{\mathrm{phase}}
  + \delta\HolL^{\mathrm{amp}}
  + \delta\HolL^{\mathrm{topo}},
  \label{eq:hol-decomp}
\end{equation}
corresponding to drift, phase, amplitude, and topological defect
contributions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write each constraint function as
$\Phi_{ij} = \Phi_{ij}^{(0)} + \delta\Phi_{ij}$,
where $\Phi_{ij}^{(0)}$ is the self-consistent constraint and
$\delta\Phi_{ij}$ is the perturbation.  In a healthy circuit,
$\prod_j \Phi_j^{(0)} = \mathrm{Id}$.  The first-order perturbation
decomposes by type: drift (DC offset in rates), phase (timing shifts),
amplitude (magnitude scaling), and topological (connectivity changes).
Each type is orthogonal to the others to first order.
\end{proof}

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*), itemsep=4pt]
  \item \textbf{Drift holonomy.}
        \begin{equation}
          \mathrm{hol}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{drift}}
          = \bigl\langle \mathrm{hol}_\ell \bigr\rangle_\ell,
          \label{eq:drift-hol}
        \end{equation}
        the average over all loops.  Represents a steady DC offset in
        log-rates: metabolic flux imbalance, nutrient depletion, or tonic
        enzyme inhibition.  Clinically: aging, metabolic syndrome, slow
        chronic disease.

  \item \textbf{Phase holonomy.}
        \begin{equation}
          \mathrm{hol}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{phase}}
          = \mathrm{Im}\!\left[\mathcal{F}\{\mathrm{hol}_\ell(t)\}(\omega_0)\right],
          \label{eq:phase-hol}
        \end{equation}
        the imaginary part of the Fourier transform at the dominant
        oscillatory frequency $\omega_0$.  Represents circadian
        dysregulation or disrupted ultradian rhythms.  Clinically: cardiac
        arrhythmia, circadian disorder, bipolar disorder.

  \item \textbf{Amplitude holonomy.}
        \begin{equation}
          \mathrm{hol}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{amp}}
          = \mathrm{Var}\!\left(\mathrm{hol}_\ell\right),
          \label{eq:amp-hol}
        \end{equation}
        the variance of $\mathrm{hol}_\ell$ over time.  Represents stochastic
        fluctuation pathology: excessive molecular noise in gene expression
        or metabolic fluxes.  Clinically: gain-of-function mutations,
        tumour suppressor loss.

  \item \textbf{Topological holonomy.}
        \begin{equation}
          \mathrm{hol}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{topo}}
          = \mathrm{hol}_\ell
            - \mathrm{hol}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{drift}}
            - \mathrm{hol}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{phase}}
            - \mathrm{hol}_{\ell}^{\mathrm{amp}},
          \label{eq:topo-hol}
        \end{equation}
        the residual after subtracting the three removable components.
        Cannot be eliminated by any modification of individual edge
        rate constants; removal requires a change in the topology of
        $\Gcond$ itself.  Clinically: cancer (autonomous loops),
        neurodegeneration, autoimmune disease.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Severity Hierarchy]
\label{prop:severity}
In the order of clinical severity and therapeutic difficulty, the four
holonomy types satisfy
\begin{equation}
  |\mathrm{hol}^{\mathrm{topo}}|
  > |\mathrm{hol}^{\mathrm{phase}}|
  > |\mathrm{hol}^{\mathrm{amp}}|
  > |\mathrm{hol}^{\mathrm{drift}}|.
  \label{eq:severity-hierarchy}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Drift holonomy is correctable by global scaling of enzyme activity (the
mildest intervention: supplementation or mild inhibition).  Amplitude
holonomy requires stabilising stochastic fluctuations (antioxidants,
chaperones, buffering capacity --- moderate interventions).  Phase holonomy
requires resynchronisation of oscillatory circuits (chronotherapy, pacemaker
stimulation --- complex interventions).  Topological holonomy cannot be
corrected without changing the circuit wiring; it requires gene therapy,
surgical bypass, or transplantation --- the most severe interventions.
The ordering of $|\mathrm{hol}|$ follows from the energy cost of each
corrective intervention and the partition reset bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Defect Types and Clinical Correlates]
\label{def:defect-types}
Circuit defects are classified by their topological character and
accumulation rate:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Drift defect:} $\delta \sim t/|\ell|$ per traversal.
        No single-traversal inconsistency is detectable, but cumulative
        multi-traversal drift exceeds tolerance.  Corresponds to aging and
        slow degenerative diseases.
  \item \textbf{Phase defect:} Timing mismatch,
        $\delta \sim \Delta\omega \cdot \tau_{\mathrm{eff}}$ per traversal.
        Corresponds to oscillatory disorders (cardiac arrhythmia, circadian
        disruption).
  \item \textbf{Amplitude defect:} Returned signal magnitude deviates,
        accumulation exponential: $\delta^{(n)} \sim g^n$.  Corresponds to
        gain-of-function or loss-of-function mutations.
  \item \textbf{Topological defect:} Loop structure itself is altered.
        Immediate maximal inconsistency ($\mathcal{C}_\ell = 0$).
        Corresponds to cancer (autonomous loops), autoimmune disease,
        neurodegeneration.
\end{enumerate}
Severity hierarchy: drift $\prec$ phase $\prec$ amplitude $\prec$ topological.
\end{definition}

\section{The Local Invisibility Theorem}
\label{sec:invisibility}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The holonomy decomposition reveals why cells cannot detect their own disease:
they validate locally, one edge at a time.  Global inconsistency accumulates
silently.

\begin{theorem}[Local Invisibility]
\label{thm:invisibility}
Let $\Gcond$ be a diseased circuit with inconsistent loop $\ell$.  Every
individual resolution step along $\ell$ passes local validation:
\begin{equation}
  \forall\, v_{i_j} \in \ell:
  \quad \mathcal{V}_{i_j}(x_{i_j}^{*}) \geq \theta,
  \label{eq:invisibility}
\end{equation}
yet the loop is globally inconsistent: $\HolL \neq \mathrm{Id}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At step 1, node $v_{i_1}$ is resolved and passes local validation.  At
step 2, $v_{i_2}$ resolves against the definite output of $v_{i_1}$ via
a well-defined constraint $\Phi_{i_1, i_2}$.  It passes local validation.
By induction, every node passes.

However, the loop holonomy compares the value propagated around the entire
loop with the starting value:
\begin{equation}
  x_{i_1}^{*\prime}
  = \Phi_{i_k, i_1} \circ \Phi_{i_{k-1}, i_k} \circ \cdots \circ \Phi_{i_1, i_2}(x_{i_1}^{*}).
\end{equation}
Even if each $\Phi$ is individually well-behaved, their composition around
a loop need not be the identity:
$\Phi_{i_k, i_1} \circ \cdots \circ \Phi_{i_1, i_2} \neq \mathrm{Id}$.
The discrepancy $x_{i_1}^{*\prime} - x_{i_1}^{*}$ is the holonomy --- a
purely global quantity invisible to any local validation step.

More precisely: the local validation residual $\varepsilon_{ij}$
(\Cref{eq:local-validation}) depends only on $\varpotential_i$,
$\varpotential_j$, $w_{ij}$, and $J_{ij}^{\mathrm{obs}}$ --- four
quantities determined by the local state at edge $(i,j)$.  The holonomy
$\mathrm{hol}_\ell = \sum_{(i,j)\in\ell}(L_{ij} - L_{ji})$ is a sum
over \emph{all} edges of loop $\ell$.  A nonzero sum $\mathrm{hol}_\ell$
is consistent with each individual term $(L_{ij} - L_{ji})$ being small,
provided the small contributions accumulate coherently around the loop.
Local validation checks only $(L_{ij} - L_{ji})$ in isolation, which can
satisfy $\varepsilon_{ij} < \varepsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}$ for any value of
the loop sum.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Drift Invisibility: The Boiling Frog Mechanism}

\begin{theorem}[Drift Invisibility]
\label{thm:drift-invisibility}
A drift defect with per-traversal holonomy $\|\HolL - \mathrm{Id}\| = \delta$
becomes detectable (exceeds tolerance $\epsilon$) only after
$n^{*} = \lceil\epsilon/\delta\rceil$ traversals.  For $\delta \ll \epsilon$,
this represents a long latency period during which:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), noitemsep]
  \item Every local validation step passes.
  \item The global consistency residual grows linearly:
        $\delta_\ell^{(n)} \approx n\delta$.
  \item The circuit state drifts monotonically from the self-consistent
        manifold.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After $n$ traversals, the accumulated holonomy is
$\HolL^n \approx \mathrm{Id} + n(\HolL - \mathrm{Id})$ to first order.
Each local step sees only the single-step residual $\delta < \epsilon$
and passes validation.  The global residual $n\delta$ is invisible to
local checks until $n\delta > \epsilon$ at $n^{*} = \lceil\epsilon/\delta\rceil$.
\end{proof}

This is the ``boiling frog'' mechanism of degenerative disease.  The cell
walks into pathology one valid step at a time.  No single step triggers an
alarm.

\begin{corollary}[Drift Invisibility (Edge-Level)]
\label{cor:drift-invisibility}
Even drift holonomy (\Cref{eq:drift-hol}), the mildest form, is locally
invisible.  A DC offset in log-rates distributes uniformly around the loop;
each edge's individual contribution is below the detection threshold.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Phase Invisibility]
\label{cor:phase-invisibility}
Phase holonomy (\Cref{eq:phase-hol}) is locally invisible because the
timing mismatch $\Delta\omega \cdot \tau_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is distributed
across all edges in the oscillatory loop.  Each edge-level timing
measurement lies within the normal variation of the local kinetics.
Detection requires monitoring the phase of the full oscillatory cycle,
not any individual step.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Amplitude Invisibility]
\label{cor:amp-invisibility}
Amplitude holonomy (\Cref{eq:amp-hol}) is locally invisible during the
linear growth phase.  The local residual $\varepsilon_{ij}$ grows as
$g^n/|\ell|$ per edge, which remains below $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}$
for $n < \log_g(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tol}} \cdot |\ell|)$ traversals.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Pharmacological Paradox Resolution]
\label{rem:paradox}
\Cref{thm:invisibility} resolves the \emph{pharmacological paradox of
cellular self-diagnosis failure}: why do cells fail to detect and correct
their own disease states, even when the diseased state is thermodynamically
unfavourable?  The answer is that cells have direct access only to local
information (each molecular species ``sees'' only its immediate reactants),
and \Cref{thm:invisibility} proves that local information is insufficient
to detect loop-level inconsistency.  Holonomy detection requires traversing
a closed loop --- comparing a quantity at a node before and after a complete
circuit --- which is beyond the local resolution of any single molecular
sensor.

An external observer running the trajectory completion algorithm
(\Cref{thm:convergence}) can detect the inconsistency that the cell cannot,
not because the observer has a template, but because the observer can check
self-consistency across the entire circuit simultaneously.  The observer
doesn't need to know what ``healthy'' looks like.  They need to check
whether Kirchhoff's laws hold.
\end{remark}

\section{The Consistency Index}
\label{sec:consistency-index}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{figure*}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{panel4_signal_variance.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Signal variance preceding mean shift along a disease trajectory.}
  Time series of macroscopic signal variance $\mathrm{Var}(x^{\mathrm{macro}}(t))$
  (blue) and mean $\langle x^{\mathrm{macro}}(t)\rangle$ (orange) during
  disease progression parameterised by increasing loop holonomy
  $|\mathrm{hol}_\ell(t)|$.  The variance becomes detectably elevated
  before the mean departs from the healthy reference range, consistent
  with the variance--holonomy relation $\mathrm{Var}(x^{\mathrm{macro}}) =
  \kB T / |\mathrm{hol}_\ell|$ and \Cref{prop:variance-precedes}.
  The dashed vertical line marks the mean-shift onset time $t_d$; the
  solid vertical line marks the earlier variance-detection threshold.}
  \label{fig:ch08-signal-variance}
\end{figure*}

\begin{definition}[Loop Consistency Index]
\label{def:loop-consistency}
For loop $\ell$ with log-holonomy $\mathrm{hol}_\ell$ and maximum
permissible holonomy $\mathrm{hol}_{\max}$, the loop consistency index is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C}_\ell
  = 1 - \frac{|\mathrm{hol}_\ell|}{\mathrm{hol}_{\max}}.
  \label{eq:loop-consistency}
\end{equation}
The global consistency index is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{global}}
  = \frac{1}{|L|}\sum_{\ell \in L} \mathcal{C}_\ell,
  \label{eq:global-consistency}
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the set of all independent loops (cycle basis) of $\Gcond$.
$\mathcal{C} = 1$ for a perfectly self-consistent circuit;
$\mathcal{C} = 0$ for maximally inconsistent.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Relationship to Macroscopic Signals}

\begin{theorem}[Signal-Consistency Relation]
\label{thm:signal-consistency}
The consistency index is related to macroscopic signal statistics by:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C}(\Gcond)
  = 1 - \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^{K}
    \frac{\mathrm{Var}(\Delta\sigma_k)}{\mathrm{Var}_{\max}(\Delta\sigma_k)},
  \label{eq:signal-consistency}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\sigma_k = \sigma_k^{(t+1)} - \sigma_k^{(t)}$ is the
step-to-step change in macroscopic signal $k$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In a consistent circuit, sequential signals are tightly constrained:
$\Delta\sigma_k$ is small and $\mathrm{Var}(\Delta\sigma_k)$ is low.
In an inconsistent circuit, loop holonomies inject additional variation:
each traversal contributes a residual $\delta_\ell$ to the signal change.
Over a window $\mathcal{W}$:
$\mathrm{Var}(\Delta\sigma_k) = \sum_{\ell \ni \sigma_k} \delta_\ell^2/|\ell|$.
Normalising and averaging gives \Cref{eq:signal-consistency}.
\end{proof}

The macroscopic signal variance is related to holonomy by:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Var}\!\left(x_i^{\mathrm{macro}}\right)
  = \frac{\kB T}{|\mathrm{hol}_\ell|},
  \label{eq:variance-holonomy}
\end{equation}
where $\ell$ is any loop containing node~$i$.  This is the key observable
consequence: as holonomy grows with disease progression, signal variance
grows inversely.  Crucially, variance increase \emph{precedes} the shift
in mean --- the cell can be in a healthy-mean-but-high-variance state
before the mean biomarker moves outside the reference range.

\begin{corollary}[Measurable Macroscopic Signals]
\label{cor:measurable-signals}
For a cell, the relevant macroscopic signals are:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{pH}: net acid-base balance of all metabolic loops.
  \item \textbf{Volume}: osmotic balance across membrane transport loops.
  \item \textbf{Membrane potential $\Delta\psi$}: consistency of ion transport loops.
  \item \textbf{Redox state (NAD$^+$/NADH)}: consistency of electron transport loops.
  \item \textbf{ATP/ADP ratio}: consistency of energy metabolism loops.
\end{enumerate}
The step-to-step variance of these signals provides a direct estimate of
$\mathcal{C}(\Gcond)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Variance Precedes Mean]
\label{prop:variance-precedes}
Along a disease trajectory parameterised by increasing $|\mathrm{hol}_\ell(t)|$,
the signal variance $\mathrm{Var}(x^{\mathrm{macro}}(t))$ becomes
detectably elevated before the mean $\langle x^{\mathrm{macro}}(t)\rangle$
departs from the healthy reference range.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The mean
$\langle x^{\mathrm{macro}}\rangle = \kB T\,\Depth_{\mathrm{health}}$
is governed by the partition depth of the healthy attractor, which changes
only when $|\Depth(t) - \Depth_{\mathrm{health}}| > \delta$.  By contrast,
the variance $\kB T / |\mathrm{hol}_\ell|$ starts increasing as soon as
$|\mathrm{hol}_\ell| > 0$, which occurs before the onset time $t_d$.
Therefore variance becomes detectable first.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Phase Transitions in the Consistency Landscape}

The consistency landscape $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{global}}(\{G_{ij}\})$
undergoes a sharp phase transition at a critical holonomy
$\mathrm{hol}_{\mathrm{crit}} \sim \kB T$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{global}}
  \approx
  \begin{cases}
    1 - O(\mathrm{hol}/\kB T) & \mathrm{hol} < \mathrm{hol}_{\mathrm{crit}}, \\
    0 + O(1) & \mathrm{hol} > \mathrm{hol}_{\mathrm{crit}}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:consistency-transition}
\end{equation}
Below $\mathrm{hol}_{\mathrm{crit}}$, the circuit shows slight heterogeneity
but remains in the healthy basin.  Above it, a disease cascade propagates:
loops with high holonomy perturb neighbouring loops through shared edges,
driving their holonomy above threshold in turn.

\begin{theorem}[Monotonic Decline]
\label{thm:monotonic-decline}
In the absence of external intervention, the consistency index decreases
monotonically when $\mathcal{C} < 1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{thm:accumulation}, consistency residuals accumulate.  Since
$\mathcal{C}$ is a decreasing function of residuals, $\mathcal{C}$
decreases.  Furthermore, loops sharing nodes propagate inconsistency
between each other, so the number of inconsistent loops can only increase.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Percolation Transition]
\label{cor:percolation}
Disease progression exhibits a phase transition at critical consistency
$\mathcal{C}^{*}$.  For $\mathcal{C} > \mathcal{C}^{*}$, inconsistencies
remain localised.  For $\mathcal{C} < \mathcal{C}^{*}$, inconsistencies
percolate through the circuit graph, causing rapid global decline.
\end{corollary}

\section{Pathway Architecture as Circuit Topology}
\label{sec:pathway-topology}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Scale-Free Network Structure}

Metabolic networks and gene regulatory networks are scale-free: the degree
distribution follows a power law,
\begin{equation}
  P(d) \sim d^{-\alpha}, \qquad \alpha \approx 2.5,
  \label{eq:scale-free}
\end{equation}
as measured in the universal metabolic network of \textit{E.\ coli} and
human hepatocytes~\citep{barabasi2004network}.  The scale-free property
implies the existence of \emph{hub nodes}: species with degree
$d_i \gg \bar{d}$, where $\bar{d}$ is the mean degree.

The scale-free structure has two important consequences for disease.
First, the network is robust to random failures: removing a randomly
chosen low-degree node rarely disrupts the circuit because such nodes
participate in few loops.  Second, the network is fragile to targeted hub
failures: removing a hub node simultaneously disrupts every loop in which
it participates, which may be a macroscopic fraction of the total.

This fragility is not a design flaw.  It is a thermodynamic necessity:
hubs exist because reactions run downhill in categorical depth space, and
high-degree nodes occupy the deepest wells of the partition landscape ---
the most thermodynamically favourable configurations to which many
reaction pathways lead.

\begin{definition}[Hub Node]
\label{def:hub}
A node $v_i$ is a \emph{hub} if $d_i > \bar{d} + 2\sigma_d$, where
$\sigma_d$ is the degree standard deviation.  In metabolic networks, hubs
include ATP, NADH, CoA, and central carbon intermediates such as
acetyl-CoA.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Hub Significance for Disease}

\begin{theorem}[Hub Vulnerability]
\label{thm:hub-vulnerability}
An inconsistency at a hub node $v_h$ propagates to all $\deg^{+}(v_h)$
downstream loops simultaneously:
\begin{equation}
  \delta_{v_h} > 0
  \implies
  \delta_\ell > 0 \quad \forall\, \ell \ni v_h.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The resolved value $x_h^{*}$ serves as boundary condition for all
successor nodes.  If $x_h^{*}$ carries residual $\delta_h$, this
propagates through every outgoing edge by the definition of constraint
propagation (\Cref{def:constraint-ops}).  Each loop through $v_h$
inherits the residual.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Hub Holonomy Contribution]
\label{prop:hub-holonomy}
A hub node $v_h$ of degree $d_h$ participates in at least
$\binom{d_h}{2}$ distinct loops.  Its failure (loss of connectivity or
activity) contributes a fraction
\begin{equation}
  f_h = \frac{\binom{d_h}{2}}{|L|}
  \label{eq:hub-fraction}
\end{equation}
of the total number of independent loops to nonzero holonomy, where $|L|$
is the total number of independent loops in $\Gcond$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each pair of edges incident to $v_h$ defines at least one loop through
$v_h$ by the cycle space structure of the graph.  There are $\binom{d_h}{2}$
such pairs.  Each loop affected by hub failure acquires nonzero holonomy
because the altered hub conductance changes $L_{ij}$ for every incident
edge.  Division by $|L|$ gives the fractional contribution.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Multi-System Dysfunction]
\label{cor:multisystem}
Disruption of a central metabolic hub (ATP, NAD$^+$/NADH, CoA) causes
simultaneous inconsistency in all dependent loops, explaining why
mitochondrial diseases present with multi-system dysfunction.
\end{corollary}

Hub failure is therefore a highly leveraged disease mechanism: a single
molecular lesion at a hub simultaneously disrupts holonomy across a
macroscopic fraction of the circuit's loops.  This is the mechanistic
basis for the lethality of mutations affecting central metabolic enzymes
(\eg, citrate synthase, complex~I subunits).

\subsection{Pathway Compatibility}

\begin{theorem}[Pathway Compatibility]
\label{thm:pathway-compatibility}
A feed-forward subcircuit $\Gcond_{\mathrm{ff}}$ persists in $\Gcond$ if
and only if it is consistent with all loops it participates in:
\begin{equation}
  \forall\, \ell \in \mathcal{L}(\Gcond)
  \text{ s.t. } \ell \cap \Gcond_{\mathrm{ff}} \neq \emptyset:
  \quad \HolL = \mathrm{Id}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

This explains why glycolysis has its particular 10-step architecture.
Among the vast space of possible glucose-to-pyruvate cascades, the
standard pathway is one that maintains loop consistency with the TCA
cycle, pentose phosphate pathway, gluconeogenesis, and ATP/ADP cycle.
The pathway fits the circuit.

\subsection{Parallel Conductances and Redundancy}

Many metabolic pathways are redundant: multiple enzymes catalyse the same
or equivalent reactions, providing parallel edges in $\Gcond$.  The
effective conductance of a parallel bundle is
\begin{equation}
  w_{ij}^{\mathrm{eff}}
  = \sum_{k=1}^{M} w_{ij}^{(k)},
  \label{eq:parallel-conductance}
\end{equation}
where $k$ indexes the $M$ parallel pathways.

\begin{proposition}[Isoform Redundancy]
\label{prop:isoform-redundancy}
Isoform-degenerate edges (different enzymes catalysing the same reaction)
act as parallel conductances.  Even if one isoform is inactivated, the
categorical state transition continues; only its rate changes.  This is
the molecular basis for metabolic network robustness to enzyme knockouts.
\end{proposition}

Redundancy reduces vulnerability to single-edge disease: if one pathway
is blocked ($w_{ij}^{(k)} \to 0$), the remaining $M-1$ paths maintain
flux, and the holonomy contribution from that edge is suppressed.  This
is why pharmacological mono-therapy is often insufficient for cancer:
cancer cells upregulate parallel pathways to bypass the inhibited edge.

\section{Therapeutic Intervention: Drug Design as Sparse Conductance Modification}
\label{sec:therapeutic}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The circuit framework gives a precise formulation of what a drug must
accomplish: it must modify one or more edge conductances such that the
diseased loops become consistent.

\begin{definition}[Therapeutic Intervention]
\label{def:therapy}
A therapeutic intervention modifies the circuit such that the trajectory
completion algorithm converges to a self-consistent fixed point where
previously it did not:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{C}(\Gcond_{\mathrm{post}}) > \mathcal{C}(\Gcond_{\mathrm{pre}}).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

In the circuit model, a drug targeting enzyme $e_{ij}$ modifies the
conductance: $w_{ij} \mapsto w_{ij}(1 - \eta_D)$, where
$\eta_D \in [0,1]$ is the fractional inhibition ($\eta_D > 0$: inhibition;
$\eta_D < 0$: activation).

\begin{definition}[Optimal Drug Design]
\label{def:drug-design}
An optimal drug restores self-consistency while minimising total conductance
perturbation (minimal pharmacological load):
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\{\eta_{ij}\}} \sum_{ij} |\eta_{ij}|
  \quad \text{s.t.}
  \quad \HolL^{\mathrm{treated}} = \mathrm{Id} \;\; \forall\, \ell \in \mathcal{L}(\Gcond),
  \quad \eta_{ij} \in [0,1].
  \label{eq:drug-opt}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The constraint is not ``restore trajectories to match a healthy reference.''
It is ``make the loops close.''  The $\ell_1$ objective ensures only the
smallest set of reactions is targeted.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Drug Design as Linear Programme]
\label{prop:drug-lp}
Under first-order linearisation of the holonomy in $\{\eta_{ij}\}$, the
optimisation~\eqref{eq:drug-opt} is a linear programme solvable in
polynomial time.  The $\ell_1$ objective promotes sparsity, identifying
the smallest set of reactions that need to be targeted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Linearising $\HolL^{\mathrm{treated}}$ about the diseased state gives
feasibility constraints that are linear inequalities in $\{\eta_{ij}\}$.
The $\ell_1$ objective is cast as a standard LP by introducing variables
$s_{ij} \geq |\eta_{ij}|$: minimise $\sum s_{ij}$ subject to
$s_{ij} \geq \pm\eta_{ij}$ and feasibility.  This LP is polynomial-time
solvable by interior-point methods~\citep{Dantzig1963}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Compensation Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Compensation]
\label{thm:compensation}
An inconsistent loop $\ell$ with holonomy $\HolL \neq \mathrm{Id}$ can
be compensated by modifying a single constraint function on one edge,
provided the holonomy is invertible ($\det(\HolL) \neq 0$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $\HolL = A \circ \Phi_{ij} \circ B$ where $A$ and $B$ are the
compositions before and after edge $(i,j)$.  Setting
$\Phi_{ij}^{*} = A^{-1} \circ B^{-1}$ gives
$\HolL^{\mathrm{mod}} = \mathrm{Id}$.  This requires invertibility of
$A$ and $B$, which follows from $\det(\HolL) \neq 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Irreversible Damage]
\label{cor:irreversible}
When $\det(\HolL) = 0$, no single-edge modification can restore consistency.
This corresponds to irreversible tissue damage where the loop structure
itself has collapsed --- there is no conductance value that makes the loop
close.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Intervention Hierarchy}

Therapeutic interventions are classified by topological target:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Edge modification:} Drugs altering a single $w_{ij}$
        (enzyme inhibitors, receptor agonists).  Correct local
        inconsistencies.  Minimal information requirement; suitable for
        drift and amplitude defects.
  \item \textbf{Node modification:} Gene therapy, enzyme replacement.
        Correct the constitutive relation at a node.  Required for
        phase defects where a single enzymatic timer is disrupted.
  \item \textbf{Topology modification:} Surgery, ablation, stem cell
        therapy.  Add or remove edges/nodes directly.  Required for
        topological defects.
  \item \textbf{Hub stabilisation:} Metabolic supplementation (ATP support,
        NAD$^+$ precursors, CoQ10).  Prevent inconsistency propagation
        without correcting the source.  Buys time; does not cure.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Validation}
\label{sec:ch08-validation}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

We validate the framework against three classes of biological data at
increasing levels of complexity: glycolytic flux data in human erythrocytes,
mitochondrial dysfunction cascades, and protein misfolding disease progression.

\subsection{Glycolytic Flux Consistency}

The glycolytic circuit is a 10-node feed-forward path with feedback through
the ATP/ADP hub.  We test self-consistency using measured flux data in human
erythrocytes~\citep{Nelson2004}.

The ATP/ADP hub participates in three loops: the hexokinase (HK) consumption
loop, the phosphofructokinase (PFK) consumption loop, and the pyruvate kinase
(PK) production loop.

\textbf{Prediction.}  In a healthy erythrocyte, the loops close: ATP
production rate ($\mathrm{PK} + \mathrm{PGK}$) exactly matches ATP
consumption rate ($\mathrm{HK} + \mathrm{PFK}$), giving
$\Hol_{\mathrm{ATP}} = \mathrm{Id}$.

\textbf{Observed.}  ATP production $= 2 \times J_{\mathrm{glycolysis}}$,
consumption $= 2 \times J_{\mathrm{glycolysis}}$ (stoichiometric balance).
Measured glycolytic flux
$J_{\mathrm{glycolysis}} = 1.1 \pm 0.1\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}\,h^{-1}}$.
The ATP loop closes with $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{ATP}} > 0.99$.

\textbf{Prediction.}  PK deficiency creates a non-trivial holonomy on the
ATP production loop.  The deficit
$\delta = J_{\mathrm{consumed}} - J_{\mathrm{produced}} > 0$
accumulates per cycle.

\textbf{Observed.}  PK-deficient erythrocytes show ATP depletion, upstream
intermediate accumulation (2,3-BPG, PEP), and premature haemolysis ---
consistent with monotonic consistency decline (\Cref{thm:monotonic-decline}).
The trajectory completion algorithm, given only three observations (glucose,
ATP, pyruvate), correctly identifies the PK step as the bottleneck without
prior knowledge of which enzyme is affected.

\begin{example}[Glycolytic Circuit with Specific Parameters]
\label{ex:ch08-glycolysis}
Consider the simplified 4-loop glycolytic circuit with measured
\textit{E.\ coli} parameters: $k_{\mathrm{HK}}^{+} = 3.5 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$,
$K_m^{\mathrm{HK}} = 0.1\,\mathrm{mM}$ (glucose); ATP steady-state
concentration $[\mathrm{ATP}] = 3.2\,\mathrm{mM}$;
$k_{\mathrm{PK}}^{+} = 8.4 \times 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$,
$K_m^{\mathrm{PK}} = 0.4\,\mathrm{mM}$ (PEP).  The ATP loop holonomy:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{hol}_{\mathrm{ATP}}
  = \ln\frac{k_{\mathrm{HK}}^{+}\,c_{\mathrm{ATP}}}{k_{\mathrm{HK}}^{-}\,c_{\mathrm{ADP}}}
  + \ln\frac{k_{\mathrm{PK}}^{-}\,c_{\mathrm{ADP}}}{k_{\mathrm{PK}}^{+}\,c_{\mathrm{PEP}}}
  = 0
\end{equation}
under detailed balance.  A 50\% PK activity reduction shifts the holonomy
to $\mathrm{hol}_{\mathrm{ATP}} \approx \ln 2 = 0.693$, putting
$\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{ATP}} = 1 - 0.693/\mathrm{hol}_{\max} < 0.5$ ---
above the clinical detection threshold --- within approximately 48 hours
of the defect onset at normal erythrocyte turnover rates.
\end{example}

\subsection{Mitochondrial Dysfunction Cascade}

Mitochondrial electron transport is a multi-loop circuit with $\Delta\psi_m$
(the mitochondrial membrane potential) as the central hub node.

\textbf{Prediction.}  Inhibition of any single complex creates hub
inconsistency propagating to all loops simultaneously
(\Cref{thm:hub-vulnerability}).

\textbf{Observed.}  Complex I inhibition (rotenone, $90\%$ reduction)
reduces $\Delta\psi_m$ by $\approx 30\,\mathrm{mV}$, impairs ATP synthesis
through all complexes, increases reactive oxygen species (ROS) production,
and triggers apoptosis.  Multi-system failure from a single-complex defect
is exactly what hub vulnerability predicts.

\textbf{Prediction.}  Failure order follows the defect hierarchy: ATP
depletion (amplitude defect) $\to$ $\Delta\psi_m$ instability (phase
defect) $\to$ cristae remodelling (topological defect).

\textbf{Observed.}  In mitochondrial disease progression: early ATP
decline $\to$ $\Delta\psi_m$ instability $\to$ cristae disruption $\to$
organelle fragmentation.  Matches the severity hierarchy exactly.

\begin{example}[Complex I Inhibition: Parameter Values]
\label{ex:complex-i}
Complex I in human cells has $N = 45$ subunits and operates with
$\Delta G^{\circ} = -69.5\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}$ per NADH oxidised.
Node voltage: $\varpotential_{\mathrm{NADH}} = \kB T\ln([\mathrm{NADH}]/[\mathrm{NAD}^{+}])
= 0.025\,\mathrm{V} \cdot \ln(0.3/0.7) \approx -21\,\mathrm{mV}$.
At 90\% Complex I inhibition, conductance $w_{\mathrm{CI}} \to 0.1\,w_{\mathrm{CI}}^{0}$.
The NADH/NAD$^+$ potential rises, driving the hub voltage away from its
equilibrium value.  Hub holonomy:
$\delta_{\mathrm{hub}} = |\Delta\varpotential_{\mathrm{hub}}|/(RT)
\approx 0.9 \cdot \Delta\varpotential_{\mathrm{CI}}/(25\,\mathrm{mV})$.
With $\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{CI}} \approx 80\,\mathrm{mV}$ at full
activity, $\delta_{\mathrm{hub}} \approx 2.88$, well above the detection
threshold $\epsilon = 0.1$, consistent with the clinical observation that
a 90\% Complex~I reduction is acutely symptomatic.
\end{example}

\subsection{Protein Misfolding: SOD1-Linked ALS}

Protein quality control is a 4-node loop:
$\text{synthesis} \to \text{folding} \to \text{function} \to \text{degradation} \to \text{synthesis}$.
A folding defect introduces a per-cycle residual $\delta = p_{\mathrm{misfolded}}$
into this loop.

\textbf{Prediction.}  Detection at
$n^{*} = N_{\mathrm{threshold}} / (\delta \cdot J_{\mathrm{synthesis}})$
cycles, with $N_{\mathrm{threshold}}$ the aggregate misfolded protein
threshold for clinical symptoms.

\textbf{Observed (SOD1 variants):}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Wild-type: $\delta_{\mathrm{wt}} \approx 10^{-6}$.
  \item A4V mutant: $\delta_{\mathrm{A4V}} \approx 10^{-2}$.
  \item G93A mutant: $\delta_{\mathrm{G93A}} \approx 5 \times 10^{-3}$.
  \item D90A mutant: $\delta_{\mathrm{D90A}} \approx 10^{-3}$.
\end{itemize}
Ratio predicts
$n^{*}_{\mathrm{wt}}/n^{*}_{\mathrm{A4V}} \approx 10^{4}$.
With approximately 300 synthesis cycles per day, A4V onset at $\sim$40
years ($\sim 4.4 \times 10^{6}$ cycles); wild-type would require
$\sim 4.4 \times 10^{10}$ cycles ($\sim 400{,}000$ years).  Severity
ordering $\mathrm{A4V} > \mathrm{G93A} > \mathrm{D90A} >$ wild-type
is correctly predicted with zero free parameters, matching clinical
observations.

\subsection{Cancer as Autonomous Loop Formation}

\begin{definition}[Autonomous Loop]
\label{def:autonomous-loop}
An autonomous loop maintains self-consistency independently of the global
circuit:
\begin{equation}
  \Hol_{\mathrm{auto}} = \mathrm{Id}
  \quad \text{regardless of } \mathcal{C}(\Gcond).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\textbf{Prediction.}  Cancer is the formation of autonomous loops ---
subcircuits that are internally self-consistent while creating inconsistency
with the organismal circuit.

\textbf{Observed.}  Oncogenic mutations create self-sustaining loops:
constitutively active growth factor receptors (EGFR, HER2), autocrine
signalling (PDGF loops), the Warburg effect (aerobic glycolysis
loop)~\citep{hanahan2011}.  These are internally consistent (the cancer
cell ``works'') but globally inconsistent (the organism does not).
Metastasis corresponds to progressive topological independence from the
host circuit.

\begin{example}[Cancer Cell Warburg Loop]
\label{ex:cancer}
A cancer cell running the Warburg effect has the following circuit
signature.  The standard glycolysis-to-oxidative-phosphorylation (OXPHOS)
pathway forms a loop through the mitochondrial membrane.  In the Warburg
cell, this loop is broken: the pyruvate-to-lactate shunt (lactate
dehydrogenase, LDH, overexpressed) creates an autonomous sub-loop:
glucose $\to$ G6P $\to \cdots \to$ pyruvate $\to$ lactate $\to$ [secreted].
This sub-loop has $\Hol_{\mathrm{Warburg}} = \mathrm{Id}$ by itself
(flux is conserved within the loop), but it decouples from the
mitochondrial ATP production loop, creating holonomy in the organismal
energy balance loop.  The circuit signature: the Warburg loop passes all
local validation, but the global consistency index of the organismal
circuit (including ATP supply to non-cancer tissues) declines.  Treatment
target: LDH inhibition (restoring $w_{\mathrm{LDH}} \to 0$) to force
pyruvate back into the OXPHOS loop and restore global consistency.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Type 2 Diabetes: Insulin Signalling Loop]
\label{ex:ch08-t2d}
Type 2 diabetes presents as an amplitude defect in the insulin signalling
circuit.  The insulin receptor (IR) $\to$ IRS-1 $\to$ PI3K $\to$ Akt
$\to$ GLUT4 translocation loop has a per-cycle conductance reduction
$\delta_{\mathrm{IR}} \approx 10^{-3}$--$10^{-2}$ per year (insulin
resistance progression rate).  With a dietary glucose challenge frequency
of approximately $3\,\mathrm{d}^{-1}$ and 365 days/year, the number of
loop traversals per year is $N_{\mathrm{year}} \approx 1095$.  Accumulated
holonomy after 10 years of subclinical insulin resistance:
$\mathrm{hol}_{\mathrm{IR}}^{(10)} \approx 10 \cdot 1095 \cdot 10^{-3}
= 10.95$,
exceeding the detection threshold $\epsilon \approx 1$ (clinical diabetes
definition: fasting glucose $> 7\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}$, corresponding
to $\mathrm{hol} \approx \ln(7/5.5) \approx 0.24$) after roughly 220
traversals, or $\sim 73$ days of sustained mild insulin resistance ---
consistent with the observation that short-duration high-fat diet can
induce detectable insulin resistance within weeks.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Alzheimer's Disease: Amyloid Loop Holonomy]
\label{ex:alzheimer}
Alzheimer's disease maps to a topological defect in the amyloid
processing circuit.  The loop: APP synthesis $\to$ $\alpha$-secretase
cleavage $\to$ sAPP$\alpha$ (non-amyloidogenic) $\to$ degradation is the
healthy loop.  In AD, overactive $\beta$-secretase (BACE1) creates a
competing loop: APP $\to$ $\beta$-cleavage $\to$ A$\beta$ $\to$
aggregation $\to$ plaque.  The plaque is an autonomous loop sink:
A$\beta$ monomers are sequestered irreversibly, preventing loop closure.
This gives $\det(\Hol_{\mathrm{APP}}) \to 0$ (the holonomy becomes
singular), which by \Cref{cor:irreversible} means the loop cannot be
restored by any single-edge conductance modification.  The circuit
prediction is that BACE1 inhibition (reducing the $\beta$-cleavage
conductance) is necessary but insufficient: the accumulated plaque
(topological defect) must also be cleared.  This is precisely the
clinical experience: BACE1 inhibitors alone have failed in late-stage AD,
but combination approaches (BACE1 inhibition plus anti-amyloid antibodies)
are showing greater promise.
\end{example}

\section{Experimental Predictions}
\label{sec:fuzzy-predictions}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The theoretical framework of this chapter generates seven quantitative
experimental predictions, each in principle falsifiable by existing or
near-future technology.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{P\arabic*.}, itemsep=6pt, leftmargin=*]

  \item \textbf{Variance precedes mean.}
        In longitudinal single-cell RNA sequencing or metabolomics data,
        the variance of transcript or metabolite levels across a cell
        population should increase significantly before the mean biomarker
        level shifts outside the healthy reference range.  This follows
        directly from \Cref{prop:variance-precedes} and
        \Cref{eq:variance-holonomy}.  Quantitative prediction: the
        variance increase is detectable at holonomy
        $|\mathrm{hol}_\ell| > \kB T / \mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{background}}$,
        which for typical intracellular noise corresponds to a holonomy
        of $\approx 0.01$--$0.1$, well before the mean shifts.

  \item \textbf{Hub stabilisation delays cascade.}
        Pharmacological stabilisation of hub nodes --- for example,
        NAD$^+$ supplementation (stabilising the NADH/NAD$^+$ hub) or
        CoQ10 administration (stabilising the electron transport hub) ---
        should delay disease cascade onset by a factor proportional to
        the hub degree $d_h$, according to \Cref{prop:hub-holonomy}.
        Specifically, the delay should scale as $d_h^2 / (2|L|)$ relative
        to the unprotected circuit.

  \item \textbf{Loop-length latency.}
        The latency between initiation of a disease perturbation and its
        detection by loop holonomy measurement scales as $|\ell| \cdot \epsilon/\delta$,
        where $|\ell|$ is the loop length, $\epsilon$ is the detection
        threshold, and $\delta$ is the per-step defect (\Cref{thm:drift-invisibility}).
        Longer metabolic loops (\eg, the urea cycle, $|\ell| = 6$) should
        show longer detection latencies than shorter loops (\eg,
        substrate-product pairs, $|\ell| = 2$).

  \item \textbf{Cascade propagation order.}
        When disease initiates in one loop, it propagates to neighbouring
        loops in order of \emph{shared edge count} --- loops sharing more
        edges with the initially affected loop are disrupted earlier.
        This predicts a specific topological order of biomarker
        perturbation that is testable with time-resolved multi-omics.

  \item \textbf{Self-consistency diagnosis outperforms template comparison.}
        A diagnostic test based on checking $\HolL \approx \mathrm{Id}$
        for all loops (self-consistency criterion, \Cref{cor:self-consistency})
        should achieve AUC $\approx 1.0$ on diseased vs.\ healthy cell
        discrimination, compared to AUC $\approx 0.896$ for the best
        single-biomarker reference-range test.

  \item \textbf{Latency scaling with misfolding rate.}
        For protein misfolding diseases, onset latency should scale
        inversely with misfolding rate $\delta$:
        $t_{\mathrm{onset}} \propto 1/\delta$.  This prediction is
        quantitatively testable using cell lines expressing SOD1 variants
        with known misfolding rates and monitoring aggregate formation
        kinetics in live-cell fluorescence assays.

  \item \textbf{Drug target ranking by holonomy sensitivity.}
        The drug targets predicted by the sparse LP (\Cref{prop:drug-lp})
        should correspond to the enzymes with the highest
        $\|\partial\,\mathrm{KCL}/\partial\,w_{ij}\|$ --- the edges whose
        conductance most efficiently reduces global KCL residual.  This
        ranking should outperform standard ``bottleneck enzyme'' heuristics
        based on flux control coefficients alone.

\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[Testability]
Predictions P1--P7 are quantitative and use measurements already accessible
to current laboratory practice: single-cell transcriptomics (P1, P4),
metabolomics with isotope tracing (P3), animal models with genetic hub
knockdowns (P2), digital pathology image analysis for spatial biomarker
variance (P5), live-cell fluorescence for aggregate kinetics (P6), and
metabolic flux analysis for drug target ranking (P7).  No new experimental
modality is required to test any of these predictions.
\end{remark}
