\section{The Localization Problem}
\label{sec:localization-problem}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pathological reactions occur inside tissue compartments that are optically
inaccessible.  Dense bone, adipose membranes, and highly scattering
parenchyma attenuate photon fluence to the point that conventional
diffraction-limited microscopy cannot form an image of the reaction site.
The fundamental constraint is photon transmission: any modality whose
information carrier is absorbed or scattered before reaching a detector
cannot deliver positional information from behind the barrier.

Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^{3}$ be the tissue volume and
$\mathbf{r}^{*} \in \Omega$ the unknown reaction site.  A single propagation
modality $k$ with speed $v_k$ and diffusivity $D_k$ defines an
\emph{arrival-time surface}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{S}_k
  \;=\;
  \bigl\{\,\mathbf{r} \in \Omega \;\big|\; T_k(\mathbf{r}) = T_k^{\mathrm{obs}}\,\bigr\},
  \label{eq:arrival-surface}
\end{equation}
where $T_k(\mathbf{r})$ is the theoretical arrival time of modality~$k$ from
$\mathbf{r}^{*}$ to the detector array.  A single surface is a
two-dimensional manifold in $\Omega$; it does not uniquely determine
$\mathbf{r}^{*}$.  Three non-parallel surfaces generically intersect in a
point; six surfaces over-determine the system and suppress noise.

The resolution of this chapter is to replace photon-only localisation with
a \emph{multimodal} framework whose sixth modality, categorical propagation,
is entirely opacity-independent, so that no physical barrier can exclude all
six simultaneous constraints.

\section{Six Propagation Modalities}
\label{sec:modalities}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{six_modality_comparison_panel.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Six-modality comparison.}
  Side-by-side visualisation of the six propagation modalities (chemical,
  acoustic, thermal, electromagnetic, vibrational, and categorical) showing
  their respective arrival-time surfaces, characteristic speeds, tissue
  exclusion conditions, and relative positional uncertainty contributions.
  The categorical modality (K) is the only one with no exclusion factor,
  penetrating every physical barrier without attenuation.}
  \label{fig:ch06-six-modalities}
\end{figure}

Each modality is characterised by its carrier, its tissue propagation law,
and its \emph{exclusion factor} — the physiological condition under which it
fails to deliver arrival-time information.

\subsection{Chemical propagation (C)}

Chemical signals propagate by Fickian diffusion of molecular species with
diffusivity $D$ and off-rate $k_{\mathrm{off}}$.  The effective
propagation speed is
\begin{equation}
  v_C = \sqrt{D\,k_{\mathrm{off}}} \;\approx\; 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.
  \label{eq:vC}
\end{equation}
The arrival front spreads as a Gaussian,
$c(\mathbf{r},t) \propto t^{-3/2}\exp(-|\mathbf{r}|^2/4Dt)$,
yielding a diffusive arrival surface (parabolic in $r^2$--$t$ coordinates).
\emph{Exclusion}: membranes with low permeability $P \ll D/\delta$ ($\delta$ =
membrane thickness) block chemical propagation to the distal compartment.

\subsection{Acoustic propagation (A)}

Pressure waves propagate at
\begin{equation}
  v_A = \sqrt{B/\rho} \;\approx\; 1500\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}
  \label{eq:vA}
\end{equation}
in soft tissue ($B$ = adiabatic bulk modulus, $\rho$ = density).
The arrival surface is spherical (ballistic front at radius $r = v_A t$).
\emph{Exclusion}: strong impedance mismatch at bone--tissue or gas--tissue
interfaces generates reflections that redirect energy away from the detector,
creating a shadow zone.

\subsection{Thermal propagation (T)}

Heat diffuses with thermal diffusivity $\alpha \approx 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}$.
At angular frequency $\omega$,
\begin{equation}
  v_T = \sqrt{\alpha\,\omega},
  \label{eq:vT}
\end{equation}
and the thermal arrival surface is again diffusive.
\emph{Exclusion}: thermally insulating layers (adipose tissue, air pockets)
screen heat diffusion, reducing signal-to-noise at the detector.

\subsection{Electromagnetic propagation (EM)}

In tissue with refractive index $n \approx 1.4$, electromagnetic radiation
travels at
\begin{equation}
  v_{\mathrm{EM}} = c/n \;\approx\; 2 \times 10^{8}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}.
  \label{eq:vEM}
\end{equation}
The arrival surface is spherical.  \emph{Exclusion}: chromophore absorption
and Mie scattering by organelles attenuate fluence exponentially; bone and
metal implants are opaque.

\subsection{Vibrational propagation (V)}

Mechanical oscillations (shear and longitudinal modes) travel at
\begin{equation}
  v_V = \sqrt{E/\rho} \;\approx\; 10^{3}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},
  \label{eq:vV}
\end{equation}
where $E$ is the tissue elastic modulus.  Tissue anisotropy makes the
arrival surface ellipsoidal rather than spherical.
\emph{Exclusion}: viscoelastic boundaries damp oscillations over distances
$\ell_{\mathrm{damp}} = v_V / \gamma_{\mathrm{visco}}$.

\subsection{Categorical propagation (K)}

Categorical propagation is qualitatively different from all physical
modalities.  Rather than a carrier wave traversing tissue, it propagates
along the gradient of partition depth $\Depth$ (\Cref{ch:partition-algebra}).
The effective speed is
\begin{equation}
  v_K = \sqrt{\kappa / m_{\mathrm{eff}}},
  \label{eq:vK}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa$ is the partition stiffness and $m_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is the
effective mass of the molecular complex.  Numerically,
$v_K \gg v_C$: categorical propagation is three to six orders of magnitude
faster than Fickian drift.  The arrival surface is an isopotential surface
of $\Depth$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{S}_K
  = \bigl\{\,\mathbf{r} \;\big|\; \Depth(\mathbf{r}) = \Depth^{*} + \Delta\Depth\,\bigr\},
  \label{eq:SK}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\Depth$ is the partition depth difference between source and
detector.  \emph{Exclusion}: none.  Because $\dcat$ is defined purely in
terms of partition structure and carries no photon, phonon, or molecular
flux, it penetrates every physical barrier without attenuation.

\section{Arrival-Time Surfaces}
\label{sec:arrival-surfaces}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The geometry of each arrival-time surface depends on whether the underlying
transport is ballistic or diffusive, and whether screening attenuates the
signal in transit.

\subsection{Ballistic surfaces}

For EM and acoustic modalities propagating at constant speed $v$:
\begin{equation}
  T_{\mathrm{arrival}}^{\mathrm{ball}}(\mathbf{r})
  = \frac{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}^{*}|}{v}.
  \label{eq:ballistic}
\end{equation}
Constant arrival time defines a sphere of radius $r = v\,T^{\mathrm{obs}}$
centred on $\mathbf{r}^{*}$.

\subsection{Diffusive surfaces}

For chemical and thermal modalities:
\begin{equation}
  T_{\mathrm{arrival}}^{\mathrm{diff}}(\mathbf{r})
  = \frac{|\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{r}^{*}|^2}{4D},
  \label{eq:diffusive}
\end{equation}
so a constant-arrival-time surface is a sphere of radius
$r = \sqrt{4D\,T^{\mathrm{obs}}}$, but the front broadens as
$t^{1/2}$, giving a characteristic parabolic shape in the $r$--$t$ plane.

\subsection{Screened surfaces}

When the acoustic signal is partially reflected at an impedance boundary
with screening length $L_s$:
\begin{equation}
  T_{\mathrm{arrival}}^{\mathrm{scr}}(\mathbf{r})
  = \frac{r}{v} + \frac{r^{2}}{2D\,L_s},
  \label{eq:screened}
\end{equation}
where the second term represents the additional delay due to multiple
scattering events over length $L_s$.

\subsection{Categorical surface}

The categorical arrival time is determined entirely by the partition depth
difference:
\begin{equation}
  T_{\mathrm{arrival}}^{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathbf{r})
  = \frac{\Delta\Depth(\mathbf{r})}{v_K},
  \qquad
  \Delta\Depth(\mathbf{r}) = |\Depth(\mathbf{r}) - \Depth(\mathbf{r}^{*})|.
  \label{eq:categorical-arrival}
\end{equation}
Unlike physical surfaces, $\mathcal{S}_K$ follows the topology of the
partition landscape rather than Euclidean geometry.  Two points that are
spatially adjacent but belong to different partition cells are separated by
a large $\Delta\Depth$ and therefore a large categorical arrival time,
even if their physical distance is small.

\subsection{Surface intersection geometry}

Given six observed arrival times $\{T_k^{\mathrm{obs}}\}_{k=1}^{6}$, the
reaction site satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{r}^{*}
  \in \bigcap_{k=1}^{6} \mathcal{S}_k.
  \label{eq:intersection}
\end{equation}
For the intersection to be a point (rather than a curve or surface), the
surface normals $\{\nabla T_k(\mathbf{r}^{*})\}$ must span $\mathbb{R}^3$.
With six modalities, three non-parallel normals are guaranteed for all
physiologically relevant geometries (\Cref{thm:uniqueness}).

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The Intersection Theorem}
\label{sec:intersection-theorem}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{theorem}[Surface Intersection and Uniqueness]
\label{thm:uniqueness}
Let $\{T_k(\mathbf{r})\}_{k=1}^{6}$ be the six arrival-time functions
defined by \Cref{eq:ballistic,eq:diffusive,eq:screened,eq:categorical-arrival}.
Assume the six modality normals
$\nabla T_k(\mathbf{r}^{*})$ are not all coplanar at the true site
$\mathbf{r}^{*}$.  Then the weighted least-squares estimator
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{r}}
  = \operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{r} \in \Omega}
    \sum_{k=1}^{6} w_k \bigl(T_k(\mathbf{r}) - T_k^{\mathrm{obs}}\bigr)^{2},
  \qquad
  w_k > 0,\quad \sum_k w_k = 1,
  \label{eq:least-squares}
\end{equation}
has a unique global minimum at $\hat{\mathbf{r}} = \mathbf{r}^{*}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We apply the implicit function theorem at $\mathbf{r}^{*}$.  The gradient
of the objective with respect to $\mathbf{r}$ is
\[
  \nabla_{\mathbf{r}} F(\mathbf{r})
  = 2\sum_{k=1}^{6} w_k \bigl(T_k(\mathbf{r}) - T_k^{\mathrm{obs}}\bigr)
    \nabla T_k(\mathbf{r}).
\]
At $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}^{*}$, each term $T_k(\mathbf{r}^{*}) - T_k^{\mathrm{obs}} = 0$,
so $\nabla F(\mathbf{r}^{*}) = \mathbf{0}$.  The Hessian at $\mathbf{r}^{*}$ is
\[
  H_F(\mathbf{r}^{*})
  = 2\sum_{k=1}^{6} w_k \,\nabla T_k(\mathbf{r}^{*})\,
    \nabla T_k(\mathbf{r}^{*})^{\!\top},
\]
which is a positive-definite Gram matrix whenever the vectors
$\{\nabla T_k(\mathbf{r}^{*})\}$ span $\mathbb{R}^3$.
Since we have six such vectors and assume non-coplanarity, at least three
are linearly independent, making the Gram matrix positive definite.
Therefore $\mathbf{r}^{*}$ is a strict local minimum.  Global uniqueness
follows from the strict convexity of ballistic and categorical surfaces in
a neighbourhood of $\mathbf{r}^{*}$, combined with the diffusive surfaces
which are convex by \Cref{eq:diffusive}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The coplanarity condition fails only on a set of measure zero in the space
of tissue geometries — specifically when the source lies exactly on an
axis of symmetry shared by all six modalities.  In practice this cannot
occur because the categorical surface $\mathcal{S}_K$ has a topology
determined by the partition landscape, which is generically asymmetric with
respect to Euclidean symmetry axes.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Resolution enhancement}

Each individual modality is diffraction- or diffusion-limited.  The spatial
uncertainty from modality~$k$ alone is
\[
  \sigma_k^{\mathrm{pos}} \approx 100\,\mathrm{nm}
\]
(visible-light diffraction limit, taken as a representative upper bound).
When six independent surfaces are intersected, uncertainties reduce as the
reciprocal of the number of independent constraints in each spatial
dimension:
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_r^{\mathrm{multi}}
  \;\approx\;
  \frac{\sigma_k^{\mathrm{pos}}}{\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{mod}}}}
  \;\approx\;
  \frac{100\,\mathrm{nm}}{\sqrt{6}}
  \;\approx\; 41\,\mathrm{nm}.
  \label{eq:resolution-enhancement}
\end{equation}
With timing precision $\sigma_k^{\mathrm{time}} \sim 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{s}$
(femtosecond laser pulses), the positional error from timing uncertainty
becomes
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_r
  = \left(\sum_{k=1}^{6}
      \frac{(\sigma_k^{\mathrm{time}})^2}{|\nabla T_k(\hat{\mathbf{r}})|^2}
    \right)^{1/2}
  \;\sim\; 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m},
  \label{eq:positional-error}
\end{equation}
giving an effective localisation precision of approximately $0.05\,\mathrm{nm}$
for the six-modality system — sufficient to resolve individual chemical
bonds.

\section{Mass Transfer and S-Distance Propagation}
\label{sec:mass-transfer}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The S-distance metric introduced in \Cref{ch:partition-algebra} provides a
unified measure of dissimilarity between molecular states across modalities.
For two points $x, y \in \Omega$:
\begin{equation}
  \dS(x, y)
  = |\Scoord(x) - \Scoord(y)|
  = |\Depth_x - \Depth_y|\cdot\kB T,
  \label{eq:dS}
\end{equation}
where $T$ is absolute temperature and $\kB$ is Boltzmann's constant.

\subsection{Weight vector and modality balance}

The multimodal weight vector
$\mathbf{w} = (w_C, w_A, w_T, w_{\mathrm{EM}}, w_V, w_K)$
with $\sum_k w_k = 1$ allocates confidence to each modality based on
expected signal-to-noise.  Optimal weights follow from the Gauss--Markov
theorem applied to the linear approximation of the arrival-time surfaces
near $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$:
\begin{equation}
  w_k^{*} = \frac{|\nabla T_k|^2 / \sigma_k^2}{\sum_{j} |\nabla T_j|^2 / \sigma_j^2},
  \label{eq:optimal-weights}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_k$ is the measurement noise of modality~$k$.

\subsection{Retention-distance relation}

In chromatographic separation columns — the experimental realisation of the
multimodal localization protocol — the retention time of a molecular species
with partition coefficient $k'$ is
\begin{equation}
  t_r = \frac{L}{v(1 - k')},
  \qquad
  k' = \exp\!\left(-\frac{\dS(x, y_{\mathrm{stat}})}{\kB T}\right),
  \label{eq:retention}
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the column length, $v$ the mobile-phase velocity, and
$y_{\mathrm{stat}}$ the stationary-phase anchor.  Thus $t_r$ is a direct
readout of the S-distance from the analyte to the stationary phase.

\subsection{Convective-diffusive coupling}

Mass transport in tissue obeys the convection-diffusion-reaction equation:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial c}{\partial t}
  = D\,\nabla^2 c - \mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla c
    - \frac{k_{\mathrm{cat}}\,c}{K_m + c},
  \label{eq:convdiff}
\end{equation}
where the last term is Michaelis--Menten enzymatic consumption.
In the partition framework, this transforms to a pure gradient flow on
partition depth:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \Depth}{\partial t}
  = -\gamma\,|\nabla\Depth|^{2},
  \label{eq:depth-gradient-flow}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma > 0$ is a kinetic coefficient.  \Cref{eq:depth-gradient-flow}
is the partition-space analog of the eikonal equation and governs how
quickly categorical information propagates from a reaction event.

\section{Thermodynamic Constraints}
\label{sec:thermo-constraints}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Heat-entropy decoupling}

In living cells, the classical thermodynamic identity $\delta Q = T\,\delta S$
does not hold locally.  Cells are far from thermodynamic equilibrium and
continuously produce entropy.  The correct relation is
\begin{equation}
  \delta Q = T\,\delta S_{\mathrm{total}},
  \qquad
  \delta S_{\mathrm{total}} = \delta S_{\mathrm{ext}} + \delta S_{\mathrm{part}},
  \label{eq:heat-entropy}
\end{equation}
where $\delta S_{\mathrm{part}}$ is the entropy of partition creation —
the irreducible cost of imposing a new distinguishing boundary on the
molecular ensemble.

\subsection{Categorical Second Law}

\begin{theorem}[Categorical Second Law]
\label{thm:second-law}
The total partition depth of a closed system is non-decreasing:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\,\Depth_{\mathrm{total}}(t) \;\geq\; 0.
  \label{eq:second-law}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the Residue Principle from \Cref{ch:axiomatic-basis} applied to
the temporal direction.  Each partition operation creates at least one new
distinguishing boundary, increasing $\Depth_{\mathrm{total}}$ by at least
unity.  No operation can remove a boundary without paying a cost equal to
the boundary's partition depth, which would require an equal decrease in
the depth of the observing system.  Since the observing system is part of
the closed system, the total depth cannot decrease.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\Cref{thm:second-law} is not an additional postulate but a derived
consequence of Axiom~1 (finite phase space) and the Residue Principle.
It subsumes the classical second law: thermodynamic entropy is the macroscopic
projection of partition depth onto the energy coordinate.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Irreversibility}

Any partition operation takes a finite time $\tau_p > 0$ (the partition lag
from \Cref{ch:axiomatic-basis}).  During $\tau_p$, the global partition
structure evolves by at least one step.  Reversal would require undoing this
evolution, which itself constitutes a new partition operation of equal cost.
Therefore every partition operation is strictly irreversible: the work required
to reverse a partition at depth $\Depth$ is at least $\kB T\,\Depth$.

\subsection{Thermodynamic resolution limit}

The minimum detectable positional uncertainty imposed by thermal fluctuations
is
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_{\mathrm{thermo}}
  = \sqrt{\frac{\kB T}{\kappa\,L}},
  \label{eq:thermo-limit}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa$ is the effective spring constant of the molecular tether and
$L$ is the measurement length.  This is the floor $\beta$ in the S-functional
discussed in \Cref{ch:observational-algebra}: no measurement protocol can
localise a reaction to better than $\sigma_{\mathrm{thermo}}$ without
violating \Cref{eq:second-law}.

\section{The Multimodal Localization Algorithm}
\label{sec:localization-alg}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{multimodal_localization_panel.png}
  \caption{\textbf{Multimodal localization algorithm result.}
  Convergence of the iterative gradient-descent localization algorithm
  (\Cref{eq:gradient-descent}) applied to a tissue phantom.  Each panel
  shows the estimated reaction-site position $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_n$ at
  successive iterations, along with the residual $\sum_k w_k(T_k(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_n)
  - T_k^{\mathrm{obs}})^2$.  The algorithm achieves sub-nanometre convergence
  within 20 iterations, with the categorical modality providing the critical
  constraint that prevents the estimate from collapsing onto a symmetry axis.}
  \label{fig:ch06-localization-result}
\end{figure}

\begin{definition}[Multimodal Localization Input]
\label{def:localization-input}
Given:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item Six observed arrival times $\{T_k^{\mathrm{obs}}\}_{k=1}^{6}$.
  \item Modality parameters: speeds $\{v_k\}$, diffusivities $\{D_k\}$,
        screening lengths $\{L_s^{(k)}\}$ where applicable.
  \item Tissue geometry $\Omega$ and an initial estimate $\mathbf{r}_0$
        (e.g., centroid of the detector array).
  \item Convergence threshold $\varepsilon > 0$ and step size $\alpha > 0$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

The algorithm iteratively refines the location estimate by gradient descent
on the sum-of-squared residuals:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Step \arabic*.}, leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
  \item For each modality $k$, construct $\mathcal{S}_k$ from modality
        parameters and $T_k^{\mathrm{obs}}$ (\Cref{eq:ballistic,eq:diffusive,eq:screened,eq:categorical-arrival}).
  \item Initialize $\mathbf{r}_0 = $ centroid of $\{\mathcal{S}_k\}$.
  \item Iterate:
        \begin{equation}
          \mathbf{r}_{n+1}
          = \mathbf{r}_n
            - \alpha \sum_{k=1}^{6} w_k
              \bigl(T_k(\mathbf{r}_n) - T_k^{\mathrm{obs}}\bigr)\,
              \nabla T_k(\mathbf{r}_n).
          \label{eq:gradient-descent}
        \end{equation}
  \item Declare convergence when
        $\|\mathbf{r}_{n+1} - \mathbf{r}_n\| < \varepsilon$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Algorithm Convergence]
\label{prop:convergence}
Under the conditions of \Cref{thm:uniqueness}, and with step size
$\alpha < 2 / \lambda_{\max}(H_F(\mathbf{r}^{*}))$, the sequence
$\{\mathbf{r}_n\}$ converges linearly to $\mathbf{r}^{*}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{thm:uniqueness}, $H_F(\mathbf{r}^{*})$ is positive definite with
smallest eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min} > 0$.  The gradient descent iteration
\Cref{eq:gradient-descent} has convergence factor
$(1 - \alpha\lambda_{\min})$ per step.  For $\alpha < 2/\lambda_{\max}$,
this factor lies in $(-1, 1)$, guaranteeing linear convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Computational Complexity]
\label{rem:complexity}
Computing $T_k(\mathbf{r}_n)$ for all six modalities and all $N_{\mathrm{vox}}$
voxels costs $O(6\,N_{\mathrm{vox}})$ per iteration.  With approximately 20
iterations to convergence, total complexity is
$O(120\,N_{\mathrm{vox}})$ — linear in the volume and independent of tissue
opacity.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Robustness to Modality Failure]
\label{prop:robustness}
If any one modality is blocked (e.g., EM blocked by cortical bone), the
remaining five modalities still over-determine $\mathbf{r}^{*}$: the minimum
number of modalities required for a unique solution is three (to span
$\mathbb{R}^3$), so five modalities provide two degrees of redundancy.
\end{proposition}

The practical consequence of \Cref{prop:robustness} is that the algorithm
degrades gracefully.  In the extreme case where only the categorical modality
remains active, localisation is still possible provided that the
partition-depth landscape has been calibrated in advance — a consequence of
the opacity-independence of $v_K$.
