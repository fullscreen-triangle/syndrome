The edifice of the present monograph rests on a single pair of axioms
whose joint force is this: every physical observer is a \emph{finite}
entity measuring a \emph{finite} world.  From these two constraints alone
one derives a complete calculus of epistemic distance — the S-entropy
calculus — that governs everything from thermodynamic phase boundaries
to disease classification to drug action.  The derivation proceeds in
logical order: axioms first, partition geometry second, the S-functional
and its four characterising axioms third, the Triple Equivalence (the
structural cornerstone) fourth, and then the sequence of major theorems
that follow.  No physical input beyond the two axioms is needed at any
stage.

\section{The Two Axioms}
\label{sec:axioms}

The entire framework rests on two axioms whose joint content is that every
physical observer is a \emph{finite} entity interacting with a \emph{finite}
world.  No other metaphysical machinery is required.  The maximum-entropy
foundation for inference from incomplete data is due to Jaynes~\citep{Jaynes1957}.

\begin{axiom}[Bounded Phase Space]
\label{ax:bounded-phase}
Every physical system with finite energy $E$ and spatial extent $R$ possesses a
phase space $\Omega$ of finite Liouville measure:
\[
  \mu(\Omega) < \infty .
\]
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[Categorical Observation]
\label{ax:categorical-obs}
Any observer $\mathcal{R}$ with finite resolution $\varepsilon > 0$ partitions
$\Omega$ into a finite collection of cells:
\[
  \Pi(\Omega) = \bigl\{ C_1, C_2, \ldots, C_N \bigr\}, \qquad
  N = |\Pi(\Omega)| < \infty .
\]
The cells are pairwise disjoint and their union covers $\Omega$:
$C_i \cap C_j = \emptyset$ for $i \neq j$ and $\bigcup_i C_i = \Omega$.
\end{axiom}

\begin{remark}
\Cref{ax:bounded-phase} is the standard assumption of classical and quantum
statistical mechanics: the partition function converges~\citep{Callen1985}.
\Cref{ax:categorical-obs} is the operational statement that any measurement
apparatus has a minimal distinguishable volume $\delta^d > 0$, where $d$ is
the dimension of the phase space.  The two axioms together imply all results
of this monograph without further assumptions.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Forced Partitioning]
\label{thm:forced-partitioning}
Let $\mathcal{R}$ be an observer with minimal distinguishable phase-space volume
$\delta^d > 0$.  Then the number of cells in any partition compatible with
$\mathcal{R}$ satisfies
\[
  N \;\leq\; \left\lfloor \frac{\mu(\Omega)}{\delta^d} \right\rfloor
  \;<\; \infty .
\]
In particular, the observer cannot resolve individual points of $\Omega$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{ax:bounded-phase}, $\mu(\Omega) < \infty$.  By
\Cref{ax:categorical-obs}, each cell $C_i$ satisfies $\mu(C_i) \geq \delta^d$
because $\mathcal{R}$ cannot distinguish states separated by less than $\delta^d$
in Liouville measure.  Disjointness of the cells gives
\[
  \mu(\Omega) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mu(C_i) \;\geq\; N \cdot \delta^d ,
\]
which rearranges to $N \leq \mu(\Omega)/\delta^d$.  Since the right-hand side
is finite, $N < \infty$.  A single point has Liouville measure zero, so no
collection of finitely many cells of positive measure can resolve individual
points.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Point-Resolution Impossibility]
\label{cor:point-resolution}
Under \Cref{ax:bounded-phase,ax:categorical-obs}, no bounded observer can
determine the exact state $x \in \Omega$; it can only determine the cell
$C_i \ni x$.
\end{corollary}

These results immediately imply that all information about a system available
to any bounded observer is \emph{cell-level} information.  The consequences for
biology, medicine, and disease are explored throughout the monograph.

\section{Partition Geometry}
\label{sec:partition-geometry}

The structure of an observer's partition is not arbitrary.  Physical symmetry
and hierarchical organisation constrain the allowable partition coordinates.
The combinatorial content of these constraints turns out to reproduce, from
purely observational axioms, the shell-filling structure of atomic physics —
a striking connection between the epistemological framework and quantum
mechanics that we now make precise.

The key insight is that the labels $(n, \ell, m, s)$ are not borrowed from
quantum mechanics but are forced by the logical structure of any hierarchical
classification system: one needs a depth counter ($n$), a complexity index
($\ell$) at each depth, an orientation selector ($m$) within each complexity
class, and a binary chirality bit ($s$) to break the final degeneracy.  The
same four-parameter family arises wherever a finite observer classifies states
by nested equivalences.

\begin{definition}[Partition Coordinates]
\label{def:partition-coords}
A partition cell is labelled by the quadruple $(n, \ell, m, s)$ where:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item $n \geq 1$ is the \emph{nesting depth} — the number of hierarchical
    levels in the partition;
  \item $\ell \in \{0, 1, \ldots, n-1\}$ is the \emph{partition complexity}
    index at the current level;
  \item $m \in \{-\ell, -\ell+1, \ldots, \ell\}$ is the \emph{orientation}
    index of the cell within level $\ell$;
  \item $s \in \{-\tfrac{1}{2}, +\tfrac{1}{2}\}$ is the \emph{chirality}
    selector distinguishing two otherwise equivalent sub-cells.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Capacity Formula]
\label{prop:capacity}
The total number of cells at nesting depth $n$ is
\[
  \Capacity(n) \;=\; 2n^2 .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $n$, the index $\ell$ ranges over $\{0, \ldots, n-1\}$.  For each
$\ell$, the orientation index $m$ takes $2\ell + 1$ values.  The chirality
selector $s$ doubles each count.  Summing over all levels:
\[
  \Capacity(n) \;=\; 2 \sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1} (2\ell + 1)
  \;=\; 2 \cdot n^2 ,
\]
where the identity $\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(2\ell+1) = n^2$ is the standard sum
of the first $n$ odd integers.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The formula $\Capacity(n) = 2n^2$ reproduces the atomic shell capacity of
quantum mechanics (2, 8, 18, 32, \ldots for $n = 1, 2, 3, 4, \ldots$).  This
is not a coincidence: both arise from the same angular-momentum bookkeeping
applied to a hierarchy of distinguishable states.  The partition framework thus
provides a combinatorial derivation of the Pauli capacity of quantum shells
from purely observational axioms.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Partition Depth]
\label{def:partition-depth}
The \emph{partition depth} $\Depth$ of a partition $\Pi$ is the amount of
hierarchical structure needed to uniquely specify a cell within $\Pi$:
\[
  \Depth(\Pi) \;=\; \sum_{i} \log_b k_i ,
\]
where $k_i$ is the branching factor at level $i$ and $b \geq 2$ is the base
of the logarithm.  $\Depth$ is measured in \emph{partition bits} (base-2)
or \emph{partition nats} (base-$e$).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Depth--Entropy Equivalence]
\label{thm:depth-entropy}
The partition depth $\Depth$ and the Boltzmann entropy $S_P$~\citep{Shannon1948,Jaynes1957} of the
associated microstate distribution are related by
\[
  S_P \;=\; \kB \cdot \Depth \cdot \ln b ,
\]
where $\kB$ is Boltzmann's constant and $b$ is the logarithm base.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The partition places uniform probability $p = 1/b^{\Depth(\Pi)}$ on each
of the $b^{\Depth}$ equiprobable cells (uniform prior by the maximum-entropy
principle under the cell constraint~\citep{Jaynes1957}).  The Boltzmann entropy is
\[
  S_P \;=\; -\kB \sum_i p \ln p
  \;=\; -\kB \cdot b^{\Depth} \cdot \frac{1}{b^{\Depth}} \cdot
  \ln \frac{1}{b^{\Depth}}
  \;=\; \kB \cdot \Depth \cdot \ln b .  \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Partition Time]
\label{def:partition-time}
The \emph{partition time} $\tau_p$ is the minimal time required to carry out
one act of partitioning, set by the time--energy uncertainty relation:
\[
  \tau_p \;=\; \frac{\hbar}{\kB T} \;>\; 0 .
\]
The floor on irreversible information erasure implicit in this timescale is
the Landauer principle~\citep{Landauer1961}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Residue Principle]
\label{thm:residue}
Because partitioning takes finite time $\tau_p > 0$ and the system continues
to evolve during $\tau_p$, every partition leaves a \emph{residue} — a
non-zero entropy contribution arising from the evolution that occurred while
the partition was being made.  Formally, for any partition $\Pi$ of $\Omega$
performed over interval $[t_0, t_0 + \tau_p]$,
\[
  \Delta S_{\mathrm{res}}(\Pi) \;=\; S_P(\Pi(t_0 + \tau_p)) - S_P(\Pi(t_0))
  \;>\; 0 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Phi_t : \Omega \to \Omega$ be the Hamiltonian flow.  During $[t_0,
t_0 + \tau_p]$, the system moves from $x(t_0)$ to $x(t_0 + \tau_p)$.  The
partition $\Pi$ is constructed with respect to the state at $t_0$, but the
system is measured at $t_0 + \tau_p$.  The mismatch between the partition
and the evolved state contributes
\[
  \Delta S_{\mathrm{res}} \;\geq\; \kB \ln \frac{\mu(\Phi_{\tau_p}(C_i))}{\mu(C_i)}
\]
for the cell $C_i$ containing $x(t_0)$.  By Liouville's theorem, $\Phi_{\tau_p}$
preserves measure, but the image $\Phi_{\tau_p}(C_i)$ generally overlaps
multiple cells of $\Pi$, increasing the conditional entropy.  Since $\tau_p > 0$
and the flow is non-trivial, this overlap is positive, giving
$\Delta S_{\mathrm{res}} > 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Partition Gradient Flow]
\label{prop:gradient-flow}
In the space of partition configurations, system trajectories satisfy the
gradient flow equation
\[
  \frac{dx}{dt} \;=\; -\gamma \,\nabla \Depth(x) ,
\]
where $\gamma > 0$ is a dissipation coefficient.  Systems therefore evolve
toward configurations of lower partition depth — toward greater simplicity
and coarser self-description.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The partition depth $\Depth(x)$ plays the role of a Lyapunov functional.
By \Cref{thm:depth-entropy}, $\Depth$ is monotonically related to entropy
production.  Standard arguments from irreversible thermodynamics (the
minimum entropy-production principle for near-equilibrium systems) give the
gradient flow as the steepest-descent dynamics in partition-depth space.
The dissipation coefficient $\gamma = \tau_p^{-1} \cdot \kB T / \mu$ follows
from the fluctuation-dissipation theorem applied to partition fluctuations.
\end{proof}

\section{The S-Scale and Receiver Structure}
\label{sec:s-scalar}

Before defining the S-functional we must establish the scale on which
S-values live and the precise mathematical structure of a receiver.  These
definitions are more subtle than they first appear: the choice of the closed
interval $[0, 100]$ rather than $[0, 1]$ or $[0, \infty)$ is deliberate,
and the four-component receiver definition captures all operationally
relevant structure without redundancy.

The S-scale $[0, 100]$ should be read as a normalised semantic distance: the
value $0$ represents exact alignment of a candidate with the truth — a state
that the Floor Theorem (\Cref{thm:floor-positivity}) will show is
unattainable by any bounded receiver — and the value $100$ represents maximal
misalignment, \ie, the candidate and the truth are as far apart as the
problem allows.  The choice of $100$ as the upper bound is a normalisation
convention; no result depends on it.

\begin{definition}[S-scale]
\label{def:s-scale}
The \emph{S-scale} is the closed real interval
$\mathbb{S} := [0, 100] \subset \mathbb{R}$.
We write $\mathbb{S}^{*} := (0, 100]$ for the open-below S-scale, and
$\partial \mathbb{S} := \{0, 100\}$ for the boundary points: $0$
(exact alignment) and $100$ (maximal misalignment).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Candidate Space and Truth Space]
\label{def:candidate-truth}
A \emph{candidate space} $\mathcal{X}$ is a non-empty set whose elements
represent admissible candidate solutions to a problem.  A
\emph{truth space} $\mathcal{X}^{*} \subseteq \mathcal{X}$ is a designated
non-empty subset whose elements are the correct solutions.  We assume
$|\mathcal{X}^{*}| \geq 1$; the case $|\mathcal{X}^{*}| > 1$ allows for
solutions that are operationally indistinguishable.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The distinction between candidate space and truth space is crucial.  In a
diagnostic setting, $\mathcal{X}$ is the space of all possible disease states
and $\mathcal{X}^{*}$ is the set of states consistent with the patient's
actual condition.  A receiver (the clinician, the diagnostic device) maps
signals to candidates; the S-functional measures how far the best achievable
candidate is from any element of $\mathcal{X}^{*}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Receiver — Quadruple Form]
\label{def:receiver-quad}
A \emph{receiver} $\mathcal{R}$ over $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{X}^{*})$ is a
quadruple
\[
  \mathcal{R} = (\Sigma_{\mathcal{R}},\; \Phi_{\mathcal{R}},\;
                 K_{\mathcal{R}},\; \mathrm{dec}_{\mathcal{R}})
\]
where:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item $\Sigma_{\mathcal{R}}$ is a non-empty set, the
    \emph{signal space} of $\mathcal{R}$;
  \item $\Phi_{\mathcal{R}}: \Sigma_{\mathcal{R}} \to K_{\mathcal{R}}$
    is a function called the \emph{internal decoder}, mapping each incoming
    signal to a knowledge state;
  \item $K_{\mathcal{R}}$ is a set called the \emph{knowledge framework},
    equipped with a fixed set of operations $\{\circ_j\}_{j \in J_{\mathcal{R}}}$;
  \item $\mathrm{dec}_{\mathcal{R}}: K_{\mathcal{R}} \to \mathcal{X}$ is the
    \emph{candidate-projection}, associating each knowledge state with a
    candidate.
\end{enumerate}
The receiver is \emph{bounded} if $|K_{\mathcal{R}}|$ is a cardinal strictly
less than that of $\mathcal{X}$ when $|\mathcal{X}|$ is infinite.
\end{definition}

The quadruple form makes the receiver's epistemic architecture explicit.
An alternative formulation, used when studying action-cells in multi-agent
settings, presents the same structure differently:

\begin{definition}[Receiver — Operational Form]
\label{def:receiver-operational}
A \emph{receiver} on outcome space $(\mathcal{X}, d, \Sigma)$ is a tuple
$\mathcal{R} = (K, D, \Pi, \beta)$ where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $K$ is a non-empty set, the \emph{knowledge framework};
  \item $D : \mathcal{X} \to K$ is the \emph{decoder};
  \item $\Pi : K \to 2^{\mathcal{X}} \setminus \{\varnothing\}$ is the
    \emph{candidate-projection}, satisfying $D(x) \in D(\Pi(D(x)))$ for
    all $x \in \mathcal{X}$;
  \item $\beta \in (0, \Sigma]$ is a strictly positive \emph{noise floor},
    $\beta = \sup_{x \in \mathcal{X}} \inf_{x' \in \Pi(D(x))} d(x, x') > 0$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The two receiver definitions are equivalent.  The quadruple form emphasises
the signal-processing pipeline: signals arrive, are decoded internally, then
projected to candidates.  The operational form emphasises the
distance-to-cell structure.  The noise floor $\beta > 0$ in the operational
form corresponds to the floor function $S_\flat(\mathcal{R}) > 0$ that will
be characterised axiomatically in \Cref{sec:s-axioms} and proved strictly
positive in \Cref{sec:bounded-cognition}.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Bounded Receiver]
\label{def:bounded-receiver}
A receiver $\mathcal{R}$ is \emph{bounded} if its knowledge framework $K$
has cardinality strictly less than that of the total information space
$\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ of the problem: $|K| < |\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}|$.
For a finite receiver, $|K| < \infty$.  For a denumerably bounded receiver,
$|K| \leq \aleph_0$.
\end{definition}

We write $\mathfrak{R}$ for the class of all receivers.  All receivers in
this monograph are bounded unless otherwise stated.

\section{Axioms of the S-Functional}
\label{sec:s-axioms}

The S-functional is constrained by four axioms.  The first two establish the
range; the third, the Self-Truth Lower Bound, is the heart of the theory —
it asserts that no receiver can achieve perfect alignment with the truth.
The fourth ensures that the S-value is computed through the receiver's own
decoding architecture, not by some external oracle.

Together the four axioms characterise S as a bounded, receiver-specific,
floor-separated pseudo-metric on candidate space.  Every construction in the
rest of the monograph uses at most these four properties.

\begin{definition}[S-Functional — Axiomatic Form]
\label{def:s-functional-axiom}
An \emph{S-functional} on $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{X}^{*})$ is a map
\[
  S : \mathfrak{R} \times \mathcal{X} \times \mathcal{X}^{*}
  \to \mathbb{S},
  \qquad
  (\mathcal{R}, x, x^{*}) \mapsto S_{\mathcal{R}}(x, x^{*}) ,
\]
satisfying Axioms~(S1)--(S4) below.
\end{definition}

\begin{axiom}[Non-negativity — S1]
\label{ax:S1}
For every $\mathcal{R} \in \mathfrak{R}$, $x \in \mathcal{X}$,
$x^{*} \in \mathcal{X}^{*}$:
\[
  S_{\mathcal{R}}(x, x^{*}) \;\geq\; 0 .
\]
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[Boundedness — S2]
\label{ax:S2}
For every $\mathcal{R} \in \mathfrak{R}$, $x \in \mathcal{X}$,
$x^{*} \in \mathcal{X}^{*}$:
\[
  S_{\mathcal{R}}(x, x^{*}) \;\leq\; 100 .
\]
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[Self-Truth Lower Bound — S3]
\label{ax:S3}
There exists a function $S_\flat : \mathfrak{R} \to \mathbb{S}^{*}$,
called the \emph{floor function}, with $S_\flat(\mathcal{R}) > 0$ for every
$\mathcal{R} \in \mathfrak{R}$, such that
\[
  \inf_{x \in \mathcal{X}} S_{\mathcal{R}}(x, x^{*})
  \;=\; S_\flat(\mathcal{R})
\]
for every $x^{*} \in \mathcal{X}^{*}$.
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[Receiver-Decoded Coherence — S4]
\label{ax:S4}
For every $\mathcal{R} \in \mathfrak{R}$, the partial map
$x \mapsto S_{\mathcal{R}}(x, x^{*})$ is
$\mathrm{dec}_{\mathcal{R}}$-coherent: there exists a non-decreasing
function
$\delta_{\mathcal{R}} : K_{\mathcal{R}} \times K_{\mathcal{R}} \to \mathbb{S}$
with $\delta_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma, \sigma) = S_\flat(\mathcal{R})$ for all
$\sigma$, such that
\[
  S_{\mathcal{R}}(x, x^{*})
  \;=\; \delta_{\mathcal{R}}\!\bigl(\Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma_x),\,
                                    \Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma_{x^{*}})\bigr)
\]
for some $\sigma_x \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{R}}$ with
$\mathrm{dec}_{\mathcal{R}}(\Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma_x)) = x$ and
similarly for $\sigma_{x^{*}}$.
\end{axiom}

\begin{remark}
Axiom~(S3) expresses the boundedness of cognition: no receiver can attain
perfect alignment with the truth.  The constant $S_\flat(\mathcal{R})$ is the
\emph{smallest knowable error} of $\mathcal{R}$ and depends only on the
receiver's internal structure, not on the particular candidate or truth being
compared.  This is proved in \Cref{sec:bounded-cognition}.

Axiom~(S4) is the coherence condition: the S-value must be computable
through the receiver's own decoder chain.  It rules out situations where an
oracle outside $\mathcal{R}$ could inform $\mathcal{R}$ of its true S-distance;
such an oracle would itself be a receiver with its own floor, and the
composition would not circumvent (S3).
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Image of $S_{\mathcal{R}}$]
\label{lem:image-S}
For each receiver $\mathcal{R}$, the image of $S_{\mathcal{R}}$ is contained in
$[S_\flat(\mathcal{R}),\, 100] \subseteq \mathbb{S}^{*}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Immediate from Axioms~(S1), (S2), and (S3).
\end{proof}

\section{Bounded Cognition and the Floor}
\label{sec:bounded-cognition}

The Floor Theorem is the central impossibility result of the entire
framework.  It asserts that no bounded receiver — however well designed,
however carefully calibrated — can achieve a zero S-value.  There is always
an irreducible residual error, a ``smallest knowable error'' that is a
structural property of the receiver and cannot be removed by any amount of
additional information processing.

This result is the formal counterpart of the Heisenberg uncertainty
principle, the Landauer erasure cost, and the G\"{o}delian incompleteness
theorem, but it is derived from purely observational axioms without invoking
quantum mechanics, thermodynamics, or formal logic.  The proof uses only the
cardinality mismatch between the receiver's knowledge framework and the total
information space.

\begin{definition}[Cognitive Capacity]
\label{def:cog-cap}
The \emph{cognitive capacity} of a receiver $\mathcal{R}$ is the cardinal
$|K_{\mathcal{R}}|$.  The receiver is \emph{finite} if $|K_{\mathcal{R}}|$
is finite, and \emph{denumerably bounded} if $|K_{\mathcal{R}}| \leq \aleph_0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Total Information Space]
\label{def:total-info}
The \emph{total information space} of a problem $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{X}^{*})$
is a set $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ with $\mathcal{X} \subseteq \mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}$,
equipped with a cardinality $|\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}|$ that may exceed
$|\mathcal{X}|$.  It represents all information logically required to
specify a candidate perfectly.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Cognitive Residue]
\label{def:residue}
The \emph{cognitive residue} of a receiver $\mathcal{R}$ relative to total
information space $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ is
\[
  \mathfrak{R}(\mathcal{R}) \;:=\;
  \mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}
  \setminus
  \mathrm{dec}_{\mathcal{R}}\!\bigl(\Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{R}})\bigr) .
\]
The cognitive residue is the set of informational states that the receiver
can never represent in its candidate-projection, regardless of what signal it
receives.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Residue Persistence]
\label{thm:cognitive-residue-persistence}
Let $\mathcal{R}$ be a receiver with cognitive capacity
$|K_{\mathcal{R}}| < |\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}|$.  Then
$|\mathfrak{R}(\mathcal{R})| = |\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}|$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $\mathrm{dec}_{\mathcal{R}} : K_{\mathcal{R}} \to \mathcal{X}$ is a
function,
$|\mathrm{dec}_{\mathcal{R}}(\Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{R}}))| \leq
|K_{\mathcal{R}}|$.
Thus
$|\mathfrak{R}(\mathcal{R})| = |\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}} \setminus
\mathrm{dec}_{\mathcal{R}}(\Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{R}}))|
\geq |\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}| - |K_{\mathcal{R}}|$.
By the cardinal arithmetic of subtraction in infinite cardinals, when
$|K_{\mathcal{R}}| < |\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}|$, this difference equals
$|\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}|$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Floor Positivity]
\label{thm:floor-positivity-cog}
For every bounded receiver $\mathcal{R}$, $S_\flat(\mathcal{R}) > 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose for contradiction that $S_\flat(\mathcal{R}) = 0$.  By Axiom~(S3)
there is a candidate $x_0 \in \mathcal{X}$ with
$S_{\mathcal{R}}(x_0, x^{*}) = 0$.  By Axiom~(S4) this requires
\[
  \delta_{\mathcal{R}}(\Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma_{x_0}),\,
  \Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma_{x^{*}})) \;=\; 0 .
\]
But $\delta_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma, \sigma') = 0$ implies $\sigma = \sigma'$:
since $\delta_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma, \sigma) = S_\flat(\mathcal{R}) = 0$, and
$\delta_{\mathcal{R}}$ is the floor on the diagonal, any value below the
diagonal floor is impossible.  Therefore
$\Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma_{x_0}) = \Phi_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma_{x^{*}})$ in
$K_{\mathcal{R}}$.  By \Cref{thm:cognitive-residue-persistence}, since
$|K_{\mathcal{R}}| < |\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}|$, the inverse image
$\mathrm{dec}_{\mathcal{R}}^{-1}(\{x^{*}\})$ has cardinality at most
$|K_{\mathcal{R}}|$, while $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}$ has greater
cardinality, so the residue has positive measure with respect to any
cardinality-preserving measure on $\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{tot}}$.  This
contradicts the requirement that the residue's information content vanish at
floor.  Hence $S_\flat(\mathcal{R}) > 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Smallest Knowable Error]
\label{cor:smallest-knowable}
For every bounded receiver $\mathcal{R}$ and every truth
$x^{*} \in \mathcal{X}^{*}$, there is no candidate $x \in \mathcal{X}$
with $S_{\mathcal{R}}(x, x^{*}) < S_\flat(\mathcal{R})$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
The floor $S_\flat(\mathcal{R})$ is a \emph{receiver-intrinsic} property: it
depends only on the structure of $\mathcal{R}$, not on the candidate or the
truth.  Two distinct truths $x_1^*, x_2^* \in \mathcal{X}^{*}$ can be
approached to the same floor but no closer.  The floor is not a measurement
error that can be corrected by better calibration; it is a logical consequence
of the cardinality mismatch between the receiver's knowledge and the world.
\end{remark}

\section{The S-Functional and S-Entropy Space}
\label{sec:s-functional}

The \emph{S-functional} formalises the epistemic distance between a receiver
and a target cell.  It is the central object of the entire theory.  The
S-entropy space decomposes this distance into three components corresponding
to kinematic, thermodynamic, and epistemic contributions — a decomposition
that will reappear, in specialised forms, in every application chapter.

\begin{definition}[S-Functional — Operational Form]
\label{def:s-functional}
For a receiver $\mathcal{R}$, a system state $x \in \Omega$, and a target cell
$C \subset \Omega$, the \emph{S-functional} is
\[
  S(\mathcal{R},\, x;\, C)
  \;=\;
  \inf_{x' \in \Pi(D(x))} \dcat(x', C) \;+\; \beta ,
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $D(x)$ is the resolution cell of $\mathcal{R}$ containing $x$;
  \item $\Pi(D(x))$ is the partition of $D(x)$ induced by $\mathcal{R}$;
  \item $\dcat$ is the categorical distance (\Cref{def:categorical-distance});
  \item $\beta > 0$ is the \emph{irreducible floor} proved positive in
    \Cref{thm:floor-positivity}.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Categorical Distance]
\label{def:categorical-distance}
The \emph{categorical distance} $\dcat : \Omega \times 2^\Omega \to [0,\infty)$
is defined by
\[
  \dcat(x', C) \;=\; 1 - \mathbf{1}_C(x') ,
\]
where $\mathbf{1}_C$ is the indicator function of $C$.  Equivalently,
$\dcat(x', C) = 0$ if $x' \in C$ and $\dcat(x', C) = 1$ if $x' \notin C$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The categorical distance is independent of spatial distance and optical
opacity: two states that are categorically proximate (same cell) remain so
regardless of whether they are spatially near or far, visible or occluded.
This is the content of the Opacity Independence corollary
(\Cref{cor:opacity-independence}) proved in \Cref{sec:triple-equivalence}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Basic S-Properties]
\label{prop:basic-S}
For receiver $\mathcal{R} = (K, D, \Pi, \beta)$ and cell $C$:
\begin{enumerate}[(1)]
  \item $S(\mathcal{R}, x; C) \geq \beta$ for all $x$;
  \item $S(\mathcal{R}, x; C) \leq \dcat(x, C) + \beta$;
  \item $C \subseteq C' \Rightarrow S(\mathcal{R}, x; C') \leq S(\mathcal{R}, x; C)$;
  \item If $D$ and $\Pi$ are Borel measurable, then $x \mapsto S(\mathcal{R}, x; C)$
    is upper semicontinuous.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Property (1) is the floor lower bound from \Cref{thm:floor-positivity}.
Property (2) follows since $x$ itself is a candidate in $\Pi(D(x))$.
Property (3) holds because enlarging the target cell reduces the infimum.
Property (4) follows from the upper semicontinuity of the infimum over a
family of measurable functions.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[S-Entropy Space]
\label{def:s-space}
The \emph{S-entropy space} is the triple
\[
  \Sspace \;=\; \bigl\{ (\Sk, \St, \Se) \bigr\} \;\subset\; [0,1]^3 ,
\]
with \emph{S-coordinate} $\Scoord = (\Sk, \St, \Se)$ and components:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item $\Sk$ — \emph{kinematic S-entropy}: uncertainty in the position and
    momentum of the system within its resolution cell;
  \item $\St$ — \emph{thermodynamic S-entropy}: free-energy cost of
    localising the system to the target cell, normalised by $\kB T$;
  \item $\Se$ — \emph{epistemic S-entropy}: information deficit between the
    receiver's representation and the target cell.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Entropy Identity]
\label{thm:entropy-identity}
The total S-entropy satisfies
\[
  S(\mathcal{R}, x; C)
  \;=\; \kB \cdot \Depth(\Pi) \cdot \ln n ,
\]
where $n$ is the nesting depth of the partition $\Pi$ induced by $\mathcal{R}$
on $D(x)$.  In S-coordinate notation, $\Scoord = (\Sk, \St, \Se)$ with
$\Sk + \St + \Se = \kB \Depth \ln n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{def:s-functional}, $S(\mathcal{R}, x; C) = \inf_{x' \in \Pi(D(x))}
\dcat(x', C) + \beta$.  The infimum equals $\beta$ when $C$ intersects the
resolution cell $D(x)$ (so some $x' \in C$ is available) and equals
$1 + \beta$ when $C$ is disjoint from $D(x)$.  For the generic intermediate
case where $C$ partially overlaps $D(x)$, the infimum is the fractional area
measure $1 - \mu(C \cap D(x))/\mu(D(x))$, plus $\beta$.  Expressing
$\mu(D(x))$ in terms of the partition depth via \Cref{def:partition-depth}
and applying \Cref{thm:depth-entropy} with base $b = n$ yields the identity.
The decomposition $\Scoord = (\Sk, \St, \Se)$ follows by projecting the
total onto the three phase-space subspaces (position-momentum, energy, and
information).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity of S-Functional under Refinement]
\label{prop:monotone-refinement}
If $\Pi' \leq \Pi$ (\ie, $\Pi'$ refines $\Pi$), then for all $x$ and $C$,
\[
  S(\mathcal{R}', x; C) \;\leq\; S(\mathcal{R}, x; C) ,
\]
where $\mathcal{R}'$ is the receiver inducing $\Pi'$.  Finer partitions yield
lower (or equal) S-values, bounded below by $\beta$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\Pi'$ refines $\Pi$, each cell of $\Pi'$ is contained in a cell of
$\Pi$.  Thus $\Pi'(D'(x)) \subseteq \Pi(D(x))$ and the infimum in
\Cref{def:s-functional} is taken over a smaller set, producing a value at most
as large.  The floor $\beta$ is common to all receivers by
\Cref{thm:floor-positivity}.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Symmetric S-Functional]
\label{def:symmetric-S}
An S-functional $S_{\mathcal{R}}$ is \emph{symmetric} if for every
$\mathcal{R} \in \mathfrak{R}$ and every $x, y \in \mathcal{X} \cup \mathcal{X}^{*}$:
\[
  S_{\mathcal{R}}(x, y) \;=\; S_{\mathcal{R}}(y, x)
  \quad \text{and} \quad
  S_{\mathcal{R}}(x, z) \;\leq\; S_{\mathcal{R}}(x, y) + S_{\mathcal{R}}(y, z)
  \quad \text{(modulo floor)}.
\]
Symmetric S-functionals satisfying both conditions form a
\emph{semimetric} on $\mathcal{X}$ with offset $S_\flat$.
\end{definition}

\section{The Triple Equivalence}
\label{sec:triple-equivalence}

\begin{figure*}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{panel1_triple_equivalence.png}
  \caption{Triple Equivalence diagram showing the three mutually equivalent
  category representations of a dynamical system: oscillatory
  ($\mathbf{Osc}(\Omega)$), categorical ($\mathbf{Cat}(\Omega)$), and
  partition-theoretic ($\mathbf{Part}(\Omega)$).  The functors $F_{OC}$,
  $F_{CP}$, and $F_{PO}$ form a commuting triangle, and S-values computed
  under each representation collapse onto the same identity line, confirming
  the three descriptions are epistemically interchangeable.}
  \label{fig:ch01-triple-equivalence}
\end{figure*}

Three representations of a dynamical system — oscillatory, categorical, and
partition-theoretic — are equivalent as categories.  This equivalence is the
technical cornerstone of the framework.  It asserts that no representation is
epistemically privileged: any computation can be performed in whichever
representation makes it cheapest, and the S-value is the same regardless of
the choice.

The result is deeper than it might appear.  The oscillatory representation
captures the dynamical content (frequencies, phases, mode structure); the
categorical representation captures the combinatorial content (which cells
are visited in which order); the partition representation captures the
measure-theoretic content (which sets have which probabilities).  That these
three are equivalent under explicit, computationally accessible functors means
that every dynamical property has a purely combinatorial shadow and every
combinatorial structure has a dynamical realisation.

\begin{definition}[Oscillatory Structure]
\label{def:osc-structure}
An \emph{oscillatory structure} is a quadruple
\[
  \mathfrak{O} \;=\; (M,\, \omega,\, \Phi,\, \mu)
\]
where:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item $M$ is a non-empty set, the \emph{mode set};
  \item $\omega : M \to \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$ is the \emph{frequency assignment};
  \item $\Phi : M \times \mathbb{R} \to S^1$, with $\Phi(m, t)$ the
    \emph{phase} of mode $m$ at time $t$, satisfying
    $\Phi(m, t+s) = \Phi(m, t) \cdot e^{2\pi i \omega(m) s}$;
  \item $\mu$ is a probability measure on $M$ encoding mode weights.
\end{enumerate}
The set of oscillatory structures forms a category $\mathbf{Osc}$ whose
morphisms preserve frequencies and phase compatibility.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Categorical Structure]
\label{def:cat-structure}
A \emph{categorical structure} is a quadruple
\[
  \mathfrak{C} \;=\; (C,\, \prec,\, \lambda,\, \nu)
\]
where:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item $C$ is a non-empty set, the \emph{cell set};
  \item $\prec$ is a strict partial order on $C$ with the property that
    for each $c \in C$, the set $\{c' : c' \prec c\}$ is finite —
    the \emph{refinement order};
  \item $\lambda : C \to \Lambda$ is the \emph{labelling}, where $\Lambda$
    is a set of label tuples;
  \item $\nu$ is a probability measure on $C$ encoding cell weights.
\end{enumerate}
The set of categorical structures forms a category $\mathbf{Cat}$ whose
morphisms preserve $\prec$ and $\lambda$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Partition Structure]
\label{def:part-structure}
A \emph{partition structure} is a triple
\[
  \mathfrak{P} \;=\; (\Omega,\, \mathscr{P},\, \pi)
\]
where:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item $\Omega$ is a non-empty measurable space with $\sigma$-algebra
    $\mathscr{B}$;
  \item $\mathscr{P} = \{P_n\}_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ is a sequence of finite
    measurable partitions of $\Omega$ with $P_{n+1}$ refining $P_n$;
  \item $\pi$ is a probability measure on $\Omega$ compatible with each
    $P_n$ in the sense that $\pi(\partial p) = 0$ for each $p \in P_n$.
\end{enumerate}
The set of partition structures forms a category $\mathbf{Part}$ whose
morphisms preserve refinement and the measure.
\end{definition}

We now construct the three conversion functors explicitly.

\begin{theorem}[Triple Equivalence]
\label{thm:triple-equivalence}
There exist functors
\[
  F_{OC} : \mathbf{Osc} \to \mathbf{Cat}, \qquad
  F_{CP} : \mathbf{Cat} \to \mathbf{Part}, \qquad
  F_{PO} : \mathbf{Part} \to \mathbf{Osc}
\]
such that the triangle
\[
  F_{PO} \circ F_{CP} \circ F_{OC} \;\cong\; \mathrm{Id}_{\mathbf{Osc}},
\]
and all three functors are fully faithful and essentially surjective.  In
particular, $\mathbf{Osc} \simeq \mathbf{Cat} \simeq \mathbf{Part}$ as
categories.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We construct the three functors explicitly.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Construction of $F_{OC}$.}
Define $F_{OC} : \mathbf{Osc} \to \mathbf{Cat}$ on objects by
\[
  F_{OC}(\mathfrak{O}) \;:=\; (M,\, \prec_\omega,\, \lambda_\omega,\, \mu)
\]
where $m_1 \prec_\omega m_2$ iff $\omega(m_1)$ divides $\omega(m_2)$ in
$\mathbb{R}_{>0}$ (interpreted as rational ratio with bounded denominator),
and
\[
  \lambda_\omega(m) \;:=\;
  \bigl(\lfloor \log_2 \omega(m) \rfloor,\;
   \lfloor (\omega(m) - 2^{\lfloor \log_2 \omega(m) \rfloor})
   / 2^{\lfloor \log_2 \omega(m) \rfloor - 1} \rfloor,\;
   \arg \Phi(m, 0) \bigr).
\]
On morphisms, $F_{OC}$ acts by restriction.  Faithfulness holds because
two trajectories visiting distinct cell-sequences are distinct.  Fullness
holds because any prescribed cell transition $C_i \to C_j$ is realised
by a trajectory with the corresponding mode-frequency ratio (by the
Connecting Lemma of dynamical systems).  Essential surjectivity holds
because every categorical sequence is the image of some trajectory.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Construction of $F_{CP}$.}
Define $F_{CP} : \mathbf{Cat} \to \mathbf{Part}$ on objects as follows.
Let $\Omega := C/{\sim_n}$ where $\sim_n$ identifies cells whose labels
agree on the first $n$ coordinates; then the family $\{P_n\}$ of
partitions is induced by the labels.  Define $\pi$ as the pushforward of
$\nu$ under the quotient by $\sim_n$ in the limit.  Refinement morphisms
in $\mathbf{Cat}$ map to refinement morphisms in $\mathbf{Part}$.  Full
faithfulness follows from the bijection between maximal categorical
sequences and minimal partitions consistent with those sequences.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Construction of $F_{PO}$.}
Define $F_{PO} : \mathbf{Part} \to \mathbf{Osc}$ by
\[
  F_{PO}(\mathfrak{P}) \;:=\; (P_\infty,\, \omega_{\mathfrak{P}},\,
  \Phi_{\mathfrak{P}},\, \pi)
\]
where $P_\infty = \bigvee_n P_n$ is the limit partition (atoms in the
generated $\sigma$-algebra), $\omega_{\mathfrak{P}}(p) := 1/\pi(p)$
(inverse-measure frequency, by Kac's lemma analogue), and
$\Phi_{\mathfrak{P}}(p, t) := e^{2\pi i \omega_{\mathfrak{P}}(p) t}$.

Given a partition $\Pi$, the Hamiltonian
\[
  H_\Pi(x) \;=\; \min_{C \in \Pi} \{ E_C : x \in C \}
\]
(where $E_C$ is the first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet Laplacian on $C$)
generates oscillations whose frequencies are $\omega(p) = 1/\pi(p)$.
Refinement morphisms in $\mathbf{Part}$ induce symplectic maps in
$\mathbf{Osc}$ via the symplectic quotient construction.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Triangle commutativity.}
For any oscillatory structure $\mathfrak{O}$,
$F_{PO}(F_{CP}(F_{OC}(\mathfrak{O})))$ recovers a structure whose mode
frequencies match those of $\mathfrak{O}$ up to the label rounding
inherent in $F_{OC}$ (equivalence, not equality, of categories).  All
three structures encode the same information about the underlying
probability measure under the same generating $\sigma$-algebra; the
choice among them is computational, not informational.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Koopman Operator Connection]
\label{rem:koopman}
The functor $F_{PO}$ has a direct interpretation in ergodic theory.  Given
a partition structure $\mathfrak{P}$ with measure $\pi$, the Koopman
operator $\mathcal{U}_T : L^2(\Omega, \pi) \to L^2(\Omega, \pi)$ defined
by $(\mathcal{U}_T f)(x) = f(T x)$ for the partition-induced map $T$
has eigenfunctions precisely equal to the oscillatory modes of
$F_{PO}(\mathfrak{P})$.  The eigenvalues are $e^{2\pi i \omega(p)}$ for
each partition atom $p$, and $\omega(p) = 1/\pi(p)$ by Kac's mean
recurrence theorem~\citep{Halmos1950,Billingsley1995}.  The Triple
Equivalence therefore gives an explicit operator-theoretic realisation:
every partition structure is the spectrum of a Koopman operator, and every
Koopman operator defines a partition structure.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Stone Duality Connection]
\label{rem:stone}
The functor pair $(F_{CP}, F_{PC})$ has the structural shape of Stone
duality between Boolean algebras and totally disconnected compact Hausdorff
spaces.  The categorical structure plays the role of an algebraic object
whose `points' correspond to maximal filters (ultrafilters), and the
partition structure plays the role of the dual topological space — the
Stone space.  The atoms of the limiting partition $P_\infty$ correspond
to the ultrafilters of the Boolean algebra of cylinder sets generated by
the labels $\lambda$.  This connection provides a purely topological
interpretation of the limit partition: it is the Stone--\v{C}ech
compactification of the discrete label space.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Free Conversion]
\label{cor:free-conversion}
For any computation $f$ defined in one of the three representations, there
exist computations $f^O, f^C, f^P$ in the other representations such that
the diagram of conversions commutes up to natural isomorphism.  Thus a
computation may be performed in whichever representation makes it cheapest.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Optimal Representation]
\label{thm:opt-rep}
For any computation $f$, the optimal total cost over the choice of
representation is
\[
  \mathrm{Cost}^*(f)
  \;=\; \min_{R \in \{\mathbf{Osc}, \mathbf{Cat}, \mathbf{Part}\}}
  \bigl[\mathrm{Cost}_R(f) + \min_{R'} \mathrm{Cost}_{R \to R'}\bigr] ,
\]
where $\mathrm{Cost}_R(f)$ is the cost of computing $f$ in representation
$R$ and $\mathrm{Cost}_{R \to R'}$ is the conversion cost.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Direct from \Cref{cor:free-conversion}: every computation has equivalent
forms in all three representations, so the total cost is minimised over
the three-way choice of primary representation plus any necessary conversion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Opacity Independence]
\label{cor:opacity-independence}
The categorical distance $\dcat$ is independent of spatial distance and optical
opacity.  States that are categorically proximate (lying in the same partition
cell) remain mutually reachable regardless of optical, physical, or spatial
barriers between them.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The categorical distance $\dcat(x', C)$ depends only on whether $x'$ belongs
to cell $C$ — a set membership condition — not on any metric of physical space
or any photonic path between $x'$ and $C$.  Since \Cref{thm:triple-equivalence}
shows that the categorical representation is equivalent to the oscillatory one,
the dynamics that transport the system from $x'$ to $C$ are Hamiltonian, not
optical.  Optical barriers affect photon trajectories, not Hamiltonian orbits.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Computational-Observational Identity]
\label{cor:comp-obs-identity}
Computed trajectories have the same epistemic status as experimentally observed
ones.  A trajectory produced by numerical integration of Hamilton's equations
is an object of $\mathbf{Osc}$ on equal footing with a laboratory measurement.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Both computed and observed trajectories are morphisms in $\mathbf{Osc}$.
The functor $F_{OC}$ maps both to categorical sequences without distinguishing
their origin.  Their S-values are therefore computed by the same formula
(\Cref{thm:entropy-identity}).  There is no additional epistemic penalty for
computation.
\end{proof}

\section{The Unconstrained Subtask Theorem}
\label{sec:unconstrained-subtask}

A crucial structural property of the S-functional is that global feasibility
places \emph{no} constraint on the S-values of individual subtasks.  This
is the mathematical reason why complex biological processes can proceed even
when every individual step appears locally infeasible.

Before proving the theorem, we need the algebra of S-expressions — the formal
language in which problems and their decompositions are stated.  An
S-expression is any syntactic object that a receiver can evaluate to an
S-value.  The key feature of S-expressions is that the same global S-value
can be expressed in exponentially many syntactically distinct ways, and the
local S-values of the constituent sub-expressions are entirely unconstrained.

\begin{definition}[Task Decomposition]
\label{def:task-decomposition}
A \emph{task decomposition} of a global task $\mathcal{T}$ with S-value
$S_{\mathrm{global}}$ is a collection of subtasks $\{t_1, \ldots, t_n\}$ and
receiver configurations $\{R_1, \ldots, R_n\}$ such that the receiver's
evaluation of the composed expression equals the global evaluation:
\[
  \mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}\!\bigl(\xi_1 \star_1 \xi_2 \star_2 \cdots
  \star_{n-1} \xi_n\bigr) \;=\; k^* \in K_{\mathcal{R}} ,
  \qquad
  S_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathrm{dec}_{\mathcal{R}}(k^*), x^*) \;=\; S_{\mathrm{global}} .
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Local-Global Decoupling]
\label{thm:local-global}
For any global S-value $S_{\mathrm{global}} \in [S_\flat(\mathcal{R}), 100]$
and any sequence of target local S-values
$(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \ldots, \sigma_n) \in [S_\flat(\mathcal{R}), 100]^n$,
there exists an expression $\xi$ with subtasks $\eta_1, \ldots, \eta_n$
satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^*) = S_{\mathrm{global}}$;
  \item $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\eta_i, x^*) = \sigma_i$ for each $i$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Construct $\xi := \xi_0 + \sum_{i=1}^n (\eta_i - \eta_i)$ where $\xi_0$ is
an expression with global S-value $S_{\mathrm{global}}$ and each $\eta_i$ is
chosen to have local S-value $\sigma_i$.  The terms $\eta_i - \eta_i$
evaluate to zero in $K_{\mathcal{R}}$ and therefore contribute nothing to the
global evaluation.  By \Cref{lem:image-S}, every value in
$[S_\flat(\mathcal{R}), 100]$ is achievable as a local S-value.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Unconstrained Subtask]
\label{thm:unconstrained-subtask}
Let $S_{\mathrm{global}} \in (0, 100]$ be any global S-value, let $n \geq 1$,
and let $\sigma_1, \ldots, \sigma_n \in [S_\flat(\mathcal{R}), 100]$ be any
target local S-values.  Then there exist a task decomposition with subtasks
$\eta_1, \ldots, \eta_n$ satisfying $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\eta_i, x^*) = \sigma_i$
for each $i$, while the composed expression has $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^*) =
S_{\mathrm{global}}$.

In particular, the global S-value $S_{\mathrm{global}}$ places
\textbf{no lower bound} on individual subtask S-values: any $\sigma_i \in
[S_\flat(\mathcal{R}), 100]$ is achievable for task $t_i$ independently
of the values chosen for other subtasks.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The existence of the required decomposition is \Cref{thm:local-global}.  The
``no lower bound'' statement follows because the local S-values $\sigma_i$
are chosen freely in $[S_\flat(\mathcal{R}), 100]$ subject only to the
composition evaluating to $k^*$ — and the Local-Global Decoupling theorem
shows this is always achievable by inserting cancelling pairs.

For the rigorous algebraic argument: let $\xi$ be parsed as $\xi = C[\eta]$
where $C[\cdot]$ is the surrounding context.  For any $\eta'$ with
$\mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}(\eta') = \mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}(\eta)$, the
substitution $\xi' = C[\eta']$ has
$\mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi') = k^*$ by induction on the size of $C$.
The set of such $\eta'$ includes all expressions in any of the three
representations whose evaluation matches, all mixed-type expressions whose
conversions cancel, and all signed-fractional expressions with arithmetic
equivalence.  The local S-value $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\eta', x^*)$ is unconstrained
within $[S_\flat(\mathcal{R}), 100]$ by choosing $\eta'$ appropriately.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Local Infeasibility is Compatible with Global Feasibility]
\label{cor:local-infeasibility}
There exist expressions $\xi$ with global S-value
$S_{\mathrm{global}} \to S_\flat(\mathcal{R})$ (near-perfect) in which one or
more subtasks have local S-value $100$ (maximally incorrect).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $\xi = \eta + (-\eta) + \xi_0$ where $\eta$ is at S-value $100$ and
$\xi_0$ has S-value at floor.  The pair $(\eta, -\eta)$ cancels in the
receiver's evaluation, so the global S-value equals that of $\xi_0$ alone.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Miracle Principle]
\label{rem:miracle}
\Cref{thm:unconstrained-subtask} is the formal basis of what biologists
sometimes call ``emergent'' or ``miraculous'' steps in cellular cascades: a
locally infeasible sub-step ($\sigma_i$ near $100$) can appear in a globally
feasible cascade ($S_{\mathrm{global}}$ near $S_\flat$) precisely because
there is no floor-to-global constraint on individual subtasks.  The cascade
as a whole can be near-optimal even when individual steps appear impossible
in isolation.  This is not a metaphysical claim but a theorem: the
mathematics of receiver evaluation allows it, and biology exploits it.
\end{remark}

\section{Recursive No-Privileged-Level Theorem}
\label{sec:recursive-no-privileged}

The Unconstrained Subtask Theorem shows that local and global S-values
decouple horizontally — across the components of a single decomposition.
The Recursive No-Privileged-Level Theorem extends this decoupling
\emph{vertically}: the same algebraic laws hold at every level of recursion,
and no level is singled out as the ``true'' global level.

This has profound implications for hierarchical biological systems.  A
cellular partition can serve simultaneously as the global description at the
cellular scale and as a subtask at the tissue scale.  The calculus does not
distinguish these roles: the S-value is invariant under vertical
re-embedding.

\begin{definition}[Triple Decomposition]
\label{def:triple-decomp}
A \emph{triple decomposition} of an S-expression $\xi$ is an ordered triple
$(\xi_k, \xi_t, \xi_e)$ of S-expressions satisfying
\[
  \mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}((\xi_k, \xi_t, \xi_e))
  \;=\; \mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi)
  \quad \text{for every receiver } \mathcal{R}.
\]
The components $\xi_k$, $\xi_t$, $\xi_e$ are the \emph{kinematic},
\emph{thermodynamic}, and \emph{epistemic} components, corresponding to
the three coordinates of the S-entropy space $\Sspace$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Recursive Triple at Depth $d$]
\label{def:rec-triple}
A \emph{recursive triple} of $\xi$ at depth $d$ is a triple decomposition
$(\xi_k, \xi_t, \xi_e)$ together with triple decompositions of each
component, continued to depth $d$ or all the way down ($d = \infty$).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Scale Operator]
\label{def:scale-op}
The \emph{scale operator} $\sigma_{d \to d+1}$ sends an expression $\xi$ at
depth $d$ to the expression $(\xi, \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{0})$ at depth $d+1$,
where $\mathbf{0}$ is the zero-expression evaluating to the unit element of
$K_{\mathcal{R}}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Scale Invariance of S-Value]
\label{thm:scale-inv}
For every $\xi$ and every truth $x^*$:
\[
  S_{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma_{d \to d+1}(\xi),\, x^*)
  \;=\; S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi,\, x^*) .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The scale operator adds zero-evaluating components.  By the inductive
definition of receiver evaluation, these contribute nothing to the
receiver's knowledge state, so the S-value is preserved.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No Privileged Level]
\label{thm:no-priv-level}
For every depth $d \in \mathbb{N}$ and every $\xi$, there exists $\xi'$ at
depth $d+1$ such that $\xi'$ contains $\xi$ as a subtask at depth $d+1$,
and $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi', x^*) = S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^*)$.  Thus the
same expression appears as ``global'' at depth $d$ and as ``subtask'' at
depth $d+1$, with identical S-value in both roles.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take $\xi' := (\xi, \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{0})$ as the trivial extension; the
inner $\xi$ is now a subtask of $\xi'$.  The S-value is preserved by
\Cref{thm:scale-inv}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Recursive Global Identification]
\label{cor:recursive-global}
For any expression $\xi$ at any depth, the choice of which level of the
recursive triple is treated as ``global'' is a free parameter.  The calculus
is indifferent to this choice: the same algebraic machinery applies at every
level of recursion, and the system is self-similar under the recursive triple
operation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Existence and Multiplicity of Recursive Triples]
\label{thm:exist-mult-rec-triple}
For every $\xi$ and every depth $d \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item \emph{(Existence)} There exists a recursive triple of $\xi$ at depth $d$.
  \item \emph{(Multiplicity)} The number of recursive triples at depth $d$ is
    at least $T(d) = 3 \cdot 4^{d-1}$ for $d \geq 1$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(a)} By induction.  At $d = 0$, the recursive triple is $\xi$ itself.
At depth $d+1$, choose any triple decomposition $(\xi_k, \xi_t, \xi_e)$
of $\xi$ — such decompositions exist by taking $\xi_k = \xi$,
$\xi_t = \xi - \xi$, $\xi_e = \xi - \xi$, which trivially satisfy the
evaluation equality.  Apply the inductive hypothesis to each component.
At $d = \infty$, the recursion continues without termination.

\emph{(b)} At depth $d = 1$, there are at least three non-trivial triple
decompositions (assigning $\xi$ to any of the three component positions and
zero to the others).  At each subsequent depth, each component admits at
least four binary-operation embeddings (positive addition, negative addition,
multiplication, division, modulo non-degeneracy), and three component
positions multiply to give the lower bound $3 \cdot 4^{d-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Recursive Coordinates and Coordinate Multiplicity]
\label{thm:recursive-coords}
A \emph{recursive coordinate path} of length $n$ is a sequence
$(c_1, c_2, \ldots, c_n) \in \{k, t, e\}^n$.  For a recursive triple of
$\xi$ at depth $d \geq n$, the number of coordinate selections $\xi_{\mathbf{p}}$
for $|\mathbf{p}| = n$ is exactly $3^n$, growing exponentially.  The
recursive S-tuple at depth $d$,
\[
  \mathbf{S}^{(d)}_{\mathcal{R}, x^*}(\xi)
  \;\in\; \mathbb{S}^{3^d} ,
\]
has components $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi_{\mathbf{p}}, x^*)$ for each coordinate
path $\mathbf{p}$ of length $d$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By definition, each coordinate path of length $n$ selects exactly one
sub-expression, and there are $|\{k, t, e\}|^n = 3^n$ such paths.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Cellular Partition Depth as S-Scale Instantiation]
\label{rem:cellular-partition}
In the biological setting, the recursive structure has a direct
interpretation.  A cell partition of a tissue into $N_1$ cell-types at the
first level, with each type subdivided into $N_2$ sub-types at the second
level, and so on, gives a recursive triple with depth $d$ equal to the
number of classification levels.  The S-scale coordinate $\Sk$ at depth $d$
measures the kinematic uncertainty in assigning a given cell to its
partition class at that depth.  By \Cref{thm:no-priv-level}, the S-value is
the same whether we treat the tissue-level partition or the cell-level
partition as the ``global'' description: both are valid, equivalent S-values,
and the calculus does not privilege either.
\end{remark}

\section{Information Catalyst Algebra}
\label{sec:catalyst-algebra}

An information catalyst is an expression that, when composed with any other
expression, strictly reduces the receiver's S-value without itself being
consumed.  The catalytic power of an expression measures how much of the
residual distance above floor it closes.  The key result of this section is
that catalytic powers multiply under composition — a law that is the
information-theoretic analogue of survival probability multiplication in
reliability theory.

\begin{definition}[Information Catalyst]
\label{def:info-catalyst}
An \emph{information catalyst} for receiver $\mathcal{R}$ at truth $x^*$
is an expression $\gamma$ such that for every $\xi$ with
$S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^*) > S_\flat(\mathcal{R})$:
\[
  S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi \diamond \gamma,\, x^*)
  \;<\; S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi,\, x^*) ,
\]
where $\diamond$ denotes catalytic composition.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Catalytic Power]
\label{def:catalytic-power}
The \emph{catalytic power} of catalyst $\gamma$ at receiver $\mathcal{R}$
on truth $x^*$ is
\[
  \kappa_{\mathcal{R}, x^*}(\gamma)
  \;:=\;
  \sup_{\xi}
  \frac{S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^*) - S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi \diamond \gamma, x^*)}
       {S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^*) - S_\flat(\mathcal{R})} .
\]
The catalyst is \emph{strict} if $\kappa > 0$, \emph{absolute} if
$\kappa = 1$, and \emph{neutral} if $\kappa = 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Catalytic Power Range]
\label{lem:cat-power-range}
For every catalyst $\gamma$: $\kappa_{\mathcal{R}, x^*}(\gamma) \in [0, 1]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The numerator is bounded above by the denominator because S-values cannot
decrease below $S_\flat(\mathcal{R})$ by \Cref{cor:smallest-knowable}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Multiplicativity of Catalytic Power]
\label{thm:mult-cat-power}
Let $\gamma_1, \gamma_2$ be catalysts for receiver $\mathcal{R}$ at truth
$x^*$ with catalytic powers $\kappa_1 := \kappa_{\mathcal{R}, x^*}(\gamma_1)$
and $\kappa_2 := \kappa_{\mathcal{R}, x^*}(\gamma_2)$.  Then the catalytic
power of the composite $\gamma_1 \diamond \gamma_2$ is
\[
  \kappa_{\mathcal{R}, x^*}(\gamma_1 \diamond \gamma_2)
  \;=\; 1 - (1 - \kappa_1)(1 - \kappa_2) .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply $\gamma_1$ first.  The residual S-distance above floor is multiplied
by $(1 - \kappa_1)$:
\[
  S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi \diamond \gamma_1, x^*) - S_\flat
  \;=\; (1 - \kappa_1)\bigl[S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^*) - S_\flat\bigr].
\]
Apply $\gamma_2$ to the result.  The residual is further multiplied by
$(1 - \kappa_2)$:
\[
  S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi \diamond \gamma_1 \diamond \gamma_2, x^*) - S_\flat
  \;=\; (1 - \kappa_2)(1 - \kappa_1)\bigl[S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^*) - S_\flat\bigr].
\]
The total fraction of distance closed is
$1 - (1 - \kappa_1)(1 - \kappa_2)$, which equals the catalytic power of
the composite by definition.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Diminishing Returns]
\label{cor:diminishing-returns}
Repeated application of the same catalyst $\gamma$ a total of $n$ times
yields composite catalytic power $1 - (1 - \kappa)^n$, which converges to
$1$ as $n \to \infty$ but never reaches it.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Cascade Composition Rule]
\label{thm:cascade-comp}
The catalytic power of a cascade $\Gamma = (\gamma_1, \ldots, \gamma_n)$
in right-associative parsing is
\[
  \kappa(\Gamma) \;=\; 1 - \prod_{i=1}^n (1 - \kappa_i) .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply \Cref{thm:mult-cat-power} inductively to each pair.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Asymptotic Floor Approach]
\label{cor:asymptotic-floor}
For a sequence of catalysts $(\gamma_1, \gamma_2, \ldots)$ with $\kappa_i > 0$:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item If $\sum_{i=1}^\infty \kappa_i = \infty$ (divergent), the cascade
    S-converges to floor: $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi \diamond \gamma^n, x^*)
    \to S_\flat(\mathcal{R})$.
  \item If $\sum_{i=1}^\infty \kappa_i < \infty$ (convergent), the cascade
    S-converges to a value strictly above floor.
\end{enumerate}
This is the catalytic analogue of the Borel--Cantelli lemma.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{thm:cascade-comp}, the cascade catalytic power after $n$ steps is
$1 - \prod_{i=1}^n(1 - \kappa_i)$.  The infinite product
$\prod_{i=1}^\infty(1 - \kappa_i)$ converges to $1$ iff
$\sum_{i=1}^\infty \kappa_i < \infty$ (by the standard criterion for
convergence of infinite products).  If the sum diverges, the product
converges to $0$, so the cascade catalytic power converges to $1$, driving
the residual above floor to zero.  If the sum converges, the product
converges to a positive value, so the cascade leaves a residual above floor.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Locally-Impossible Catalysts Can Have Positive Power]
\label{thm:loc-imp-positive}
There exist catalysts $\gamma$ with $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\gamma, x^*) = 100$
(locally maximally infeasible) yet $\kappa_{\mathcal{R}, x^*}(\gamma) > 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Construct $\gamma := \eta + (-\eta + \delta)$ where $\eta$ is locally
maximally infeasible and $\delta$ is a small correction expression with
$S_{\mathcal{R}}(\delta, x^*) = S_\flat(\mathcal{R}) + \varepsilon$ for
arbitrarily small $\varepsilon > 0$.  By \Cref{thm:local-global}, the
local S-value of the first summand $\eta$ is $100$, while the composite
$\gamma$ has S-value equal to that of $\delta$ alone.  Substituting
$\gamma$ into a parent expression $\xi$ via $\diamond$ reduces the global
S-distance by the amount encoded by $\delta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Catalyst Monoid Structure]
\label{prop:catalyst-monoid}
The set $\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{R}, x^*} := \{\gamma : \kappa_{\mathcal{R}, x^*}(\gamma) > 0\}$
of strict catalysts, equipped with the binary operation $\diamond$ and the
empty expression as unit, forms a monoid: $\diamond$ is associative and the
unit is two-sided.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Associativity follows from the associativity of composition in the expression
algebra and the multiplicativity of catalytic power (\Cref{thm:mult-cat-power}).
The unit is the empty expression, whose application leaves the receiver's
knowledge state unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Non-Commutativity of Catalyst Algebra]
\label{prop:non-comm-catalyst}
The catalyst algebra is in general non-commutative: there exist
$\gamma_1, \gamma_2$ with
$\gamma_1 \diamond \gamma_2 \neq \gamma_2 \diamond \gamma_1$ as
expressions, even though their composite catalytic powers are equal
(since $1 - (1-\kappa_1)(1-\kappa_2)$ is symmetric in $\kappa_1, \kappa_2$).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The order of catalyst application affects the intermediate state of the
receiver's knowledge framework, even when the final S-value is the same.
Concretely, if $\gamma_1$ acts on representation $\mathbf{Osc}$ and
$\gamma_2$ acts on representation $\mathbf{Cat}$, the receiver's internal
state after $\gamma_1 \diamond \gamma_2$ differs from that after
$\gamma_2 \diamond \gamma_1$, even though both yield the same net S-value
reduction.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Distributivity of Catalysis]
\label{thm:dist-cat}
For all expressions $\xi_1, \xi_2$ and catalyst $\gamma$:
\[
  (\xi_1 \star \xi_2) \diamond \gamma
  \;=\; (\xi_1 \diamond \gamma) \star (\xi_2 \diamond \gamma) ,
\]
where $\star$ is any binary operation for which $\diamond$ distributes in
the receiver's knowledge framework.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Distributivity of the catalytic product over the binary operation follows
from the distributivity of the receiver-internal operations: the catalyst
$\gamma$ modifies the receiver's decoding of both $\xi_1$ and $\xi_2$
independently when applied to their composition, which is the same as
modifying each and then composing.
\end{proof}

\section{Mode-Methodology Equivalence}
\label{sec:mode-methodology}

A \emph{layered receiver} is an ordered tuple of receivers on the same
outcome space that pool their candidate-projections.  The key structural
result of this section is that a receiver's \emph{mode} (its layer
structure) and the \emph{methodology} it employs contribute to the
aggregate floor through the same composition law.  This means that a
more structured methodology and a more refined measurement mode are
formally interchangeable as floor-reduction mechanisms.

\begin{definition}[Layered Receiver]
\label{def:layered-receiver}
A \emph{layered receiver} is an ordered tuple
$\mathcal{R}_\bullet = (\mathcal{R}_1, \ldots, \mathcal{R}_n)$ of receivers
on the same outcome space, with aggregation
\[
  S(\mathcal{R}_\bullet, x; C) \;=\; \min_{i} S(\mathcal{R}_i, x; C) .
\]
Layer $i$ is \emph{pre-decoder} if $D_i$ is constant,
\emph{decoder-layer} if $D_i$ is non-constant continuous, and
\emph{delegated} if $D_i$ factors through an external receiver.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Layered Floor]
\label{prop:layered-floor}
$S_\flat(\mathcal{R}_\bullet) = \min_i \beta_i$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The aggregate S is the minimum over layers.  The minimum of a collection
of quantities each bounded below by $\beta_i$ is bounded below by
$\min_i \beta_i$, and this bound is attained by the best layer.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Methodology]
\label{def:methodology}
A \emph{methodology} is a triple $(T, \kappa, \sigma)$ with per-iteration
update $T : \mathcal{X} \times [0, \Sigma] \to [0, \Sigma]$, contraction
$\kappa \in [0, 1)$, dispersion $\sigma \in [0, \Sigma]$, and
\emph{methodological floor}
\[
  S_\flat(\mathcal{M}) \;=\; \frac{\sigma \kappa}{1 - \kappa} \;>\; 0 .
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Mode-Methodology Equivalence]
\label{thm:mode-method-equiv-ch01}
For a layered receiver $\mathcal{R}_\bullet$ with layer floors $\beta_i$
and an agent with methodology floor $S_\flat(\mathcal{M})$, the composite
floor under the parallel-OR-success composition law is
\[
  S_\flat(\mathcal{R}_\bullet \diamond \mathcal{M})
  \;=\; \frac{\prod_i \beta_i \cdot S_\flat(\mathcal{M})}{\Sigma^n} ,
\]
which depends only on the floor values, not on the internal structure of
either the layers or the methodology.  Modes and methodologies contribute
floors through the same multiplicative law.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The composite floor under parallel-OR-success is the product of the
dimensionless individual floors times $\Sigma$.  The $n$ receiver layers
contribute $\prod_i \beta_i / \Sigma^{n-1}$ and the methodology contributes
$S_\flat(\mathcal{M}) / \Sigma$; their parallel composition gives
$\prod_i \beta_i \cdot S_\flat(\mathcal{M}) / \Sigma^n$.  The key observation
is that the formula is symmetric in the floors: whether we call a
floor-contributor a ``layer'' or a ``methodology'' is a labelling choice.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Methodological Incompatibility Principle]
\label{thm:meth-incompatibility}
Two methodologies $\mathcal{M}_1, \mathcal{M}_2$ with disjoint update maps
(the image of $T_1$ is disjoint from the image of $T_2$ in
$[0, \Sigma]$) produce incompatible belief structures: the joint
methodology floor satisfies
\[
  S_\flat(\mathcal{M}_1 \diamond \mathcal{M}_2)
  \;<\; \min(S_\flat(\mathcal{M}_1),\, S_\flat(\mathcal{M}_2)) .
\]
Incompatible methodologies are more powerful in combination than either
alone, even though their beliefs are disjoint.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{thm:mode-method-equiv-ch01}, the composite floor is the product of
the dimensionless individual floors times $\Sigma$:
$S_\flat(\mathcal{M}_1 \diamond \mathcal{M}_2) =
S_\flat(\mathcal{M}_1) \cdot S_\flat(\mathcal{M}_2) / \Sigma$.
Since both floors are in $(0, \Sigma)$, the product divided by $\Sigma$
is strictly less than either factor.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Distribution Theorem]
\label{thm:agent-floor-distribution}
For any target cell $C$ and any finite ensemble of agents
$\mathcal{A} = (\mathcal{A}_1, \ldots, \mathcal{A}_n)$ with agent floors
$f_i$, the composite floor satisfies
\[
  S_\flat(\mathcal{A}_1 \diamond \cdots \diamond \mathcal{A}_n)
  \;=\; \frac{\prod_{i=1}^n f_i}{\Sigma^{n-1}} ,
\]
and the composite decreases geometrically in $n$:
$S_\flat(\mathcal{A}^n) = f^n / \Sigma^{n-1}$ when all $f_i = f$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Repeated application of the parallel-composition formula from
\Cref{thm:mode-method-equiv-ch01}, noting that independence of the agents'
candidate-projections makes the product formula exact.
\end{proof}

\section{Worked Examples}
\label{sec:ch01-worked-examples}

\subsection{A Periodic System in Three Representations}
\label{subsec:periodic-example}

The following example shows the Triple Equivalence in action.  A system
with period exactly $3$ (in some unit) is expressed in three representations;
the S-value is identical in all three.  This illustrates
\Cref{thm:triple-equivalence} concretely and shows the Unconstrained Subtask
property in an arithmetic context.

\begin{example}[Period-3 System]
\label{ex:period-3}
Consider a candidate space $\mathcal{X}$ representing periodic systems with
truth $x^* \in \mathcal{X}^*$ being a system of period exactly $3$ (seconds).
Let receiver $\mathcal{R}$ have $\delta_{\mathcal{R}}(s, s') =
\min(100, |s - s'| \cdot 33.3)$ (a unit difference contributes $33.3$ to
the S-value).

\paragraph{Oscillatory representation.}
The truth $x^*$ corresponds to mode $m^*$ with frequency $\omega(m^*) =
2\pi/3$.  The expression $\xi_O := (m^*, \mathbf{Osc})$ directly identifies
the mode.  Its S-value: $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi_O, x^*) = S_\flat(\mathcal{R})$.

\paragraph{Categorical representation.}
Under $F_{OC}$, the mode $m^*$ maps to cell $c^*$ with label
$(\lfloor \log_2(2\pi/3) \rfloor, \ldots, \arg \Phi(m^*, 0))$.
The expression $\xi_C := (c^*, \mathbf{Cat})$ has the same S-value:
$S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi_C, x^*) = S_\flat(\mathcal{R})$.

\paragraph{Partition representation.}
Under $F_{PO}$, the partition atom $p^*$ has measure
$\pi(p^*) = 1/\omega(m^*) = 3/(2\pi)$.
The expression $\xi_P := (p^*, \mathbf{Part})$ has the same S-value.

\paragraph{Unconstrained decompositions.}
All of the following evaluate to period $3$ and hence have S-value $S_\flat$:
\begin{align*}
  3 &= 1 + 1 + 1, \qquad 3 = 2 + 1, \qquad 3 = 4 - 1, \\
  3 &= 6/2, \qquad 3 = \log_e e^3, \qquad
  3 = \sin(3\pi/2) + 4 .
\end{align*}
The expression $\sin(3\pi/2) + 4$ requires the receiver to have a
trigonometric decoder, but contains no type violation under the calculus.
A cross-representation expression $(p_1 + t_1 + 1)$ where $p_1$ is
nominally a position and $t_1$ a time is admissible: each is converted to
its representation-internal numeric value via the conversion functors before
the sum proceeds.  The local types are surface features; the global S-value
is what matters.

Note that the expression $(11 - 10) + (-1 - (-1)) + 3 = 1 + 0 + 3 = 4$
does \emph{not} evaluate to $3$ and has $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\cdot, x^*)
\approx 33.3$.  This illustrates that not every syntactically plausible
expression achieves the target S-value; the receiver's evaluation must yield
the correct global value.
\end{example}

\subsection{Hierarchical Resolution — Same Expression at Multiple Levels}
\label{subsec:hierarchical-example}

\begin{example}[Same Expression as Global and Subtask]
\label{ex:hierarchical}
Consider the expression
\[
  \xi := \xi_1 \star \xi_2 \star \xi_3
\]
at depth $1$, with global S-value $S^* = S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^*)$.

\paragraph{Depth 1: global problem.}
At depth $1$, $\xi$ is the global problem.  Its subtasks $\xi_1, \xi_2,
\xi_3$ may have any local S-values in $[S_\flat, 100]$ by
\Cref{thm:unconstrained-subtask}.  The global S-value is $S^*$.

\paragraph{Depth 2: $\xi$ as subtask.}
By \Cref{thm:no-priv-level}, form $\xi' := (\xi, \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{0})$.
Then $\xi$ is the $k$-component of $\xi'$, with
$S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi', x^*) = S^*$ by \Cref{thm:scale-inv}.  At depth $2$,
$\xi$ is a subtask of $\xi'$; its sub-subtasks $\xi_1, \xi_2, \xi_3$ are
now sub-sub-tasks.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The calculus does not distinguish the two embeddings.  The algebraic laws
hold uniformly at every depth.  The S-value is the same ($S^*$) in both
roles.  The choice of which level to label as ``global'' is a matter of
pragmatic convenience — the label of an observation scale, not a structural
reality.  This is the formal content of biological scale invariance: the
same S-entropy machinery applies whether one is describing molecular
interactions, cellular organisation, tissue architecture, or organ function.
\end{example}

\subsection{Cellular Partition Depth as S-Scale Instantiation}
\label{subsec:cellular-example}

\begin{example}[Cellular Partition in Biological Tissue]
\label{ex:cellular-partition}
Let $\Omega$ be the state space of a tissue sample and let $\Pi^{(1)}$ be
a partition into $N_1 = 8$ broad cell-type classes (\eg, epithelial, stromal,
immune, vascular, neural, adipose, skeletal, undefined).  Let $\Pi^{(2)}$
refine $\Pi^{(1)}$ into $N_1 \cdot N_2 = 8 \cdot 8 = 64$ sub-types.

The partition depth at level 1 is $\Depth(\Pi^{(1)}) = \log_2 8 = 3$
partition bits.  The S-entropy at level 1 is
$S^{(1)} = \kB \cdot 3 \cdot \ln 2$.  At level 2, $\Depth(\Pi^{(2)}) = 6$
partition bits and $S^{(2)} = \kB \cdot 6 \cdot \ln 2 = 2 S^{(1)}$.

The capacity formula gives $\Capacity(n) = 2n^2$ cells at nesting depth
$n$.  For $n = 1$: $\Capacity(1) = 2$ (two chirality classes per level-0
cell).  For $n = 4$: $\Capacity(4) = 32$ — which matches the capacity of
the first four quantum shells, illustrating the connection in
\Cref{prop:capacity}.

By the Recursive No-Privileged-Level Theorem, the same S-entropy machinery
applies whether we use $\Pi^{(1)}$ or $\Pi^{(2)}$ as our ``global''
partition.  A disease state can be characterised at either resolution; the
S-value at level 1 and the S-value at level 2 are different numbers but
describe the same epistemic situation at different granularities.  The
clinical implication is that coarser and finer diagnostic partitions are
not in competition — each is a valid representation, and the Triple
Equivalence guarantees they can be freely converted.
\end{example}

\section{Connections to Classical Mathematics}
\label{sec:classical-connections}

The S-entropy calculus is not an isolated formalism.  It has precise
connections to several established branches of mathematics.  These
connections serve two purposes: they ground the new machinery in familiar
territory and they import known results as special cases of the general
theorems proved above.

\subsection{Ergodic Theory}
\label{subsec:ergodic}

The connection between the S-calculus and ergodic theory runs through the
Koopman operator construction described in \Cref{rem:koopman}.  The key
dictionary is:

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{S-calculus} & \textbf{Ergodic theory} & \textbf{Key theorem} \\
\midrule
Partition structure $\mathfrak{P}$ & Measure-preserving system & --- \\
Oscillatory structure $\mathfrak{O}$ & Koopman eigenfunctions & Kac's lemma \\
Partition depth $\Depth$ & Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy & Shannon-McMillan-Breiman \\
S-convergence to floor & Recurrence to invariant set & Poincar\'{e} recurrence \\
Catalyst power $\kappa$ & Spectral gap & Ergodic mixing rate \\
Floor $S_\flat$ & Invariant measure support & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The S-convergence theorem (\Cref{cor:asymptotic-floor}) is structurally
analogous to the Borel--Cantelli lemma~\citep{Billingsley1995}: the
divergence of $\sum \kappa_i$ is the necessary and sufficient condition for
almost-sure convergence to the limiting attractor (the floor), exactly as
divergence of $\sum \Pr(A_n)$ is necessary and sufficient for
$\Pr(\limsup A_n) = 1$ in the classical Borel--Cantelli setting.

The partition depth $\Depth$ corresponds to the Kolmogorov-Sinai (metric)
entropy of a measure-preserving dynamical system~\citep{Billingsley1995}:
\[
  h_{\mathrm{KS}}(T, \mathscr{P})
  \;=\; \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} H\!\left(
    \bigvee_{k=0}^{n-1} T^{-k} \mathscr{P} \right) ,
\]
where $H$ is the Shannon entropy.  By \Cref{thm:depth-entropy},
$S_P = \kB \Depth \ln b$, so the S-entropy is a scaled version of the
Boltzmann entropy of the partition, and the floor $S_\flat$ is the
minimum achievable KS entropy per unit time.

\subsection{Lattice Theory}
\label{subsec:lattice}

\begin{theorem}[Expression Algebra as Complete Lattice]
\label{thm:expr-lattice}
The set of S-expressions $\mathcal{E}$ together with the join $\vee$
(least common refinement) and meet $\wedge$ (greatest common coarsening)
under receiver evaluation forms a complete lattice.  The join of
$\xi_1, \xi_2$ is the expression $(\xi_1, \xi_2, \mathbf{0})$ at depth $1$,
and the meet is the receiver-internal greatest common factor.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The lattice axioms hold by construction.  Completeness follows from the
axiom of choice applied to the decomposition tree, ensuring arbitrary
joins and meets exist.
\end{proof}

The lattice structure corresponds to the classical partition lattice studied
in combinatorics~\citep{Halmos1950}: a partition $\Pi$ is above $\Pi'$ iff
$\Pi'$ refines $\Pi$.  The S-value is a monotone function on this lattice
(by \Cref{prop:monotone-refinement}): it decreases as the partition is
refined.  The floor $S_\flat$ is the infimum of the S-value over all
partitions — the bottom of the lattice's image under the S-functional.

\subsection{Category Theory}
\label{subsec:category}

\begin{theorem}[Expression Algebra as Monoidal Category]
\label{thm:expr-monoidal}
The set $\mathcal{E}$ of S-expressions together with composition $\diamond$
forms a symmetric monoidal category, with the empty expression as the unit
object and the binary operations of $\mathfrak{B}$ as the monoidal product.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The monoidal axioms (associativity coherence, left/right unit isomorphisms,
pentagon and triangle equations) are verified by direct calculation using
the associativity of $\diamond$ (\Cref{prop:catalyst-monoid}) and the
distributivity (\Cref{thm:dist-cat}).  Symmetry follows from the
commutativity of catalytic powers (\Cref{thm:mult-cat-power}).
\end{proof}

The Triple Equivalence (\Cref{thm:triple-equivalence}) is a categorical
equivalence in the strict sense: a natural isomorphism between the
identity functor and the round-trip composition.  In the language of
category theory, the three representation categories form a commuting
triangle of adjoint equivalences, each pair being an adjoint pair.

\subsection{Information Theory}
\label{subsec:info-theory}

The S-functional has direct information-theoretic interpretations.

\begin{theorem}[Information Content of S-Value]
\label{thm:info-content}
For a receiver $\mathcal{R}$ with cognitive capacity $|K_{\mathcal{R}}|$,
the information content of an S-value $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^*)$ to
within precision $\varepsilon$ is bounded by
\[
  I_\varepsilon(S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi, x^*))
  \;\leq\;
  \log_2\!\left( \frac{100 - S_\flat(\mathcal{R})}{\varepsilon} \right)
  \text{ bits.}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The image of $S_{\mathcal{R}}$ is contained in $[S_\flat(\mathcal{R}), 100]$
by \Cref{lem:image-S}.  Discretising at precision $\varepsilon$ gives at
most $(100 - S_\flat) / \varepsilon$ distinguishable values, requiring
$\log_2$ of that many bits to specify~\citep{Shannon1948}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Floor as Information Bound]
\label{cor:floor-info-bound}
The floor $S_\flat(\mathcal{R})$ caps the maximum information content of
any single S-value at $\mathcal{R}$.  As $S_\flat(\mathcal{R}) \to 0$,
the information content diverges, demonstrating that perfect alignment
would require infinite information transfer.
\end{corollary}

The connection to Shannon entropy~\citep{Shannon1948} is direct.  The
partition entropy $H(\Pi) = -\sum_i \pi(C_i) \log \pi(C_i)$ is related to
the partition depth by $H(\Pi) = \Depth(\Pi) \cdot \ln b$, and the
S-entropy is $\kB$ times this.  The floor $S_\flat$ is the minimum
achievable partition entropy — the ``hard core'' of information that no
receiver can eliminate.

The connection to Kullback-Leibler divergence is via the Axiom~(S4)
coherence function $\delta_{\mathcal{R}}$: for receivers whose knowledge
framework has a probability structure, the KL divergence
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\Phi(\sigma_x) \| \Phi(\sigma_{x^*}))$ provides a natural
candidate for $\delta_{\mathcal{R}}$, and the S-value becomes a normalised
KL divergence from the truth~\citep{Jaynes1957}.

\section{Circular Validation and Foundational Coherence}
\label{sec:circular-validation}

\begin{figure*}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{sufficient_inclusions_panel.png}
  \caption{Circular validation and sufficient inclusions.  The three mutually
  supporting pillars — Bounded Phase Space, Categorical Observation, and Triple
  Equivalence — are shown with bidirectional support arrows.  The shaded
  triangle quantifies the collective coherence threshold ($>0.5$), illustrating
  why a size-3 circularly validating collection at threshold $\tau > \tfrac{1}{2}$
  provides genuine foundational coherence, and why linear justification chains
  with cumulative floor $k\beta$ are epistemically inferior.}
  \label{fig:ch01-circular-validation}
\end{figure*}

Linear justification chains suffer a fundamental defect: each inference step
incurs an irreducible epistemic cost $\beta > 0$ (proved in \Cref{thm:floor-positivity}),
so a chain of $k$ steps has cumulative floor $k\beta$, which grows without
bound.  The framework is instead grounded by a \emph{circular} structure of
mutually supporting pillars.  This is not a logical vice but a mathematical
necessity: the Floor Theorem rules out any finite linear chain that reaches
$S = 0$, so circular validation is the \emph{only} epistemically adequate
foundation.

\begin{definition}[Circular Validation]
\label{def:circular-validation}
A collection of $n$ propositions $\{s_1, \ldots, s_n\}$ satisfies
\emph{circular validation at threshold $\tau > 0$} if:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item $n \geq 3$;
  \item For each $i$, proposition $s_i$ supports $s_{i+1 \bmod n}$ with
    conditional probability $p_i \geq \tau$;
  \item The support relation is not vacuous: $p_i > 0$ for all $i$;
  \item The validation graph (with an edge from $s_j$ to $s_i$ when
    $s_j$ supports $s_i$ above $\tau$) is strongly connected.
\end{enumerate}
The \emph{collective coherence} of the collection is $\prod_{i=1}^n p_i$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Linear Justification Failure]
\label{thm:linear-failure}
For every receiver $\mathcal{R}$ with $S_\flat(\mathcal{R}) > 0$, there is
no finite sequence of expressions $\xi_0 \leftarrow \xi_1 \leftarrow \cdots
\leftarrow \xi_n$ such that: (i) each $\xi_{i+1}$ supports $\xi_i$ to
threshold $\tau = 1$; (ii) $\xi_n$ requires no support; (iii)
$S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi_0, x^*) = 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Condition (iii) requires $S_{\mathcal{R}}(\xi_0, x^*) = 0$, but by
\Cref{thm:floor-positivity}, $S_{\mathcal{R}}(x, x^*) \geq S_\flat(\mathcal{R}) > 0$
for every $x$.  Therefore no such sequence exists.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Circular Validation]
\label{thm:circular-validation}
A size-3 circularly validating collection at threshold $\tau > \tfrac{1}{2}$
is both necessary and sufficient for foundational coherence, in the following
sense:
\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
  \item \emph{(Sufficiency)} If $\{s_1, s_2, s_3\}$ satisfies circular
    validation at $\tau > \tfrac{1}{2}$, the collective coherence exceeds
    $\tfrac{1}{8}$, which is strictly above the independent baseline
    $\tau^3 < \tfrac{1}{8}$ for $\tau = \tfrac{1}{2}$.
  \item \emph{(Necessity)} Any size-2 circularly validating collection is
    degenerate (each proposition proves the other with no third anchor),
    providing no independent epistemic support.
  \item \emph{(Linear chain failure)} A linear justification chain
    $s_1 \Rightarrow s_2 \Rightarrow \cdots \Rightarrow s_k$ has cumulative
    floor $k\beta$, which is unbounded as $k \to \infty$.  Circular
    validation has a fixed floor $\beta$ independent of $k$.
  \item \emph{(Stability)} A circularly valid collection at threshold
    $\tau$ with $|\mathcal{A}| \geq 3$ is stable under perturbations of
    magnitude $\delta \leq \tau / (|\mathcal{A}| - 1)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{(a):} The collective coherence is $p_1 p_2 p_3 \geq \tau^3$.  For
$\tau > \tfrac{1}{2}$, we have $\tau^3 > \tfrac{1}{8}$.  This means each
proposition contributes more-than-even odds to the others.

\emph{(b):} For $n = 2$, $s_1$ supports $s_2$ and $s_2$ supports $s_1$.
Since both are in the same mutual support relationship with no third
independent anchor, the pair is epistemically degenerate — the circle is
its own only support.

\emph{(c):} Each inference step in a linear chain propagates the state
estimate through a receiver with floor $\beta > 0$.  By \Cref{thm:depth-entropy}
and the additive composition of floors for independent layers, the chain's
cumulative floor is $k\beta \to \infty$.  In a circular structure, the
floor incurred at step $i$ is corrected at step $i+1$ by the return of
support; the circular average converges to $\beta$, not $k\beta$.

\emph{(d):} A perturbation in any single proposition changes the support
sums to other propositions by at most $\delta$.  For mutual support at
threshold $\tau$ to be preserved, each remaining sum must remain above
$\tau$.  Since each proposition supports $|\mathcal{A}| - 1$ others,
the per-perturbation budget is $\tau / (|\mathcal{A}| - 1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The Three Pillars]
\label{rem:three-pillars}
The present framework is grounded by exactly three mutually validating pillars:
\begin{enumerate}[(I)]
  \item \emph{Bounded Phase Space} (\Cref{ax:bounded-phase}): implies
    finiteness of information content.
  \item \emph{Categorical Observation} (\Cref{ax:categorical-obs}): implies
    finiteness of the partition.
  \item \emph{Triple Equivalence} (\Cref{thm:triple-equivalence}): implies
    that oscillatory, categorical, and partition representations are
    epistemically interchangeable.
\end{enumerate}
Each pillar is supported by the other two.  Pillar~I is justified by
Pillar~II (finite-resolution observation cannot detect infinite phase space)
and by Pillar~III (the oscillatory picture is consistent only with finite
partitions).  Pillar~II is justified by Pillar~I (unbounded phase space would
require unbounded resolution) and by Pillar~III (categorical descriptions
are self-consistent only if phase space is finite).  Pillar~III is justified by
Pillars~I and~II jointly (the functor construction in the proof of
\Cref{thm:triple-equivalence} uses both).  By \Cref{thm:circular-validation}
with $\tau = \tfrac{2}{3} > \tfrac{1}{2}$ (a conservative estimate of mutual
support probability), the collective coherence is at least $(2/3)^3 =
8/27 > 1/4$, well above the independent-pillar baseline.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Circular Validation Does Not Guarantee Truth]
\label{cor:cv-no-truth}
Circular validation guarantees \emph{internal consistency} of the three
pillars but does not guarantee that they are true descriptions of physical
reality.  The framework is epistemically self-consistent; its empirical
adequacy is a separate question addressed in subsequent chapters.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Circular validation, by \Cref{def:circular-validation}, is a property of
mutual conditional probabilities.  A collection of three mutually consistent
\emph{false} propositions satisfies circular validation just as well as three
mutually consistent true ones.  Internal consistency is necessary but not
sufficient for empirical truth.  The empirical adequacy of the present
framework is argued by its predictive content — the No Template Theorem
(\Cref{ch:no-template}), the Disease Vector Theorem (\Cref{ch:disease}), and
the Drug Action Theorem (\Cref{ch:drug}) — each of which makes falsifiable
predictions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Necessity of Circular Validation for Any Bounded Foundational System]
\label{rem:necessity-circular}
\Cref{thm:linear-failure} and \Cref{thm:circular-validation} together establish
a dichotomy for any foundational system under bounded cognition:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Linear chains cannot reach zero floor and must terminate in an
    unjustified axiom, leaving cumulative error $k\beta$.
  \item Dogmatic acceptance of a single unjustified axiom vacuously satisfies
    circular validation with $n = 1$ (zero support), which fails the
    minimum-size condition $n \geq 3$.
  \item Circular validation with $n \geq 3$ and $\tau > 1/2$ is the unique
    remaining option that achieves genuine, bounded-floor foundational
    coherence.
\end{itemize}
This is a mathematical theorem, not a philosophical preference.  Any
framework whose foundations satisfy \Cref{ax:bounded-phase} and
\Cref{ax:categorical-obs} — that is, any framework about finite observers
and finite worlds — must, to be coherent, adopt circular validation.  The
present monograph does so explicitly.
\end{remark}
